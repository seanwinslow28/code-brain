Audience: one listener, Sean — a product manager with NO engineering background who runs a 12-agent autonomous AI system in production every day.

CALIBRATION FOR THIS MODULE: this is a PARTIAL mirror, so draw the line sharply and do not blur it in either direction. What he HAS built, and you should name: instrumentation (health ledgers and nightly manifests separate from the conversation), a quality gate that emits its rejection reasons rather than only recording passes, an automated nightly critic running over another agent's output on a schedule, hierarchical cost caps that project spend and refuse before the call, and — the strongest one — a real launch gate with written numeric thresholds and a named human sign-off, which he built for his personal website by instinct: three browser engines green, a seven-route sweep at 7 of 7, a 95th-percentile frame budget under 16.7 milliseconds, a one-pixel settle tolerance, a pinned byte budget, and the rule that nothing merges until he has personally wheeled through every page. That IS the artifact this module asks for, and he already made one.

THE DISTINCTION THAT CARRIES THE MODULE: cost control is not unit economics. A cap stops a bill; unit economics asks whether the thing makes money when it works. He is genuinely good at the first and has never once done the second — there is no revenue side to his fleet, no cost per successful outcome, no margin. Say that plainly.

What he has NEVER done and must be taught from zero: run an experiment; put a MODEL behind a rollout gate rather than merging code behind one; sample traces for structured human review; measure whether a judge agrees with a human; price anything; negotiate with another person about who owns a model, a threshold, or the data it learns from; or run an incident where the thing that broke was probabilistic. Do NOT teach him what an agent, a harness, a model, an API or a router is.

- DO define every term in plain language on first use: trace, unit of analysis, random versus error-weighted sampling, error analysis, open coding, axial coding, golden dataset, holdout hygiene, eval contamination, LLM-as-judge, judge-human agreement, precision and recall versus raw agreement, position bias, length bias, self-enhancement bias, concreteness bias, Goodhart's law, weak-judge Goodharting, shadow mode, canary, champion/challenger, unit of randomisation, kill switch, rollback time budget, launch criteria, covariate shift, label shift, concept drift, cost per successful outcome, gross margin, outcome-based pricing, blameless postmortem.
- CRITICAL RULE: never mention a company, incident, paper, researcher or tool without a one-sentence setup first explaining what it is and why it is being mentioned. Assume no prior familiarity. This is the single most important instruction.
