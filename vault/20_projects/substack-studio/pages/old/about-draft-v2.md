I was a husky youth with an appetite for stupidity. I'd plant myself three feet from a static TV with an extra large bowl of Cinnamon Toast Crunch and mainline whatever the networks were pushing. Saturday mornings were a sacrament. My parents' living room was the church. Years went by and I used that same appetite to teach myself animation, write multiple screenplays, and piece together a few short films.

It never quit. Just kept circling back for the next thing to take apart. A few years ago I ended up three feet from a different screen, a whole wall of them, mainlining whatever the models could cough up the way I once mainlined cartoons. Same appetite, new channel. I went down the rabbit hole expecting a cheap party trick and found something stranger: a machine that would make anything I asked and care nothing about why it mattered. It could draw all day. It just couldn't draw like me.

So that became the job. Not prompting harder, but teaching it my taste until what came back was recognizably mine. I believed that enough to build an entire pipeline around it, called anima, where I keep the timing, the casting, and the taste, and a fleet of agents does the grunt work and proposes. They never get to say cut. That's mine.

That's the whole place, really. I take one real creative job, start it on the soulless first pass you'd normally bail on, and drag it move by move until it has a pulse. Then I hand you the recipe so you can run it on your own stuff.

I'm still that husky kid three feet from the glow, bowl in my lap, itching to make the thing I can't stop thinking about. The cereal's gone and the screen got wider, but the appetite is the exact same. If you've still got yours, pull up a spot on the floor.
