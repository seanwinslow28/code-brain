# Start Here

You asked AI to make you something. A picture, a paragraph, a character, whatever it was. It handed back something confident and competent and completely dead. The same glossy, same-y thing everybody else gets. You looked at it for a second, felt a little stupid for even trying, and closed the tab.

Me too. The difference is I'd spent weeks building the machine that handed me the slop. I asked it for a few lines in my own voice, and it gave me "Every rep is a vote for the person you're becoming." I typed one word back at it. GROSS.

So I did what you probably did. I piled on rules. Be less generic. Sound more like me. Every rule made it worse. Somebody will tell you that part's on you, that you just need to prompt it better. They're lying to you. You cannot write it a good enough memo on who you are. It reads your adjectives back to you as a stranger who skimmed your bio.

Here's the part nobody mentions: you didn't fail. You quit one step early. What it was missing was never a sharper prompt. It was your taste, and taste doesn't load in from a description. It goes in through evidence, handed over slow.

That's the whole idea here. AI isn't your replacement. It's a wildly talented intern with no taste yet, fast as hell, better-read than both of us, and raised by a committee of strangers before it ever met you. The job is to teach it yours, until it quits handing you the factory setting and starts making work that's recognizably you, on demand.

There's a question almost nobody is actually answering: does feeding the machine your references and your taste really kill that AI look? I'm not going to tell you it does. I'm going to show you. Every piece here takes one real creative job, starts on the soulless first pass you'd have quit on, and directs it move by move until it's got a pulse. Then I hand you the recipe, stripped down so you can run it on your own work tonight.

I came up through the actual craft, animation, writing, character design, and then I taught a fleet of agents to help me make it. Not an analyst who wandered into creativity looking for a take. A maker who went and learned the machines. (Longer story's in About.)

**What you'll get here**

- **Take Two:** the main event. One creative job, soulless first, then directed into something that's actually yours, with the method you can steal.
- **Tool drops:** every so often I build you a thing that kills a specific grind, packaged so you never have to touch a line of code.
- **Fix My Mess:** send me your worst AI output and watch me drag it back to life in public.

**Start with [Take Two #1].** That's the whole thesis in one piece.

No hype, no "you're prompting it wrong," nothing to install. Just a creative showing you how he taught the machine his taste, so you can go teach it yours.
