# I have no sisters to reference

*Three months teaching Claude to write like my favorite writers.*

The skill I built kept putting punch lines on my sisters.

I'm an only child. And it wasn't just the sisters. I'd be writing about agentic workflows and it would end with some sort of callback to hanging out with my Mom.

It was leaning David Sedaris. Sedaris writes about his family a lot, and comedic deflation is how I tend to write anyway. So the jokes kept landing on my family, within a technical essay.

I thought this would be clever.

I was commuting home on the Staten Island ferry, re-reading Dharma Bums, admiring the way Kerouac keeps a sentence flowing to verbally punch you in the face multiple times to really drive the point home. Around then I found Nate B. Jones' YouTube. He's got episodes about finding ways to teach LLMs your personal taste.

What if I could make Claude write like this?

So I went down a rabbit hole. I researched and synthesized each author. Jack Kerouac, Hunter S. Thompson, David Sedaris, Kurt Vonnegut. I broke down their style and crammed everything into a single skill, and I pointed it at my essays, my documents, my social posts, anything that required good writing instead of typical AI slop.

Kerouac, sort of. The model definitely kept the sentence going on and on, but it didn't have the same effect.

Neither of the other two ever worked. Vonnegut always sounded like a children's book author floating through the void. Thompson felt like I was injecting caffeine into my veins at 2am with all of my agents cheering me on.

The models weren't doing a bad job. They were doing exactly as I instructed. They can interpret the mechanics and break down how they write. They couldn't provide the actual soul behind the writing. Every output that came back felt foreign and cringy, and it sounded nothing like me.

By then I'd been at it about three months.

So obviously I didn't delete it. I kept some of the authors' tendencies in there to keep the good parts. It still popped up in the worst ways.

About a month or two ago I finally broke down. I was looking into starting a Substack, and everything being sent my way felt too cringy and had nothing to do with me and my personal experiences. It was always trying too hard to be elegant, and the jokes were painfully unfunny.

I completely removed them. Kept everything I had written. Started fresh.

I incorporated the skill chain, injected more rules, fed in some of my own work. I had Claude give me writing exercises to get more of me and less of them. It was still popping up here and there, which is when I turned to Alex Lieberman's approach with the content machine and stopped asking the model to write like anybody at all.

The models will try to break writing down like a math problem, and mechanics can only get you so far in this medium. Becoming a good writer takes time, lived experiences, and sleepless nights spilling your guts onto a keyboard because you need to get it out of you. You can start off copying your main influences, but as you keep typing, all of your inspirations slowly meld into one cohesive voice that only you could have come up with.

The same idea applies to teaching the model how to capture that voice.

So now I have it grill me instead. I brain dump an idea into Claude and have it invoke the /creative-partner skill to analyze everything I've spewed into it. It starts asking me questions to flesh the idea out and make it more coherent. It gives me some ideas on how to expand or where to pivot. Then, after multiple rounds of pushing me in different potential directions, it gives me the final decision.

A few weeks ago OpenAI quietly stopped ChatGPT from writing in the style of named authors. Living or dead. Copyright suits.

People are going to be pretty disappointed with the results. No matter how hard you try to have a model copy someone's style and add it into your own work, it won't feel right putting it out there in the world.
