# I Don't Have Any Sisters

*Three months of cramming four authors into one writing skill, and the delete that finally made it sound like me.*

I was on the ferry home re-reading *Dharma Bums* when I got the idea that cost me three months.

Kerouac keeps a sentence going well past the point where a normal sentence gives up, and by the time he finally lets go of you he's punched you in the face four or five times to make sure the thing landed. I'd found Nate B. Jones' YouTube around then too, and he has a whole run of episodes on teaching an LLM your personal taste. So the thought arrived on its own. What if I could make Claude write like this?

Down the hole I went. I researched and synthesized four writers I love. Kerouac, Hunter S. Thompson, David Sedaris, Kurt Vonnegut. I broke each one down into mechanics, crammed all four into a single writing skill, and pointed it at everything I put words into. Essays, documents, social posts, anything that deserved better than the usual AI slop.

The essays kept routing to Sedaris. That part actually made sense, because comedic deflation is how I tend to write anyway. So the model went out and got itself a family. Every punchline it built landed on my mother and my sisters.

I don't have sisters. I'm an only child.

It gets better. I'd be three paragraphs deep into agentic workflows, mid technical explanation, and the thing would button the whole essay on a warm little callback to hanging out with my Mom.

That's not the model blowing it. Sedaris writes about his family, I asked for Sedaris, and it handed me a family. It did exactly what I told it to do. I just never told it who I was.

The other three didn't save me either. Kerouac gave me the length and none of the effect, so the sentence would run on and on and never get around to the part where it hits you. Vonnegut came back sounding like a children's book author floating through the void. Thompson felt like injecting caffeine into my veins at 2am with all of my agents cheering me on.

A model can take a writer apart and show you every gear in the machine. It can't hand you the reason the guy sat down to write it.

I kept at it anyway for about three months. First I tried the half measure, which is what everybody tries. Keep a few of the tendencies, hold onto the good parts, sand the rest off. Turns out the good parts come with the bad ones stapled to the back, and it kept popping up in the worst possible spots.

Then a month or two ago I broke down. I was looking into starting a Substack, and everything coming back at me felt cringy and had nothing to do with me or anything I'd actually lived. So I brought in the skill chain, injected more rules, fed it my own writing, and started scrubbing the authors out. It still tried way too hard to be elegant. The jokes were painfully unfunny. I had Claude write me exercises to get more of me and less of them, and the four of them were still surfacing here and there, which is when I gave up on the surgery and went over to Alex Lieberman's content machine approach.

The fix was the delete key. I pulled all four of them out completely, kept every word I'd written myself, and started fresh.

A few weeks ago OpenAI quietly stopped ChatGPT from writing in the style of named authors, living or dead. It's a legal move, copyright suits, lawyers doing lawyer things. The sanctioned workaround is that you can still describe the techniques, you just can't name the writer. I think people are going to be pretty disappointed with what comes back. No matter how hard you push a model to copy somebody's style and drop it into your own work, it won't feel right putting it out there in the world.

Here's what I know now that I didn't know on that ferry.

The models will try to break writing down like a math problem, and mechanics can only get you so far in this medium. Becoming a good writer takes time, lived experiences, and sleepless nights spilling your guts onto a keyboard because you need to get it out of you. You can start off copying your main influences. But as you keep typing, all of them slowly meld into one voice that only you could have come up with. Teaching a model to capture that voice works the same way.

So the job flipped. I don't ask it to sound like anyone now. I brain dump an idea into Claude, have it invoke the /creative-partner skill, and let it grill me on the pile I just spewed into it. It reads the whole mess and starts asking questions to make the idea coherent. It tells me where I could expand, where I might want to pivot, and pushes me in a few directions I wouldn't have gone on my own. Multiple rounds of that. Then it hands the final call back to me, every single time.

Four writers I love couldn't get me to sound like myself. A few rounds of questions did.

The authors are gone. My own writing stayed. Nobody's brought up my sisters since.
