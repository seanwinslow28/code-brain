Nothing Like Me
I stuffed Kerouac, Thompson, Sedaris, and Vonnegut into one skill. They did the assignment. I still sounded like a stranger.

I was commuting home on the Staten Island ferry, re-reading Dharma Bums, admiring the way Kerouac lets a sentence keep flowing so it can punch you more than once before you're done with it. Around then I found Nate B. Jones on YouTube. He has episodes about teaching a model your personal taste. I thought, what if I could make Claude write like this?

I thought I was being clever.

I researched four writers I love. Jack Kerouac, Hunter S. Thompson, David Sedaris, Kurt Vonnegut. I broke down how they write and crammed all of it into a single skill, the kind of thing that would supposedly pour that prose into my essays, my docs, my posts, anything that needed to not come out as typical AI slop.

The models can map the mechanics. They could not give me the soul sitting under any of it.

Every output came back foreign and cringy. Not because the model botched the job. It did exactly what I instructed. It just never sounded like me.

Sedaris ate most of the essays. That part is on me. Comedic deflation is how I tend to write, and the model grabbed his version of it. His version has a family in it. So the jokes kept landing on my mother and my sisters.

I'm an only child. I don't have sisters.

I'd be writing about agentic workflows and the whole thing would end on hanging out with my Mom. I can't remember the punch lines, which is probably a mercy. Family drama does not belong in a technical essay.

Kerouac showed up as length. The sentence would just keep going. No punch.

Vonnegut sounded like a children's book author who'd drifted into the void.

Thompson felt like I was injecting caffeine into my veins at 2am with all of my agents cheering me on.

I kept tinkering for about three months. I even tried the half measure, leaving some of their tendencies in so I wouldn't lose the good parts. They still popped up in the worst ways.

A month or two ago I was looking into starting a Substack, and I finally broke down. Everything coming back felt too cringy and had nothing to do with me, or with anything I'd actually lived. It was straining to be elegant. The jokes were painfully unfunny.

I pulled the authors out completely. Kept everything I'd written. Started fresh. Skill chain, more rules, my own work fed in, writing exercises from Claude to get more of me and less of them. It still leaked here and there. That's why I turned to Alex Lieberman's content machine.

What I didn't know on that ferry is that a model will break writing down like a math problem, and mechanics only get you so far in this medium. Getting good takes time, a life you've actually lived, and the nights you empty your guts onto a keyboard because it has to come out of you. You can start by copying your influences. Keep typing and they melt into one voice that only you could have come up with.

Teaching a model that voice works the same way.

I dump a messy idea into Claude now and invoke /creative-partner. It grills me. It digs through whatever I just spewed, asks questions until the thing is coherent, offers a pivot or a way to grow it, and shoves me around for a few rounds. I keep the last decision.

You can borrow somebody else's tricks all day. It still won't feel right putting that out in the world with your name on it.

I know because I tried.
