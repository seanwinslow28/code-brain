# What Are These Guys Even Doing?

*Ten agents running, and nothing noticeable coming out.*

I had Claude Code up and Obsidian up, going through the daily driver results, and I'd only set these things up a week or two earlier so I was excited to see what they'd made. There was nothing in there. Not bad output, nothing noticeable at all, just a lot of noise and a lot of background activity that wasn't showing any results or progress, and the whole thing felt like a mess.

It was supposed to be an idea machine.

I'd started the project hoping my vault would become something self-improving, where the agents would take the notes I fed in, go out and web search against them, make connections, log everything, and bring it back into my daily note as some new idea I wouldn't have thought of, or something I hadn't realized was connected. Notes, articles, research, life hacks, anything I plugged in. That was the point of all ten of them.

I was curious enough by then to check under the hood, so I asked Claude to tell me what these agents were actually doing, whether they were producing work, whether any of it was meaningful.

It came back with a lot of errors and poor output and things I didn't necessarily need.

The worst was an agent I'd called process-inbox, which had been burning through credits and failing every single time, wasting money and crashing because whatever I'd pointed it at was too much for it to get through, and it had been doing that for a week or two while I had no idea. Most of the rest were producing nothing either, a huge batch of failures. They couldn't reach my other tools in headless mode, which I tried to fix and couldn't, and the CLI was having issues in certain places.

I had thought all of these agents were doing the idea work for me. That's what I created them for, and that's what I assumed was going on every night while I slept. After the audit I found out what their roles actually were, and that they kept failing at those, and it turned out those were two separate problems. I had planned way too far ahead and overshot, off the back of a decent amount of research that led me astray, because it was never what I needed for my own projects.

So I put the kibosh on everything and went back through the list with Claude, cutting them, cutting them, cutting them. We need this. We don't need that. This one doesn't do anything for me. This one's repetitive. Ten agents, eight of them disabled, and the judgment in the end was mine.

Eight days after the cull I built my own vault synthesizer to finally get the agentic idea factory.

I waited a week to see if it would do what I was hoping it would do. It summarized. It took the notes and docs and research sitting in my vault and handed me back a sentence about what each thing was, and I didn't need that, because what I wanted was the thing that would take my ideas and give me something I probably wouldn't have thought of on my own. So I tinkered with it, and tinkered with it, and tinkered with it, and it took weeks before I worked out that the real problem was that it had no web search. It was never going out into the world at all. It was reading me my own notes back.

That's when I created my Vault Critic on Codex, which could actually look outside, and that became the beginning of the real idea factory I'd been after in the first place.

Now nothing starts until I've scoped it out with my creative-partner skill and we've gone back and forth long enough that we're both aligned on what I'm actually trying to build. It gives suggestions, and then finally we come to a conclusion, and then it goes onto a wayfinder map where every piece of it gets ticketed as a GitHub issue, and I knock those out one at a time, and at the end I test the thing to make sure it does what I built it to do.

The eight are still in there. I never took them out of code-brain and I never use them and I don't intend to. They sit in the project as a reminder of what not to do.
