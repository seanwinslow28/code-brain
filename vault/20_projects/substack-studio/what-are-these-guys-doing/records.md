## The MATTR finding — a conflict between two of the machine's own rules

The analyzer flagged narrower vocabulary than Sean's voice (0.805 against a 0.807 floor). Measured
before acting on it:

- **His own dictated answers on this topic: MATTR 0.733.**
- His typed answers, run #2: 0.821. His hand-rewritten final, run #2: 0.843.
- This draft: 0.805.

The draft is already **0.07 more lexically diverse than the only material it is legally allowed to
use.** Closing the remaining 0.002 requires adding words he did not say, which the origin law
forbids. **The analyzer is asking for something the constitution prohibits.**

Two consequences worth carrying forward:

1. **Dictation trades lexical diversity for volume.** 2.5x the words per answer, and speech repeats
   itself far more than written prose. Belongs on #197 as a measured cost beside the measured gain.
2. **The MATTR gate is mis-calibrated for transcript-only drafts.** Its baseline is built on
   finished prose. Any draft restricted to a dictated transcript starts near 0.73 and cannot reach
   0.807 honestly. The gate needs either a transcript-relative floor or an exemption for
   content-machine runs. Not fixed here; recorded.

One thing the flag DID surface correctly before it was dismissed: six of Sean's own distinctive
words never reached the draft (`under the hood`, `curious`, `agentic`, `cull`, `life hacks`,
`huge batch of failures`). That was under-shaping in a new form, reaching for plainer words of mine
instead of sharper ones of his. All six restored. **Synonym-swapping moved MATTR the wrong way;
restoring his vocabulary moved it the right way.** The lesson is about the source of the words, not
the arithmetic.
