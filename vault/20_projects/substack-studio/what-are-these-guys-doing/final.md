\# What Are These Guys Even Doing?

\*Ten agents running, and nothing noticeable coming out.\*

I had Claude Code up and Obsidian up, going through the daily driver results, and I'd only set these things up a week or two earlier so I was excited to see what they'd made. My excitement immediately turned to confusion when I found that there was nothing of use in there. Just rows  of blank markdown files from agents piling up jack and squat.

It was supposed to be an idea machine.

I'd started the project hoping my vault would become something self-improving, where the agents would take the notes I fed in, go out and web search against them, make connections, log everything, and bring it back into my daily note as some new idea I wouldn't have thought of, or something I hadn't realized was connected. Notes, articles, research, life hacks, anything I plugged in. 

I immediately pulled up Claude and told it to check under the hood to see what these agents were supposed to be doing and why my vault feels less like an agentic second brain and more of a glorified notes app. 

It came back and basically said, “This shit is fucked, dude”.

I had a process-inbox agent that was supposed to be organizing everything I fed inside the vault, but it had just been burning through credits and hitting a brick wall. I had multiple daily driver agents for different parts of the day to tell me what to focus on in the morning and what I had accomplished in the evening. They both failed each day and told me to figure it out on my own. The rest of the top notch squad either failed or served no purpose. 

After the audit I realized I’m mostly to blame. I read through each of their roles and I had no idea what I even assigned. I built these agents based off hours of research on random builders on the internet, creating their own personal agents that needed them for completely different jobs.

So I put the kibosh on everything and went back through the list with Claude. “We need this. We don't need that. This one doesn't do anything for me. This one's repetitive. That one owes me money, this one smells weird”.

Ten agents, eight of them disabled, and the judgment in the end was mine.

Eight days after the cull, I sat down and tried to accomplish what I initially set out to do with the previous batch. I built a vault synthesizer to finally get the agentic idea factory. I waited a week to see if it would do what I was hoping it would do. It took the notes, docs, and research sitting in my vault and handed me back a summarized sentence. “This is a blog post about Stripes Minions”.   
This was producing more of a… validation factory.  

So I tinkered with it and tried my best to have it spit out a semblance of an idea, but it just wrote three sentences instead of one. After another look under the hood and I realized it was just reading me my own notes back because it couldn’t access the web to actually expand on anything.

GOD DAMMIT. BACK TO THE DRAWING BOARD. HOW THE HELL AM I GOING TO–

Wait. What about… Codex? That should be able to access the web… and that’s when the Vault Critic was born.

After weeks of bashing my face against the keyboard, I like to think I’ve grown a bit from this experience. Now I have a full prep session before any building occurs. I scope it all out with my creative-partner skill, going back and forth until we're both aligned on what I'm actually trying to build. It gives suggestions, and then finally we come to a conclusion, and then it goes onto a wayfinder map where every piece of it gets ticketed as a GitHub issue, and I knock those out one at a time, and at the end I test the thing to make sure it does what I built it to do.

The eight are still in there. I never use them and I don't intend to. They sit in the project staring at me in a cold, dark folder. They sit there as a reminder of what not to do.
