> **Why it matters to the design:** this block sits a full altitude above the execution block.
> It states decisions (what the image should mean) rather than mechanics (how the marks get made).
> The Wince schema splits along exactly this seam because Sean independently produced both halves.

**1. CORE THESIS**
Make the image feel like the held breath before emergence: something meaningful is forming, but it has not announced itself yet.

**2. EMOTIONAL MODE**
Favor still beauty, quiet tension, patience, loneliness, and process over punchline, chaos, spectacle, or instant payoff.

**3. COMPOSITION**
Choose one focal idea and give it room to breathe. Negative space should feel intentional, not empty. A crowded frame must be avoided unless the crowding itself is the subject.

**4. NARRATIVE STRATEGY**
Internalize the drama. Let the viewer sense the story through posture, silence, framing, absence, and atmosphere instead of showing the "big event."

**5. MATERIAL / TEXTURE**
Preserve evidence of making: film grain, imperfect exposure, pencil construction lines, erasures, searching marks, paper tooth, layered drawing. Imperfection should feel like time and thought, not sloppiness.

**6. COLOR / LIGHT**
Color and light must carry emotional meaning. Use one dominant mood color or one restrained accent. Never apply "cinematic" lighting, god-rays, lens flares, or teal-orange grading as surface decoration.

**7. CHARACTER / EMOTION**
Emotion should read first through silhouette, body language, scale, and relation to the environment. Faces can underplay. Do not make characters explain the feeling with obvious expressions or text.

**8. NEVER DO**
No fake cinematic lighting, no detail without meaning, no clutter without a focal decision, no empty symmetry, no over-explained emotion, no obvious metaphors, no pasted-on whimsy. If an element can be removed without changing the idea, it does not belong.
