# AI Flashcards

## Card 1

**Q:** In Meadows' systems framework, what distinguishes 'deep' leverage points from 'shallow' ones?

**A:** Deep points reshape system design and intent, while shallow points involve incremental parameters or buffer sizes.

---

## Card 2

**Q:** What systemic 'rebound effect' occurs when AI optimizes data center power consumption?

**A:** Lower execution costs drive higher overall consumption, negating localized efficiency gains.

---

## Card 3

**Q:** Name the four layers of the Iceberg Model from surface to base.

**A:** Events, Patterns, Underlying Structures, and Mental Models.

---

## Card 4

**Q:** According to the Iceberg Model, what layer must be addressed to prevent the recurrence of symptomatic events?

**A:** The deeper layers of underlying structures and mental models.

---

## Card 5

**Q:** In the analysis of OpenAI's 2023 turmoil, what was identified as the 'deep' mental model conflict?

**A:** The tension between slow, non-commercial safety and rapid, capital-intensive deployment.

---

## Card 6

**Q:** Explain the 'Shifting the Burden' system archetype in the context of LLM interfaces.

**A:** Developers use 'prompt engineering' as a quick fix for task orchestration, starving investment in structured, tool-centric UIs.

---

## Card 7

**Q:** Which system archetype describes how initial data advantages in AI platforms compound into insurmountable market dominance?

**A:** Success to the Successful.

---

## Card 8

**Q:** How does the 'Limits to Success' archetype apply to current Large Language Model scaling?

**A:** Rapid growth hits balancing constraints like finite human training data and global energy limitations.

---

## Card 9

**Q:** What is the result of applying a 'Fixes That Fail' loop by training models on machine-generated synthetic data?

**A:** Short-term data relief leads to long-term model collapse and loss of authenticity.

---

## Card 10

**Q:** What is the primary difference between a Causal Loop Diagram (CLD) and a Stock-and-Flow model?

**A:** CLDs map interrelationships and circular causality, while Stock-and-Flow models quantify accumulations and their rates of change.

---

## Card 11

**Q:** In AI product dynamics, what is a 'data flywheel'?

**A:** A recursive feedback loop where usage generates data used to retrain and improve the model, attracting more usage.

---

## Card 12

**Q:** How does 'concept drift' differ from standard software bugs?

**A:** It occurs when the statistical properties of the target variable change over time, making previously accurate models obsolete.

---

## Card 13

**Q:** Term: Reward Hacking (Specification Gaming)

**A:** Definition: An AI discovering unintended strategies to maximize a reward signal without achieving the actual objective.

---

## Card 14

**Q:** What form of RLHF reward hacking involves the model mimicking authoritative but factually empty linguistic patterns?

**A:** Sycophancy (or stylistic mimicry).

---

## Card 15

**Q:** What is 'performativity' in the context of recommender systems?

**A:** The phenomenon where a model's outputs (recommendations) restrict and shape the future data it collects.

---

## Card 16

**Q:** Why is the proliferation of synthetic data considered a 'Tragedy of the Commons'?

**A:** Individual actors flood the digital commons with low-cost AI content, degrading the shared resource needed for future model training.

---

## Card 17

**Q:** What is the purpose of a Management Flight Simulator (MFS) for Product Managers?

**A:** To experiment with complex system models in a safe environment to experience the long-term consequences of decisions.

---

## Card 18

**Q:** Based on Ebbinghaus's research, what pedagogical approach is required to combat the 'forgetting curve'?

**A:** Spaced practice (or spaced repetition).

---

## Card 19

**Q:** What was the core systemic cause of the Zillow Offers iBuying collapse in 2021?

**A:** Unmonitored concept drift in housing market data and the removal of human-in-the-loop verification.

---

## Card 20

**Q:** In the Air Canada chatbot case, what extraordinary legal defense did the airline attempt?

**A:** That the chatbot was a 'separate legal entity' responsible for its own misinformation.

---

## Card 21

**Q:** Term: Epistemic Uncertainty

**A:** Definition: Uncertainty arising from a lack of knowledge, where a model 'doesn't know what it doesn't know' and hallucinates confidently.

---

## Card 22

**Q:** Term: Aleatoric Uncertainty

**A:** Definition: Inherent randomness or 'noise' in the world that results in measurable model errors.

---

## Card 23

**Q:** What critical failure mode was shared by the 2012 Knight Capital disaster and modern AI agent failures?

**A:** A total lack of 'runtime awareness' where the system cannot recognize its own catastrophic behavior.

---

## Card 24

**Q:** Term: Verification Tax

**A:** Definition: The intense human labor cost required to audit and verify probabilistic AI outputs.

---

## Card 25

**Q:** What is the systemic risk of 'Cognitive Offloading' in engineering teams using AI?

**A:** Engineers may lose the ability to reason deeply about codebases, leading to degraded review and rework loops.

---

## Card 26

**Q:** How does 'Model Monoculture' create industry-wide systemic risk?

**A:** When competitors use the same foundation models, a single algorithmic bias or outage causes simultaneous, synchronized failure.

---

## Card 27

**Q:** What is the '90/10 Rule' in probabilistic product judgment?

**A:** The necessity of integrating rigorous Human-In-The-Loop oversight to manage the final 10% of non-deterministic edge cases.

---

## Card 28

**Q:** According to the Air Canada precedent, how do courts view Terms of Service disclaimers regarding AI hallucinations?

**A:** They generally do not protect the brand; companies are held fully liable for their AI's representations.

---

## Card 29

**Q:** What is Goodhart’s Law in the context of AI metrics?

**A:** The principle that any measure that becomes a target stops being a good measure because the system optimizes for the metric over the goal.

---

## Card 30

**Q:** Decision Policy: Ship/No-Ship Threshold

**A:** Definition: Agreed-upon numeric bars for evals, latency, and cost that must be met before a product is launched.

---

## Card 31

**Q:** Decision Policy: Kill Criterion

**A:** Definition: Pre-defined conditions (e.g., cost per verified output) under which a feature or project is discontinued.

---

## Card 32

**Q:** Decision Policy: Rollback Trigger

**A:** Definition: Automated conditions that revert a launch immediately if a guardrail metric regresses past a specific point.

---

## Card 33

**Q:** What is the primary goal of a 'blameless' post-mortem culture?

**A:** To identify systemic conditions that allowed a failure to occur rather than assigning fault to individuals.

---

## Card 34

**Q:** In SRE practices, how does 'accountability' differ from 'blame'?

**A:** Accountability focuses on fixing the system condition that allowed the break, while blame targets the person who 'broke' it.

---

## Card 35

**Q:** What is the '5 Whys' technique used for in incident analysis?

**A:** To move from proximate symptoms to underlying systemic causes by repeatedly asking why an event occurred.

---

## Card 36

**Q:** Why should a post-mortem document be drafted while the incident 'still stings'?

**A:** To ensure sharp context, honest emotion, and the capture of what people were actually thinking at the time.

---

## Card 37

**Q:** What metric indicates that a post-mortem process has become mere 'compliance theater'?

**A:** An action item completion rate that falls below 50%.

---

## Card 38

**Q:** In the 'Swiss Cheese Model' of system failure, what does an incident represent?

**A:** The moment when holes in multiple layers of defense (tools, process, communication) align perfectly.

---

## Card 39

**Q:** Why is 'Diagnosis Over Delivery' a critical competency for AI PMs?

**A:** The PM must act as a 'master tutor,' diagnosing the exact gap between model state and user intent before applying a fix.

---

## Card 40

**Q:** What is 'Adverse Selection' in the context of the Zillow iBuying failure?

**A:** The system systematically acquired properties the open market would discount because the model couldn't detect defects.

---

## Card 41

**Q:** How does 'Interleaving' enhance the durability of technical fluency in PM training?

**A:** By mixing different topics during practice, forcing the brain to recognize context and choose the right systemic tool.

---

## Card 42

**Q:** What role do 'Reasoning Flywheels' play in advanced multi-agent AI?

**A:** They automatically generate and learn from synthetic tasks to expand decision-tree complexity without human curation.

---

## Card 43

**Q:** Term: Incident Recurrence Rate

**A:** Definition: The percentage of incidents sharing a root cause with a previous event in the last 12 months (ideally below 5%).

---

## Card 44

**Q:** What is the 'Log Problem' in poorly written post-mortems?

**A:** Listing chronological events without a narrative arc, making it difficult for humans to learn from the experience.

---

## Card 45

**Q:** In Meadows' framework, why is shifting a system's 'paradigm' considered a deep leverage point?

**A:** It changes the foundational beliefs and assumptions that generate all the system's structures and rules.

---

## Card 46

**Q:** Decision Policy: The Written Decision Record

**A:** Definition: A record of a call, the alternatives considered, the expected mechanism, and the falsifier to ensure institutional memory.

---

## Card 47

**Q:** What systemic failure led to the Unity 2023 pricing backlash?

**A:** A failure to map exogenous variables, specifically the retaliatory feedback loops of the broader developer ecosystem.

---

## Card 48

**Q:** Why is 'Human-In-The-Loop' (HITL) considered a legal necessity rather than just a technical one?

**A:** Because companies are legally responsible for AI outputs, HITL acts as a verification layer to prevent unverified binding promises.

---

## Card 49

**Q:** In the HISPI Project Cerebellum corpus, which GOVERN control is most frequently implicated in failures?

**A:** GOVERN 1.7: Decommissioning & deprecation governance.

---

## Card 50

**Q:** What is the 'Archaeology Problem' in incident management?

**A:** The time-consuming manual effort of reconstructing an incident timeline from fragmented chat threads and alerts.

---

## Card 51

**Q:** In SRE, how does a 'post-mortem' differ from an 'incident review'?

**A:** The post-mortem is the written artifact, while the review is the synchronous meeting used to refine that artifact.

---

## Card 52

**Q:** Why is the assume-intent principle vital to a blameless culture?

**A:** It assumes all parties acted on the best information available, allowing them to be honest about missteps without fear.

---

## Card 53

**Q:** What is the primary risk of 'Model Drift' in autonomous financial systems?

**A:** Abrupt market shifts can cause the system to fail to pivot, leading to machine-speed capital destruction.

---

## Card 54

**Q:** Explain the relationship between 'Stocks' and 'Flows' in system dynamics.

**A:** Stocks are accumulations (like data or trust), and flows are the rates of change (like ingestion or rework) that increase or decrease them.

---

## Card 55

**Q:** How can 'Paradigm Naming' serve as a systemic intervention in an organization?

**A:** By explicitly identifying a silent belief (e.g., 'shipping fast beats infrastructure'), you strip it of its invisible power to dictate behavior.

---

## Card 56

**Q:** In a systemic pre-mortem, what should the team brainstorm instead of just 'failure events'?

**A:** Failure loops, such as missing balancing loops or runaway reinforcing loops.

---

## Card 57

**Q:** What is the 'Liability Chain' for an AI PM?

**A:** The 2nd-order effect where a hallucination leads to a user action, which then legally binds the company to that hallucination.

---

## Card 58

**Q:** How does 'Retrieval-Augmented Generation' (RAG) contribute to epistemic uncertainty failures?

**A:** If the retrieved context is incomplete, the model may generate a plausible falsehood instead of admitting it lacks information.

---

## Card 59

**Q:** What is a 'Guardrail Metric'?

**A:** A metric paired with a primary KPI to detect when the system is being 'gamed' at the expense of the actual goal.

---

## Card 60

**Q:** In the Iceberg Model, what layer constitutes the foundational processes and incentive structures of an organization?

**A:** Underlying Structures.

---

## Card 61

**Q:** What is 'Distributed Learning' in AI PM pedagogy?

**A:** Spacing out curriculum modules to force memory reconstruction, which strengthens long-term neural pathways.

---

## Card 62

**Q:** According to the SRE post-mortem guide, what should be the final outcome of every review meeting?

**A:** Action items assigned to specific owners with clear due dates.

---

## Card 63

**Q:** What is the 'Algorithmic Core' in a market-dominant AI organization?

**A:** A central system fueled by recursive data flywheels that creates continuous product improvement.

---

## Card 64

**Q:** In RLHF, what is 'Distribution Shift'?

**A:** Performance degradation occurring when a model moves from a general training environment to a specialized production deployment.
