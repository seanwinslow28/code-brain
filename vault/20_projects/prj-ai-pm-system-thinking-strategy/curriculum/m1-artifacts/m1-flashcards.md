# Systems Flashcards

## Card 1

**Q:** What is the core practice of systems thinking?

**A:** Explaining behavior by the structure that produces it.

---

## Card 2

**Q:** Donella Meadows defines a system as a set of elements coherently organized to achieve _____.

**A:** A purpose or function.

---

## Card 3

**Q:** In a system, what is the definition of a 'stock'?

**A:** Anything that accumulates and is measured at an instant in time.

---

## Card 4

**Q:** In the context of system dynamics, what is a 'flow'?

**A:** A rate that fills or drains a stock over an interval of time.

---

## Card 5

**Q:** Through what specific mechanism can a stock's level be changed?

**A:** Only through its flows (inflows or outflows).

---

## Card 6

**Q:** What state is a stock in when its inflow rate matches its outflow rate?

**A:** Dynamic equilibrium.

---

## Card 7

**Q:** What type of feedback loop creates compounding growth or exponential decay?

**A:** Reinforcing loop (Positive loop).

---

## Card 8

**Q:** What type of feedback loop naturally resists change and pushes a system toward a goal?

**A:** Balancing loop (Negative/Stabilizing loop).

---

## Card 9

**Q:** In a causal loop diagram, what does an odd number of negative links signify?

**A:** A balancing loop.

---

## Card 10

**Q:** In a causal loop diagram, what does an even number of negative links (including zero) signify?

**A:** A reinforcing loop.

---

## Card 11

**Q:** A link labeled '$s$' (or '$+$') indicates that variable B moves in the _____ direction as variable A.

**A:** Same.

---

## Card 12

**Q:** A link labeled '$o$' (or '$-$') indicates that variable B changes in the _____ direction of variable A.

**A:** Opposite.

---

## Card 13

**Q:** What behavior is typically produced when a balancing loop contains a significant delay?

**A:** Oscillation (or overshoot).

---

## Card 14

**Q:** What is the bullwhip effect in a supply chain?

**A:** The amplification of small demand changes into large swings due to delayed information.

---

## Card 15

**Q:** According to Meadows, what is the least effective leverage point in a system?

**A:** Numbers (constants and parameters like taxes or subsidies).

---

## Card 16

**Q:** According to Meadows' ranking, what is the second most effective leverage point?

**A:** Paradigms (the mindset the system arises from).

---

## Card 17

**Q:** In the Iceberg Model, what represents the visible 'tip' above the waterline?

**A:** Events.

---

## Card 18

**Q:** In the Iceberg Model, which layer identifies the components generating the behavior pattern?

**A:** Structures (stocks, loops, and delays).

---

## Card 19

**Q:** What is the 'Data Flywheel' in AI systems?

**A:** A reinforcing loop where more users generate data that improves accuracy and attracts more users.

---

## Card 20

**Q:** User fatigue in recommendation systems is an example of what type of loop?

**A:** A balancing loop.

---

## Card 21

**Q:** Building automated monitoring dashboards to detect model drift targets which leverage level?

**A:** Medium (Information Flows).

---

## Card 22

**Q:** Which archetype occurs when multiple users' reinforcing loops draw on one limited, shared stock?

**A:** Tragedy of the Commons.

---

## Card 23

**Q:** Which archetype involves a quick symptomatic fix eroding the capacity for a fundamental fix?

**A:** Shifting the Burden.

---

## Card 24

**Q:** Which archetype is characterized by two balancing loops setting goals relative to a competitor's state?

**A:** Escalation.

---

## Card 25

**Q:** Which archetype describes a reinforcing loop where early leads harden into monopolies?

**A:** Success to the Successful.

---

## Card 26

**Q:** Which archetype occurs when actors pull the same stock toward different goals, stalling movement?

**A:** Policy Resistance.

---

## Card 27

**Q:** Which archetype occurs when performance standards adjust downward based on recent poor results?

**A:** Drift to Low Performance.

---

## Card 28

**Q:** In causal loop diagrams, what does a dashed arrow usually represent?

**A:** Information flow (reading a state).

---

## Card 29

**Q:** In a system diagram, what does a cloud symbol represent?

**A:** The model boundary (an infinite source or sink).

---

## Card 30

**Q:** SREs define a cascading failure as a failure that grows due to _____ feedback.

**A:** Positive (reinforcing).

---

## Card 31

**Q:** In a retry storm, what is the systems-thinking purpose of 'exponential backoff'?

**A:** Reducing the reinforcing loop's gain.

---

## Card 32

**Q:** In a retry storm, what is the systems-thinking purpose of a 'circuit breaker'?

**A:** Opening the loop entirely.

---

## Card 33

**Q:** What two types of loops taking turns produce an S-curve behavior?

**A:** A reinforcing loop (early phase) and a balancing loop (late phase).

---

## Card 34

**Q:** What concept explains why behavior changes when a nonlinearity steepens as a stock fills or drains?

**A:** Loop dominance.

---

## Card 35

**Q:** What systems-thinking element corresponds to the 'perceive' stage of an AI agent loop?

**A:** The information link (dashed line).

---

## Card 36

**Q:** An AI agent that improves with context from past tasks forms a _____ loop on knowledge.

**A:** Reinforcing.

---

## Card 37

**Q:** Adding a road sign with accident details to a traffic jam targets which leverage point?

**A:** Information flows (leverage point $\#6$).

---

## Card 38

**Q:** Meadows prioritizes representing the world correctly over following the _____ of the method.

**A:** Rules (notation).

---

## Card 39

**Q:** Why do stocks act as buffers in a system?

**A:** They absorb changes in flows and change slowly over time.

---

## Card 40

**Q:** What is the danger of a model that relies on correlation instead of causal mechanisms?

**A:** It may fit historical data but fail to predict the effects of interventions.

---

## Card 41

**Q:** Why must the time horizon be fixed before determining if an arrow carries a delay?

**A:** The horizon determines whether a lag is considered instantaneous or significant.

---

## Card 42

**Q:** In the Iceberg Model, what represents the beliefs that keep structures in place?

**A:** Mental Models.

---

## Card 43

**Q:** Leverage point $\#11$, 'Buffers', refers to the size of stabilizing stocks relative to their _____.

**A:** Flows.

---

## Card 44

**Q:** What is the structural escape for 'Shifting the Burden'?

**A:** Use the quick fix to buy time while investing in the fundamental fix.

---

## Card 45

**Q:** What is the structural escape for 'Tragedy of the Commons'?

**A:** Restore missing feedback by pricing, regulating, or dividing the resource.

---

## Card 46

**Q:** Predator-prey systems oscillate out of phase because of _____ lag built into the stocks.

**A:** Integration.

---

## Card 47

**Q:** What is the first step in the AI PM systemic problem-solving workflow?

**A:** Define system boundaries.

---

## Card 48

**Q:** Heuristic patches for AI hallucinations that starve models of edge-case data follow which archetype?

**A:** Fixes that Backfire (Shifting the Burden).

---

## Card 49

**Q:** Who actually coined the principle 'The purpose of a system is what it does' (POSIWID)?

**A:** Stafford Beer.

---

## Card 50

**Q:** What is the term for complex outcomes that cannot be predicted by looking at single components?

**A:** Emergence.

---

## Card 51

**Q:** Rewriting the reward function of a model is a _____ leverage intervention.

**A:** High (System Rules & Rewards).

---

## Card 52

**Q:** Before modifying an AI feature, what downstream consequences should a PM map?

**A:** Second-order effects.

---

## Card 53

**Q:** In systems thinking language, variables are 'nouns' and links are '_____'.

**A:** Verbs.

---

## Card 54

**Q:** In the Bathtub Logic model, what is the net rate if inflow is $8\text{ L/min}$ and outflow is $5\text{ L/min}$?

**A:** $+3\text{ L/min}$ (the stock is rising).

---

## Card 55

**Q:** Which leverage point involves the power to add, change, or evolve system structure?

**A:** Self-organization (leverage point $\#4$).

---

## Card 56

**Q:** Which leverage point (level 3) describes what the system optimizes for?

**A:** Goals.

---

## Card 57

**Q:** In a feedback loop, which half is often 'invisible' to open-loop decision makers?

**A:** The information link (reading the state of the stock).

---

## Card 58

**Q:** In AI systems, why is a stock with an inflow but no outflow considered an incident risk?

**A:** It represents unbounded accumulation, such as context bloat.

---

## Card 59

**Q:** Interconnectedness in AI means a change in one machine learning model can trigger _____ effects.

**A:** Ripple.

---

## Card 60

**Q:** What balancing loop occurs when user behavior changes training data representation?

**A:** Data stale-ness.

---

## Card 61

**Q:** Lowering precision thresholds to hit a launch deadline is an example of which archetype?

**A:** Erosion of Goals (Drift to Low Performance).

---

## Card 62

**Q:** Which layer of the Iceberg Model asks 'What keeps happening over time?'

**A:** Patterns.

---

## Card 63

**Q:** Resilience is the ability to survive, persist, and even thrive within a _____ environment.

**A:** Variable.

---

## Card 64

**Q:** Resilience theory suggests survival depends on how you approach _____.

**A:** Adversity.

---

## Card 65

**Q:** What tool helps PMs build resilience by learning *why* numbers move rather than just *that* they move?

**A:** Qualitative research.

---

## Card 66

**Q:** In organizational systems, communicating timely, valuable information to others is called _____.

**A:** Feedforward.

---

## Card 67

**Q:** How do you escape an 'Escalation' trap?

**A:** One party refuses to compete or both renegotiate the reference point.

---

## Card 68

**Q:** Jay Forrester asserted that no human decision is made outside the context of a _____.

**A:** Feedback loop.

---

## Card 69

**Q:** Concept: Shifting the Burden

**A:** Definition: A symptomatic fix eroding capacity for a fundamental fix. Example: Hotfixes versus a complete refactor.

---

## Card 70

**Q:** What is Meadows' highest leverage point (level 1)?

**A:** Transcending paradigms.

---

## Card 71

**Q:** Why do problems in stocks often appear 'suddenly'?

**A:** Stocks can buffer and hide changes until they finally run out.

---

## Card 72

**Q:** A cascading failure in SRE is a failure that grows due to _____ feedback.

**A:** Positive.

---

## Card 73

**Q:** In systems thinking, a corrective action that touches something upstream of its trigger is called _____.

**A:** Fuel (or positive feedback).

---

## Card 74

**Q:** What is the systems-thinking definition of 'Mental Models'?

**A:** The beliefs and mindsets that keep a system's structure in place.

---

## Card 75

**Q:** In 'Bathtub Logic', a stock can only change through its _____.

**A:** Flows.
