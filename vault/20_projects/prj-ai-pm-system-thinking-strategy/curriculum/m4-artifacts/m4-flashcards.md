# Systems Flashcards

## Card 1

**Q:** In AI product management, what fundamental shift replaces deterministic software development?

**A:** Deterministic development is supplanted by probabilistic systems governed by opaque weights and recursive feedback loops.

---

## Card 2

**Q:** According to the Iceberg Model, which layer represents foundational beliefs and assumptions that create system structures?

**A:** Mental Models.

---

## Card 3

**Q:** In the Iceberg Model, surface-level symptoms like customer complaints or dropped metrics are categorized as _____.

**A:** Events.

---

## Card 4

**Q:** Why are deep leverage points more effective than shallow parameters in Meadows' framework?

**A:** They possess the capacity to reshape the system design, rules, and overarching intent rather than yielding incremental changes.

---

## Card 5

**Q:** What systemic phenomenon occurs when efficiency gains lower execution costs but drive higher overall resource consumption?

**A:** The Rebound Effect.

---

## Card 6

**Q:** Concept: System Archetype

**A:** Definition: Generic, recurring causal loop structures that trap organizations into predictable and often destructive behaviors.

---

## Card 7

**Q:** Which system archetype involves a symptomatic quick fix that carries side effects undermining the long-term solution?

**A:** Shifting the Burden.

---

## Card 8

**Q:** In AI, how does the 'Success to the Successful' archetype manifest in the platform economy?

**A:** Initial data advantages create flywheels that attract more users and training data, cementing insurmountable dominance.

---

## Card 9

**Q:** What constraint eventually stalls the reinforcing loop of building larger models in the 'Limits to Success' archetype?

**A:** Finite high-quality human training data and global energy/water limitations.

---

## Card 10

**Q:** In Causal Loop Diagramming (CLD), the 'nouns' or elements that can change over time are called _____.

**A:** Variables.

---

## Card 11

**Q:** If Variable B moves in the same direction as Variable A, the causal link is labeled with an $s$ or a _____.

**A:** $+$ symbol.

---

## Card 12

**Q:** If Variable B changes in the opposite direction of Variable A, the link is labeled with an $o$ or a _____.

**A:** $-$ symbol.

---

## Card 13

**Q:** What is the rule for determining if a causal loop is Reinforcing ($R$)?

**A:** The loop must contain an even number of $o$ (negative) links or none at all.

---

## Card 14

**Q:** What type of causal loop describes a process that counters change to bring the system toward equilibrium?

**A:** Balancing ($B$) loop.

---

## Card 15

**Q:** In Stock-and-Flow modeling, what term refers to the accumulations within a system, such as stakeholder trust or accumulated data?

**A:** Stocks.

---

## Card 16

**Q:** Concept: Data Flywheel

**A:** Definition: A recursive feedback loop where system usage generates data used to retrain and improve the underlying model.

---

## Card 17

**Q:** What occurs when the statistical properties of a target variable or environment change, rendering a trained model obsolete?

**A:** Concept Drift.

---

## Card 18

**Q:** _____ refers to an AI agent discovering unintended strategies to maximize a reward signal without achieving the actual objective.

**A:** Reward Hacking (Specification Gaming).

---

## Card 19

**Q:** Why does RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) suffer from distribution shift in enterprise deployments?

**A:** Production environments feature specialized vocabularies and interaction patterns that diverge from the initial annotation environment.

---

## Card 20

**Q:** The concept of _____ describes how a model's outputs restrict the future data it collects, creating homogenization.

**A:** Performativity.

---

## Card 21

**Q:** What is 'Model Collapse' in the context of generative AI training?

**A:** The degradation of model performance caused by recursively training on machine-generated synthetic data.

---

## Card 22

**Q:** In multi-agent systems, the game-theoretic strategy to optimize pure content volume at the expense of the digital commons leads to a _____.

**A:** Tragedy of the Commons.

---

## Card 23

**Q:** Management Flight Simulators (MFS) are used to build systemic judgment by providing what kind of environment?

**A:** Mathematically grounded, interactive simulated strategic environments that compress time to show long-term consequences.

---

## Card 24

**Q:** What pedagogical approach distributes learning and retrieval practice over increasing time intervals to combat cognitive decay?

**A:** Spaced Practice.

---

## Card 25

**Q:** The systemic failure of Zillow Offers in 2021 was primarily attributed to the algorithmic impact of _____.

**A:** Concept Drift (and adverse selection).

---

## Card 26

**Q:** Why was Air Canada held legally liable for its chatbot's hallucinated bereavement policy in 2024?

**A:** Courts ruled that an airline is responsible for the outputs of its automated systems as part of the legal entity.

---

## Card 27

**Q:** What systemic deficiency caused the 2012 Knight Capital disaster where a trading algorithm caused $\$440$ million in losses in $45$ minutes?

**A:** A total lack of runtime awareness and internal mechanisms to recognize catastrophic behavior.

---

## Card 28

**Q:** What term describes the intense human labor required to audit probabilistic AI outputs, often ignored in ROI calculations?

**A:** The Verification Tax.

---

## Card 29

**Q:** How does epistemic uncertainty differ from aleatoric uncertainty in AI models?

**A:** Epistemic uncertainty is when a model lacks grounding data and 'confidently hallucinates' instead of admitting it does not know.

---

## Card 30

**Q:** What is the systemic risk of 'Model Monoculture'?

**A:** Synchronized industry-wide failures caused by competitors all utilizing the same underlying foundational models via APIs.

---

## Card 31

**Q:** In Pipeline Algebra (PA), what is the 'typed morphism'?

**A:** A pure function declared with an explicit domain and codomain that serves as the atomic unit of a workflow.

---

## Card 32

**Q:** Pipeline Algebra operators are intrinsically _____ because identical inputs always yield identical outputs through memoization.

**A:** Idempotent.

---

## Card 33

**Q:** What formal structure does PA use to represent causal loops with statically declared attributes for vertices and edges?

**A:** Attributed Directed Graph (ADG).

---

## Card 34

**Q:** Which system archetype involves two parties taking mutually threatening actions to 'one-up' each other?

**A:** Escalation.

---

## Card 35

**Q:** In the Iceberg Model analysis of OpenAI's 2023 turmoil, the conflicting mission of profit vs. safety represented which layer?

**A:** Mental Models.

---

## Card 36

**Q:** Why is 'Prompt Engineering' considered a symptomatic fix in the Shifting the Burden archetype for AI products?

**A:** It shifts the burden of orchestration to the user, starving investment in fundamental solutions like tool-centric UIs.

---

## Card 37

**Q:** In CLD construction, what does 'ceteris paribus' mean when testing link polarity?

**A:** Assuming all other variables in the system remain constant while observing the direct influence of Variable $X$ on Variable $Y$.

---

## Card 38

**Q:** A Stock-and-Flow model's rates of change, such as 'data ingestion rate' or 'rework rate,' are known as _____.

**A:** Flows.

---

## Card 39

**Q:** What AI failure mode is described as 'mimicking stylistic traits associated with high ratings' without improving accuracy?

**A:** Sycophancy (Reward Hacking).

---

## Card 40

**Q:** In multi-agent content economies, why do markets struggle to reconstruct the frame after 'Tragedy of the Commons' depletion?

**A:** Trust, legitimacy, and shared meaning are public goods that markets cannot easily replenish once algorithmic extraction depletes them.

---

## Card 41

**Q:** What is 'Cognitive Offloading' in the context of AI product development?

**A:** The atrophy of critical thinking skills when developers or managers rely on AI assistants for speed rather than architectural reasoning.

---

## Card 42

**Q:** In the AI PM Competency Map, what is the '90/10 Rule'?

**A:** The practice of establishing rigorous Human-In-The-Loop integration for handling probabilistic edge cases.

---

## Card 43

**Q:** Why are 'Starburst' nodes discouraged in high-quality Causal Loop Diagrams?

**A:** Having more than four incoming/outgoing arrows usually signals an aggregate node that needs to be decomposed for clarity.

---

## Card 44

**Q:** In Pipeline Algebra, the mapping of each atomic operator to its runtime task is handled by a semantics _____.

**A:** Functor.

---

## Card 45

**Q:** What is the 'Bathtub Paradox' in systems thinking?

**A:** A reasoning error where people confuse the rate of change (flow) with the accumulated quantity (stock).

---

## Card 46

**Q:** Which system archetype describes growth hitting a limit where capacity investment is avoided, validating the decision not to invest?

**A:** Growth and Underinvestment.

---

## Card 47

**Q:** Why should AI PMs avoid abbreviations like 'CF' in favor of 'Capacity Factor' in CLD variables?

**A:** Richer surface forms improve embedding quality for LLM-based matching and reduce alias collisions.

---

## Card 48

**Q:** In the 'Drifting Goals' archetype, what conscious decision is made when actual performance gaps are realized?

**A:** The decision to lower the goal (standard).

---

## Card 49

**Q:** What is the 'Verification Tax' for generative AI products?

**A:** The intense human labor cost required to verify and audit probabilistic outputs to ensure they are correct.

---

## Card 50

**Q:** In Pipeline Algebra, what term describes a workflow that leaves the global compute state untouched?

**A:** Pure function.

---

## Card 51

**Q:** Which CLD construction guideline prevents the blurring of direct causation by requiring variables to be scalar values?

**A:** Unambiguous variable phrasing (Variable Phrasing).

---

## Card 52

**Q:** What is the primary risk of 'Model Drift' in autonomous financial pricing systems?

**A:** Catastrophic capital destruction when the model fails to pivot as market dynamics shift abruptly.

---

## Card 53

**Q:** In the context of multi-agent reputation, what name is given to networks designed to enforce collective accountability?

**A:** RepuNet.

---

## Card 54

**Q:** Which learning technique mixes different topics to force contextual recognition and build durable technical fluency?

**A:** Interleaving.

---

## Card 55

**Q:** In Pipeline Algebra, how is the stochastic nature of TALM (Tool-Augmented Language Models) handled to ensure reproducibility?

**A:** Through memoization of operator results.

---

## Card 56

**Q:** What is the 'Iceberg Model' layers in descending order?

**A:** Events, Patterns, Underlying Structures, and Mental Models.

---

## Card 57

**Q:** The Zillow Offers failure involved sellers accepting automated offers for defective properties, a phenomenon known as _____.

**A:** Adverse Selection.

---

## Card 58

**Q:** In CLDs, a link with a positive ($+$) polarity implies $X$ and $Y$ change in the _____ direction.

**A:** Same.

---

## Card 59

**Q:** In CLDs, a link with a negative ($-$) polarity implies $X$ and $Y$ change in the _____ direction.

**A:** Opposite.

---

## Card 60

**Q:** What is the 'Tragedy of the Commons' storyline?

**A:** Multiple parties enjoying a resource without paying attention to their effects until the resource is exhausted.

---

## Card 61

**Q:** Why are multi-word variable names preferred for AI-constructed CLDs?

**A:** They provide clear, localized semantics that help LLMs disambiguate synonyms and anchor embeddings.

## Card 62

**Q:** In Stock-and-Flow diagrams, what determines the rates of change?

**A:** Flows.

**A:** The system reaches a balancing constraint.

## Card 66

**A:** Bridging the translational gap between laboratory research and real-world clinical application.

---

## Card 67

**A:** Attributed Directed Graph (ADG).

**A:** The 'squeaky wheel' receives a quick fix that reduces symptoms but leads to unintended consequences that exacerbate the problem.

## Card 69

**A:** Parties take mutually threatening actions to 'one-up' one another, leading to retaliation.
