# Productcraft solo-builder shelf: findings

**Question.** Which books or substantial free canons treat the solo founder (org-of-one) as the operating unit on the five thin lanes from engagement one, and how well does each cover them?

**The five gaps (short names used below)**
- **G1 Leadership:** stakeholder stance when stakeholder and team are one person; who reviews and decides when AI agents do the work and the founder is the only human.
- **G2 Business:** pre-revenue cost case with no price, revenue or retention curve; founder hours as the scarce cost line.
- **G3 Discovery:** solo researcher who is also recruiter and sole executor (bias control with no second person); what a standing weekly commitment displaces.
- **G4 Delivery:** OKRs and roadmap sequencing for a solo pre-revenue builder.
- **G5 Insights:** reading a 2–8 person pilot (small-N inference, value of information).

**Method.** Author and publisher pages, author-hosted sample PDFs (tables of contents read directly where available), the authors' own essays, and a few chapter-level reviews by practitioners. No SEO listicles were used as evidence. Summary sites (Blinkist, Bookey, Shortform) were skipped. "Year" is first publication of the edition checked.

## Headline finding

The shelf splits in two, and neither half fills the gaps.

1. **Books that make the solo founder the operating unit** (Jarvis, Walling's *Start Small, Stay Small*, Levels' *MAKE*, Tringas' Micro-SaaS essays, partly Lavingia and Kahl) are mostly about mindset, niche selection, launch and lifestyle. They touch G2 (founder hours, profit before growth) and little else.
2. **Books that cover the gaps with real method** (Hubbard for G5, *The Mom Test* for G3, Maurya's *Running Lean* 3e for G2/G4) assume a founder or a team, not an org-of-one. They transfer well, but a seat will have to do the adaptation.

**No named candidate covers G1** (org-of-one leadership, or review and decision rights over AI-agent work). **No named candidate covers G3's core problem** either: none treats bias control without a second person as its subject. A few give techniques one person can run. Both are stated as "no named candidate covers it", not as proof that nothing exists. The list checked is in the table below.

## Candidate notes

### Hubbard, *How to Measure Anything* (3rd ed., Wiley 2014): shelf-worthy for G5
- **What it actually says (G5).** Ch. 9, *Sampling Reality*, and Ch. 10, *Bayes: Adding to What You Know Now*, cover small-sample inference. That includes the t-statistic's origin as a small-sample tool and non-parametric shortcuts: with eight observations, the second-smallest and second-largest values give a rough 90% interval. The chapters also argue that statistical significance is not a fixed minimum sample size. ([chapter review](https://tcagley.wordpress.com/2016/02/13/how-to-measure-anything-chapter-9-sampling-reality-how-observing-some-things-tells-us-about-all-things/); [O'Reilly ch. 7 listing](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/how-to-measure/9781118836446/c07.xhtml))
- **Rule of Five, confirmed.** Take a random sample of five from any population. There is a 93.75% chance the population *median* lies between the sample's smallest and largest values. The **Urn of Mystery** (single-sample majority rule) says that with a uniform prior on a proportion, one random draw has a 75% chance of coming from the majority. Both rules **assume random selection** ([Hubbard Decision Research](https://hubbardresearch.com/two-ways-you-can-use-small-sample-sizes-to-measure-anything/)). A hand-recruited pilot of friends is not random, so these rules bound the *median of the recruited pool*, not of the market. The seat must say this out loud.
- **Value of information (G2/G3 prioritisation).** Ch. 7, *Quantifying the Value of Information*, covers expected opportunity loss and EVPI as the ceiling on what to spend measuring. The first few observations reduce uncertainty the most. The "measurement inversion" point: the highest-value variables are often the ones nobody measures ([chapter review](https://tcagley.wordpress.com/2016/01/30/how-to-measure-anything-chapter-7-quantifying-the-value-of-information/)). VOI priced in *founder hours* is a direct bridge to G2.
- **Bonus for G1/G3.** Ch. 5, *Calibrated Estimates*, trains one person to calibrate their own probability judgements. It is one of the few bias controls on this list that needs no second person.
- **Not solo-framed:** examples are corporate and government. **Availability:** retail (Wiley/O'Reilly), plus a *Workbook* (Wiley). No author-sold DRM-free edition found.

### Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013): shelf-worthy for G3
- **G3.** The whole book assumes the founder is the interviewer. Ch. 6, *Finding Conversations*, covers recruiting (warm intros, immersion, creating a reason to talk), so the recruiter role is covered too. The core bias controls are *question-design rules* one person can apply alone: ask about the person's past behaviour, not your idea or their future opinions, and talk less. Ch. 5, *Commitment and Advancement*, swaps opinions for costly commitments (time, money, reputation) as the signal. That is a bias check that needs no second observer. ([author site](https://www.momtestbook.com/); [chapter review](https://dev.to/egepakten/the-mom-test-chapter-5-commitment-and-advancement-1k27))
- **Where it assumes a team.** Ch. 8, *Running the Process*, is built on sharing notes and reviewing calls with the founding team, and warns against becoming a "learning bottleneck" ([ch. 8 review](https://dev.to/egepakten/what-i-learned-from-the-mom-test-chapter-8-running-the-process-242h)). In an org-of-one the founder *is* the bottleneck by construction. That is exactly the unfilled part of G3.
- **G5 (partial):** Ch. 7, *Choosing Your Customers* (customer slicing), narrows the segment so a handful of conversations is enough.
- **Availability:** PDF sold direct via Gumroad (author-sold). DRM status not stated, but Gumroad files are normally unencrypted ([author site](https://www.momtestbook.com/)).

### Maurya, *Running Lean* (3rd ed., O'Reilly 2022): shelf-worthy for G2 and G4
- **G2 (partial).** The viability test replaces a spreadsheet forecast with a Fermi estimate. Set a minimum success criterion over three years, assume a revenue per customer, divide to get the customers needed, then sanity-check that against the reachable market. It runs *before* any revenue, on an assumed price ([author newsletter, 2025-07-17](https://ashmaurya.com/newsletter/2025-07-17)). The gap it leaves: the cost side is thin, and founder hours are not a line item. Pair it with Cohen (below).
- **G4 (substantive).** The *traction roadmap* is an outcome roadmap that works backward from the three-year goal in roughly 10x stages. Early products use yearly horizons, and products with traction use Now / Next / Later at 90 / 180 / 360 days. The book runs work in 90-day cycles ([author blog, "Say No to Product Roadmaps", 2024](https://ashmaurya.com/blog/say-no-to-product-roadmaps)). Parts: I Design, II Validation, III Growth ([O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/running-lean-3rd/9781098108762/)). The 90-day review ritual is written for a team, but the milestone logic transfers to one person. Nothing here is framed as OKRs.
- **Availability:** retail. The full ToC could not be fetched (O'Reilly returned 403), so chapter-level claims come from the author's own posts.

### Jason Cohen, *A Smart Bear* longform essays (free, 2012–present): shelf-worthy free canon
- **G2 (substantive).** *What is the value of one hour of a startup founder's time?* (2012) builds a risk-adjusted price for a founder hour. Start from a consulting rate, divide by the survival odds, and add a premium because the founder can't diversify. Cohen lands near $1,000/hour and uses that figure as a delegation and prioritisation rule ([essay](https://longform.asmartbear.com/value-time/)). This is the only source found that turns founder hours into an explicit cost line.
- **G3 (partial).** *The Iterative-Hypothesis customer development method* (2022) recommends writing hypotheses down *before* interviews, since recording predictions in advance makes people more objective. It also bans leading questions and says to stop when the surprises stop, not after an arbitrary count ([essay](https://longform.asmartbear.com/customer-development/)). *When you have nothing: how to find potential customers to interview* (2024) covers recruiting from zero and warns that friends and family give a validation signal the market won't ([essay](https://longform.asmartbear.com/find-customers-to-interview/)). All of this is solo-runnable.
- **G1 (touches):** decision essays (*How to simplify complex decisions by cleaving the facts*, *When should a decision be fast, or slow?*) are general, not org-of-one ([index](https://longform.asmartbear.com/)).

### Walling, *Start Small, Stay Small* (self-published 2010): partial, and the most explicitly solo book
- **Solo as unit: yes.** The preface defines the *Micropreneur* as an entrepreneur who stays solo, with no employees under any circumstances. ToC: The Chasm Between Developer and Entrepreneur; Why Niches are the Name of the Game; Your Product; Building a Killer Sales Website; Startup Marketing; Virtual Assistants and Outsourcing; Grow it or Start Over ([author-hosted ch. 1 sample](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c868198797f744833c9480e/t/5c86ce8b71c10b46a7157d49/1552338580638/startsmall-chapter1.pdf)).
- **G2 (partial):** ch. 1 asks the founder to put a dollar value on their own hours to decide what to outsource ([2023 re-read](https://yanirseroussi.com/til/2023/08/16/revisiting-start-small-stay-small-in-2023-chapter-1/)). Ch. 6 applies that to virtual assistants.
- **G3 (touches):** the niche chapter tests demand through search-keyword volume and small ad tests. That is non-interview evidence, useful as a check on a solo interviewer, but the tactics date from 2010.
- **Availability:** current site lists Amazon paperback, Kindle and Audible only ([startsmall.com](https://startsmall.com/)).

### Jarvis, *Company of One* (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2019): partial
- ToC includes: Defining a Company of One; Staying Small as an End Goal; **What's Required to Lead**; Growing a Company That Doesn't Grow; The One Customer; Scalable Systems; Launching and Iterating in Tiny Steps (chapter list from search-surfaced summaries, not publisher-verified; [review](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-company-of-one/)).
- **G1 (touches):** "What's Required to Lead" is about autonomy and giving clear direction. It does not deal with role conflict inside one person or review rights. Jarvis's "company of one" also includes small teams and employees inside larger firms, so it is not strictly solo.
- **G2 (partial):** *minimum viable profit* (profit before growth, with upper as well as lower income targets) gives a cost-side framing, but no method.
- **Availability:** retail only.

### Croll & Yoskovitz, *Lean Analytics* (O'Reilly, 2013): partial
- **Empathy stage** (*What Stage Are You At?*, then *Stage One: Empathy*): has a section on avoiding leading the witness and a floor of about 15 interviews per stage ([O'Reilly ch. 15 listing](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/lean-analytics/9781449335687/ch15.html); [search-surfaced chapter content](https://valentin.vincendon.com/book-summaries/lean-analytics)). The authors' problem-interview **scoring rubric** gives a 25-point pass line across ranking, active workarounds, engagement, referral and payment signals ([author blog, 2012](https://leananalyticsbook.com/scoring-problem-interviews/)). That is a usable G3/G5 instrument for reading a handful of interviews.
- **Mobile-app model: does *not* address a pre-revenue cost case.** The free-mobile-app model's metrics assume an install base and in-app revenue (downloads, activation, share of paying users, revenue per user, churn). The Empathy stage is qualitative. **G2 not covered.**
- Audience is explicitly "lean entrepreneur or intrapreneur", not solo ([author sneak-peek PDF](http://leananalyticsbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/Lean-Analytics-sneak-peek.pdf)). **Availability:** retail.

### Weinberg & Mares, *Traction* (Portfolio, 2015): partial
- **G4 (partial):** the *Critical Path* sets one traction goal and the minimum milestones to reach it, and treats everything else as distraction. The *50% rule* splits time between product and traction. **G3's displacement question (touches):** the 50% rule is the only explicit time-allocation rule among the books ([review](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-traction/)). A solo bootstrapper's review judged it aimed at VC-backed startups with money to spend ([mtlynch.io](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/traction/)). Author site unreachable during this check.

### Paul Graham essays (free): partial
- *The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups* (2006) makes the single founder mistake #1. The reason given: no colleague to talk you out of bad decisions or carry you through low points ([essay](https://paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html)). It **names G1 as a risk and offers no remedy**.
- *Do Things that Don't Scale* (2013): the founder recruits first users by hand and learns the most from them ([essay](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html)). That is G3's recruiter role, without any bias treatment.
- *Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule* (2009): one meeting can cost a maker half a day ([essay](https://paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)). **This is the closest source for G3's "what a standing weekly commitment displaces."**
- *Default Alive or Default Dead?* and *Ramen Profitable* assume revenue or expense curves, so they don't help much before revenue.

### Tringas, *Building Micro-SaaS Businesses* (free essay-book, 2016–17): partial
- Ch. 6 argues for capping work at 15–20 hours a week and against computing an hourly rate for early SaaS work, because each support interaction compounds. Ch. 7 covers retention and support as a solo founder ([ch. 6](https://tylertringas.com/chapter-6-thriving-in-the-long-slow-saas-grind/); [archive](https://tylertringas.com/the-micro-saas-ebook/)). **Useful as the counter-position to Cohen on G2:** Cohen prices the hour high, Tringas refuses to price it at all. Ch. 4 rejects pre-launch validation, which cuts against G3.

### Basecamp, *Getting Real* (2006, free online) and *REWORK* (2010): partial / off-target
- *Getting Real* chapters: **Fix Time and Budget, Flex Scope**; Shrink Your Time; Race to Running Software; The Three Musketeers (a team of three); Alone Time; Hire Less and Hire Later ([free book](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal)). This is G4 sequencing by fixed time budget, with a small team assumed. *Shape Up* (free) adds *appetite*, meaning time budgeted rather than estimated, but openly assumes teams and a betting table ([Shape Up](https://basecamp.com/shapeup)). *REWORK*'s "Planning is guessing" is a stance, not a method. Retail only.

### Kahl, *Zero to Sold* (2020) and *The Embedded Entrepreneur* (c. 2021–22): partial
- *Zero to Sold* stages: Preparation, Survival (incl. Mental Health, Pricing), Stability (incl. Building a Team), Growth, plus a bonus chapter, "The Many Roles of a Bootstrapped Founder" ([author guide](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/zero-to-sold/guide/)). That bonus is the nearest thing to G1's role conflict, but its content could not be read in this pass. FeedbackPanda was **two co-founders**, not solo ([author sample PDF](https://zerotosold.com/sample.pdf)).
- *The Embedded Entrepreneur*: audience discovery, problem discovery and validation, driven by watching online communities ([site](https://embeddedentrepreneur.com/)). This is G3 evidence that doesn't come from interviews, which dilutes interviewer bias, but the book doesn't frame it that way.
- **Availability:** both sold **DRM-free via Gumroad** (author-sold) as well as at retail.

### Walling, *The SaaS Playbook* (2023): off-target for this engagement
- The author scopes it himself to businesses with "some semblance of product-market fit", roughly $10–20k MRR and up. Sections: Market, Pricing, Marketing, Team, 80/20 SaaS Metrics, Mindset. It points pre-PMF lifestyle founders back to *Start Small, Stay Small* ([author-hosted sample](https://thesaasplaybook.squarespace.com/s/the-saas-playbook-sample.pdf)). **Worth holding for the B2B-leaning engagement once it has revenue**, not before. Sold via [saasplaybook.com](https://saasplaybook.com/) and retail.

### Off-target and adjacent, in brief
- **Lavingia, *The Minimalist Entrepreneur* (2021):** seven steps (profitability first; start with community; build as little as possible; sell to your first 100 customers; market by being you; grow mindfully; build the house you want to live in) ([chapter summary](https://jonathanrintala.com/book-summary/the-minimalist-entrepreneur/)). Selling to the first 100 customers doubles as discovery, but there's no method for pre-revenue cost, bias or small-N.
- **Levels, *MAKE*:** written for solo indie makers. Phases run idea → build → launch → grow → monetize → automate → exit. Validation means solving your own problem. Sold direct, watermarked, not DRM-encrypted ([readmake.com](https://readmake.com/)). It is the purest solo-unit book on the list and addresses none of the five gaps with method.
- **Sivers, *Anything You Want*:** "Delegate, but don't abdicate" and "How do you grade yourself?" ([sive.rs](https://sive.rs/a)) are the closest analogue to G1's review-rights question for delegated work, written about human staff. Sold DRM-free direct.
- **Duke, *How to Decide* (2020):** individual decision hygiene. Its feedback tools rely on other people ([publisher](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/610848/how-to-decide-by-annie-duke/)). It was not checked chapter by chapter.
- **Recent AI-agent governance writing (2026)** is enterprise-framed: autonomy tiers, approval fatigue ([example](https://kanbanzone.com/2026/who-owns-the-outcome-when-an-ai-agent-acts/)). None of it is written for an org whose only reviewer is its only human.

## Ranked recommendation (max 4)

1. **Hubbard, *How to Measure Anything*.** The one real G5 text. Its VOI chapter also prices *which* discovery is worth founder hours (G2/G3). Add a standing note that the Rule of Five and the Urn assume random draws.
2. **Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*.** Founder-as-interviewer-and-recruiter, with bias controls one person can run (past behaviour, commitments). Cheap and author-sold.
3. **Maurya, *Running Lean* 3e.** The pre-revenue viability Fermi (G2 revenue side) plus the traction roadmap and 90-day cycles (G4). It's the best available substitute for OKRs at this stage.
4. **Jason Cohen's *A Smart Bear* essays (free).** The founder-hour cost line (G2) and write-hypotheses-first interviewing (G3). Keep Tringas ch. 6 beside it as the counter-view.

Runner-up: Walling's *Start Small, Stay Small*, the only book that is solo by definition, if the seats need one book whose unit of analysis matches the client.

## Gaps left uncovered

- **G1, entirely.** No named candidate covers predicting one's own stance across conflicting roles, or review and decision rights when AI agents do the work and the founder is the only human. Graham names the risk; Sivers and Jarvis are adjacent. This lane needs a seat-authored method: for example, a pre-committed decision log with written reversal triggers, and calibration training from Hubbard ch. 5.
- **G3's core.** No named candidate treats bias control without a second person as its subject. There are only solo-runnable techniques (Mom Test question rules, Cohen's written hypotheses, Lean Analytics scoring, Kahl's observational evidence). The displacement question has only Graham's maker's-schedule essay, Traction's 50% rule and Tringas' hour cap.
- **G4 OKRs.** No candidate treats OKRs for an org-of-one. The traction roadmap is the nearest substitute.
- **G2 as one artifact.** No source combines a pre-revenue revenue estimate with founder hours as the main cost line. Maurya (revenue side) and Cohen (hour price) have to be joined by the seat.
