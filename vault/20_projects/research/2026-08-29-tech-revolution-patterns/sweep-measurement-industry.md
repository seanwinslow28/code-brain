## SOURCES

### 1. Jim Ramsburg, "Radio's Rulers: Crossley, Hooper & Nielsen"
**URL:** http://www.jimramsburg.com/radiosrulerscrossleyhooperandnielsen.html — radio historian, abridged from *Network Radio Ratings, 1932–1953*. **Classification: practitioner-historical** (deeply sourced; full page read). The richest single source in the sweep.

1. **Measurement was born from an ad-fraud audit, not audience curiosity.** 1927: advertiser Davis Baking Powder commissioned Crossley to verify its NBC program aired locally. Crossley (1963): "In a number of cities we found local commercials being substituted for the national ones, so the stations were collecting twice." First paying customer was a **buyer verifying delivery**.
2. **Subscription business launched 3 years later, buyer-funded.** "With the endorsement of the Association of National Advertisers, (ANA), and 30 subscribers each paying $65 a month, Crossley's network-wide polling service began on March 1, 1930." (~$23K/yr ≈ $440K in 2026 dollars.)
3. **Continuous demand triggered by the Depression:** consumer-research want "would become a need a few years later when the Great Depression slashed consumer spending and advertising budgets." Scarcity of ad dollars forced proof-of-delivery.
4. **First challenger (Clark-Hooper, 1934) funded by the competing medium's sellers:** "magazine publishers seeking an 'honest count' were the first subscribers" — measurement demand can come from whoever is *losing* the budget-allocation fight.
5. **Industry co-op lost to an independent for-profit.** Networks withdrew support from CAB in 1942 ("40% of its annual $350,000 in revenues"); CAB dissolved Aug 2, 1946. *Time*: "Radiomen decided last summer that the industry-financed CAB was duplicating the independent telephone poll of C. E. Hooper."
6. **Scale of the winner:** "By 1947, Hooper had over 600 subscribers, each paying from $125 to $1300 per month." The payer base migrated from buy-side to both sides.
7. **Nielsen won by acquisition + passive metering on a ~20-year horizon.** Audimeter in radio 1942; "Hooper sold his national radio and television network rating services to Nielsen in March, 1950, for an estimated $650,000." Hooper's counter — "They Have To Be Listening To Be Sold!" — is the ancestor of "the agent hit the page but did the human see anything?"
8. **Seller-funded replacement flopped on cost:** NAB's BMB "poured $1.25 Million into a survey mailed to a million households. The experiment was an expensive flop."

### 2. Inside Story, "Measuring the internet" (review of *Rating the Audience*)
**URL:** https://insidestory.org.au/measuring-the-internet/ — **Classification: scholarly review.**
- Crossley "hired in 1929 by a group of American radio advertisers" — buy-side paid first.
- Crossley established "the core of the modern ratings convention": sample + "single number," making "exposure" the tradable unit.
- Ratings are a **convention** — an agreement between buyer and seller about what counts. The business is selling the agreement.
- "Digital media users are easier to track but harder to follow" — trace abundance doesn't automatically produce a currency.

## (2) RECURRING PRECONDITIONS FOR A SELLABLE MEASUREMENT BUSINESS

1. **Money flows first; measurement is never the market-maker.** Every durable audit business arrived *after* substantial spend was allocated blind. WebTrends-in-1993 shows you can exist pre-money as a tools vendor; the durable paid layer waited for budgets.
2. **A named victim with a dollar loss.** The founding demand event is a fraud or budget fight: substituted commercials (1927), circulation lying (pre-1914), magazines losing budgets to inflated radio numbers (1934). Distrust with a P&L attached.
3. **An adversarial buyer–seller pricing negotiation that needs a common currency.** Payer == measured party (web analytics, Datadog, Flurry) → *tools* business, price-competed toward free. Adversaries (advertiser vs publisher) → *currency* business with pricing power and natural-monopoly tipping.
4. **The seller eventually pays for its own certification** — once enough sellers are audited, refusing the audit signals fraud. Seller-side revenue is the maturity phase, not the entry.
5. **Independence beats industry co-ops.** CAB and BMB lost to independent for-profits — a co-op can't discipline its own funders.
6. **Method must match the medium's trace at low cost**, and the winner converts measurement from *asking* to *metering* (Nielsen).
7. **Free-ification is the standing threat wherever measurement complements a bigger business** (Google/ads, Flurry/ad network, WebSideStory/banners, Cloudflare today). A standalone paid measurer survives only upmarket, or as the neutral third party neither side can be.

## (3) HONEST DISANALOGIES FOR AGENT TRAFFIC

- **No advertiser analogue yet.** No per-unit buyer of "agent attention" exists; the money loops are commerce conversion and content licensing. Until agent access/actions are *priced*, there's no adversarial settlement to audit. The Nielsen precondition is missing; the Datadog one (operators paying to see their own agent traffic) is present now — but that's the segment history says converges on free/bundled.
- **The measured population is ~10 companies, not 40 million households.** Agent operators are few, identifiable, and log everything themselves. The scarce asset isn't data, it's *neutrality* between site owner and agent operator — closer to ABC (certification) than Nielsen (sampling).
- **Infrastructure incumbents already own the exhaust.** Cloudflare sees 20%+ of the web and gives AI-traffic classification away free. In 1930 nobody owned the listening data; in 2026 the pipes do. A startup here is Hooper competing against a world where AT&T gave away free Hooperatings.
- **Timeline compression cuts both ways.** TV got measured in ~1 year because buyers and measurers ported from radio; agent-web measurement is porting from bot management and web analytics just as fast — "first mover" is already crowded at the medium's birth.
- **Verification-of-conduct (did the agent do what it claimed / behave on-site) has its closest ancestor in ABC-style audit and Hooper's "They Have To Be Listening To Be Sold"** — that seat (certifying agent behavior for *both* sides once agent commerce is contested) is the one history says becomes a real business, and only after agent-mediated transactions carry enough dollars that a named victim demands proof.

**Bottom-line base rate:** across seven episodes, a *paid tools* business can start at the medium's birth but converges to free/bundled/acquired; a *currency/audit* business — the durable, pricing-power kind — has never predated (a) large adversarial money flows and (b) a fraud or allocation fight with a named victim, and its first check always came from the side *spending* the money, with the measured side paying later for certification. Agent-web today has the fraud incidents but not the priced adversarial flows — history says build where the money loop will be (agent commerce/licensing settlement), and expect the pure-measurement layer to be given away by incumbents.
