# Productcraft Tier-1 book rights and acquisition audit

Accessed 2026-09-10 in the United States. No book was purchased, no account was used, and no rights holder was contacted during this audit.

## Decision

The original four verified full-text sources remain safe acquisition choices: *The Mom Test*, *User Story Mapping*, *Product Roadmaps Relaunched*, and *Shape Up*. This second-stage audit did not verify another complete, portable, rights-cleared book before purchase.

It did find a viable route for every unresolved title:

- **6 controlled purchase tests:** the public Amazon page cannot reveal whether the publisher enabled Amazon's verified-purchaser EPUB/PDF download. Buy only as a test, then inspect **Manage Your Content and Devices**. A normal Kindle download, Send to Kindle action, app offline mode, ACSM file, or Adobe-authorized EPUB does not pass.
- **5 rights/access inquiries first:** there is a promising publisher, author, institutional, or text-and-data-mining route that should be resolved before spending money.
- **0 dead ends:** every title has either a testable retailer route or a credible permissions route.

This preserves the Productcraft requirement that agents declare whether they are grounded in full text, companion material, or no corpus. Partial sources must never be presented as the complete book.

## Rights-request scope

Each inquiry should ask for both the file and the processing permission. The request should describe:

- one purchaser or a named small team;
- secure private retrieval/indexing for internal decision support;
- no model training or fine-tuning;
- no redistribution, resale, or substitute reading product;
- whether processing stays local or uses a named hosted model/API and its retention policy;
- requested source format, retention period, number of users/agents, and allowed quotation in outputs.

## Why Amazon remains a test rather than a promise

Amazon's [current DRM policy](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GDDXGH9VR22ACM8U) says verified purchasers may download EPUB/PDF only when the publisher has selected DRM-free distribution. Older titles require the publisher to reconfirm that choice. The public product pages inspected for these books did not disclose the title-level setting; the conclusive control appears after purchase in **Manage Your Content and Devices**. A successful file download answers the technical-access question, while the applicable licence still controls storage, sharing, hosted processing, and reuse.

Other platform checks reinforce the distinction:

- [eBooks.com](https://support.ebooks.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360000726656-What-is-DRM-DRM-FAQs) distinguishes DRM-protected books from explicitly DRM-free, watermarked downloads.
- [Google Play Books](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/179863?hl=en-GB) may export a normal EPUB/PDF or an ACSM licence file depending on publisher settings; an ACSM file is not the book.
- [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/shop/wiley-ebooks/) states that its ebooks are read through Wiley Reader or VitalSource and are not provided as downloadable PDF/EPUB files.
- [Cambridge Core's terms](https://www.cambridge.org/core/legal-notices/terms) contain a useful noncommercial TDM permission for lawfully accessed content, with access, sharing, automation, and retention limits.
