# Productcraft book-to-seat — findings brief

**Gate:** L5 — which books earn purchase for the private corpus, and which seat each feeds. Book *content* never lands in tracked files; this brief holds rankings, facts, and citations only.

**Method ($0):** one scoring agent per seat, seven seats including the Insights & Analytics candidate, each scoring every candidate that touches its seat on depth, author standing, free-canon overlap, readability, and seat fit, plus factual columns (edition, pages, audiobook, price, EPUB). Sixty-one distinct titles scored across the seven tables. A separate catalogue verified the free canon per seat. Web-search budgets ran out in every agent's final pass, so a handful of prices and runtimes are marked unverified rather than guessed.

**Standing rule (Sean, 2026-09-09):** the best book for the seats wins even when Sean cannot listen to it. Audiobook availability is a flag on the reading path, never a purchase gate.

## Headline recommendation

**Buy fourteen books now (~$290), ingest one free, hold ten for a second round after the first engagement, and keep three as listen-only for Sean's reading path. *Zero to GenAI Product Leader* belongs to neither studio's corpus. *Creativity Machine* is a contingent buy, Sean's call.** Every seat has at least one anchor book; no seat is empty. Two sub-lanes have no book anywhere and are carried by templates and plugins: release notes, and creative option generation.

## Contingent — Sean's call

**Dikkers, *Creativity Machine* (2026).** Sean named it. It is the only candidate covering creative option generation, which no other book in the pass does. It has a real four-step spine (generate, select, refine, beta-test) with anecdote as illustration; reviewers and the publisher treat it as general ideation, not comedy method. It is three months old with no critical reception to weigh, and it scored 18 for the Strategist and 15 for Discovery. Recommendation: buy it if the Strategist's option generation feels thin after the first engagement, the way Systemcraft held Nudelman. It is cheap and author-narrated, so buying it now costs little if Sean prefers.

## Listen only — Sean's reading path, not the corpus

- **Cagan, *INSPIRED* (2nd ed.), narrated by Cagan, 8h46m.** The reference map of the whole craft, but its method lives in the free SVPG essays and in EMPOWERED, and it anchors no seat above 18. The ideal first listen; the seats do not need its text.
- **Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*, author-narrated, 5h43m.** Its value is the figures, which text ingestion loses.

## Ruling: *Zero to GenAI Product Leader* — neither corpus

Facts from the pass: self-published under the author's own imprint (KDP ISBNs); the one professional review is Kirkus Indie, a paid-submission channel; no Goodreads page surfaced; the circulating 4.9 rating traces to marketing copy with no verifiable count; the audiobook is Amazon "Virtual Voice," AI-synthesized; no public table of contents exists. From the audiobook's chapter manifest: roughly 30% is an AI-systems primer, 42% is career and interview preparation, 10% is business-model archetypes, and 0% is organizational design. Interview prep alone outweighs strategy two to one.

Against the two studios: the AI-systems 30% is a thinner version of what Systemcraft already holds in Huyen and Nika from established publishers; the career 42% is not a studio lane in either studio; the leadership content is the smallest bucket and is AI-adoption framing, not org design. It scored 9 for the Strategist and 12 for Leadership, the lowest in both tables. It does not enter Productcraft's corpus or Systemcraft's. If Sean wants it as personal job-hunt reading, that lives in the private job-hunt lane, not a studio.

## Format rule and an honest ingestion caveat

Systemcraft's precedent buys DRM-free EPUB, PDF fallback, and ingests with book-to-skill. That worked because all five Systemcraft books were O'Reilly. **This list is mostly trade press.** Crown, Vintage, and Macmillan titles (Rumelt, Grove, Ellis & Brown, Bryar & Carr) are normally sold with DRM on every retailer; Wiley, O'Reilly, Cambridge, and self-published titles are usually DRM-free or watermark-only. book-to-skill cannot read a DRM-locked file, and this brief does not recommend stripping DRM.

What that means for the corpus pipeline: at purchase time, check each title on ebooks.com for a DRM-free edition first. Where none exists, the seat's lane manifest carries the free-canon distillate plus a "read by Sean, not ingested" flag, and the ledger's "from the canon" line can still name the framework because the seat's model knows it. This is a purchase-time task for the corpus-pipeline ticket, not a reason to drop a book.
