# Whitespace / gap-map presentation — research synthesis

**Question:** How do strong opportunity-landscape, competitive-whitespace, and gap-analysis reports present gaps so they DRIVE ACTION rather than just listing absences? Scope: the presentation/actionability of a *hero* gap-map section — field structure, the linguistic/structural move that separates an actionable gap from a passive "we don't know X" bullet, next-best-action framing, and the failure modes (false precision, absence-of-evidence vs evidence-of-absence, action paralysis).

**Bottom line:** A bare absence list is the anti-pattern. Every strong gap presentation does the same three things to each gap: (1) **states it as a need/pain, not a solution**, (2) **layers on "why it matters" + a recommended *direction*** (not a mandated build), and (3) **marks its evidence status** so an *unstudied* gap is never read as a confirmed opportunity. The single most load-bearing guardrail for *our* tool: the whitespace map is by construction **absence-of-evidence** (what the panel/evidence *missed*) — so each gap's recommended action must be **"go investigate / backfill,"** never "build," and the section must never assert a gap *is* an opportunity. That maps cleanly onto the discovery tool's existing honesty discipline (the gate) and onto the Stage-5 agent-backfill (the backfill *is* the recommended next action for every gap).

## Findings (verified — vote shown; "salvaged" = verifier completed, hand-confirmed)

1. **Frame each item as an unmet need/pain, anchored upward to a measurable outcome — never a solution** [high, 3-0]. Teresa Torres's Opportunity Solution Tree: four ordered layers (outcome → opportunity → solution → experiment); "98% of people write opportunities as solutions" and solution-framing is *the* dominant failure mode of gap mapping. Design implication: a gap statement is phrased as a customer need/pain, and the map is honest that it's a *gap in our knowledge/coverage*, not a feature to build. Sources: producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees, chameleon.io/blog/opportunity-solution-tree, miro.com/blog/mapping-product-teams-teresa-torres.

2. **The actionability test = does it connect upward to a target? Unconnected items are explicit "distractions" to exclude** [high, 3-0/2-0]. Torres: "If they don't connect to the tree, they should be considered a distraction." Operational diagnostics: the *language test* ("would a real customer naturally say this?") and "is there more than one way to address this?" (if only one, it's a solution in disguise). Design implication: a gap that doesn't tie to the run's topic/outcome shouldn't lead — and a capped, ranked set beats an exhaustive dump.

3. **finding + WHY + recommended *direction* is what converts a passive bullet into an actionable insight — and the direction is NOT a prescriptive single solution** [high, 3-0]. NN/G: "With findings alone, researchers are not able to determine why a pattern was observed or to make recommendations…"; but also, explicitly, "Insights are not meant to be prescriptive; rather, they narrow design possibilities, which can then be tested." Design implication: each gap row = **gap statement → why it matters → a recommended next *move*** (the cheapest next step), pitched at the "narrow the next step" altitude, not "here's the answer." Source: nngroup.com/articles/data-findings-insights-differences.

4. **Absence-of-evidence ≠ evidence-of-absence → encode ≥3 evidence states; "unstudied" routes to *investigate*, never *build/discard*** [high, 3-0, peer-reviewed]. PMC10065758 (Altman & Bland lineage): the fallacy is "a hypothesis assumed true or false before being scientifically and satisfactorily investigated"; "lacking evidence does not imply any benefit conclusions… or even the existence of a benefit itself." It distinguishes *extensively-tested-but-empty* (real evidence of absence) from *insufficiently-investigated* (mere absence of evidence). Design implication — **the central guardrail for our map**: a blind spot is *unstudied by this run*, so its honest status is "we didn't see X," and its action is "investigate X," not "X is whitespace, go build." The report copy must never assert a gap is a confirmed opportunity purely because the panel didn't surface it. Sources: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10065758, PMC351831 (BMJ).

5. **Prioritize at the opportunity layer (not solutions — they're incommensurable "apples to oranges"); rank gaps, give each one cheapest next action; cap the list** [high, 2-0 + unverified-corroborated]. Torres: "I don't recommend prioritizing solutions because we end up comparing a lot of apples to oranges. Instead, prioritize opportunities." Competitive-intel conventions (Infomineo) independently prescribe **lead with recommendations**, each with a specific next step, and **cap at 3–5 prioritized actions** — directly applicable to a section that *leads* the report. Counters the action-paralysis failure mode (data overload hides the real gaps; "80/20 — map only the most promising"). Sources: producttalk.org/prioritize-opportunities, infomineo.com (CI reports), kayako.com/blog/white-space-analysis.

6. **Pair each gap with a directed next step — the structural move CI uses to turn an absence into a play** [medium, unverified-but-cited, multi-source]. Battlecard practice: every gap/weakness row carries a *paired* mitigation/action — "develop mitigation strategies and action plans to address weaknesses," not a passive observation. Gap-analysis templates (ClearPoint, SkillPanel, Hyperproof) converge on action + owner + priority columns and the test "does the named owner actually *control the inputs*?" — i.e. a recommended action is only real if its actor can actually do it. For us, the "actor who controls the inputs" is **the orchestrating agent with WebSearch/WebFetch** — which is exactly why "backfill this gap" is a *real* next action and not a void. Sources: beyondthebacklog.com/competitor-battlecards, clearpointstrategy.com/blog/gap-analysis-template, skillpanel.com/blog/gap-analysis-template, hyperproof.io (gap assessment).

7. **False precision manufactures false trust — hedge low-evidence gaps, don't over-quantify** [medium, unverified-but-cited]. OrgVitality: false precision is "presentation of data… with a specificity or exactness that exceeds the actual reliability of the underlying evidence"; precise figures read as "more trustworthy… regardless of the underlying uncertainty." Antidote: "transparency, humility, and rigor," not removing numbers. Design implication: don't attach a fake confidence score to a blind spot (it has, by definition, *no* supporting evidence). Keep the map qualitative + honestly labeled "what was missed." Source: orgvitality.com/blog/false-precision.

## Refuted (excluded from the design)
- ❌ **"An actionable insight has exactly three required fields: named subject + recommended next step + closing time horizon ('this week')"** [refuted 3×, high]. Misrepresents its own source (Sopact's live triad is named-subject + open-decision-window + sufficient-context); Conifer explicitly rejects a mandated near-term horizon ("can be 2, 5, or 10 years out"); the field set is contested (4- and 6-field variants exist). → Do **not** bake owner/timeline/named-subject fields into the gap row as required. For our tool the "owner" is always the agent and the "timeline" is "next run," so these add ceremony, not signal.
- ❌ **"The design recommendation is a constituent of the insight"** [refuted, high]. NN/G treats the recommendation as a *separate downstream artifact*; the insight "narrows design possibilities." → Keep the gap's action at "narrow the next step" altitude (reframe / segment / raise tier / backfill), not "here's what to build."

## How this changed / shaped the D4 build
- **Promote, then make each gap a row, not a bullet.** Lead the ledger with the whitespace map. Each gap renders as **gap statement → why-it-matters (implicit: "the panel/evidence didn't cover this") → recommended next action** (findings 1,3,6). No bare `- {b}` bullets.
- **The recommended action is always "convert this absence into evidence," never "build."** Deterministic, honest hints: *backfill this gap (agent WebSearch/WebFetch)* · *reframe the topic* · *add `--segment <x>`* · *raise the tier*. This is the absence-of-evidence guardrail (finding 4) made operational, and it's the cheapest-next-step altitude NN/G + Torres prescribe (findings 3,5).
- **A short, ordered "sharpen the next run" action list leads the section** — cap it, don't dump every gap (finding 5). Rank by which gap most blocks the run's intent.
- **No fabricated confidence/precision on gaps** (finding 7). The map stays qualitative and labeled as *what was missed*, never as verified claims or confirmed opportunities — consistent with the §6 gate.
- **D4 + Stage-5 backfill read as one hero block:** the whitespace map states the gap and its action; the Web Supplement shows what the agent found filling it. The "actor who controls the inputs" (finding 6) is the agent, so "backfill" is a real action, not a void.
