# Nicolas Cole curriculum deconstruction

## Takeaway

- **Most of the method is public.** Cole's own blog posts, newsletters, X posts and two low-cost books describe nearly every named framework well enough to practice it without a course: Atomic Essays, 1/3/1, the 4A / Endless Idea Generator, the 2-Year Test, headline formula, niche formula, the 5 client-voice archetypes, approved language, and commodity / personality / original content.
- **The "digital brain" isn't a course. It's software and an idea.** In 2026 it shows up as (1) a free concept essay, (2) Ghostbase / Typeshare, a paid tool that builds a per-user "Digital Brain" from your uploads, and (3) AI Cole, a $50/month chatbot of *Cole's* brain, not yours. In the interview, Cole says it doesn't matter where the library lives, only that AI can reach it. That makes it a do-it-yourself job.
- **What paying buys is mostly people and a deadline:** a live cohort clock, hot-seat feedback from Cole, peer comments, co-working, and a refund-backed commitment. Ship 30 now costs $99/month inside AI Writing Skool. For a writer who already publishes and wants a voice library more than fast growth, that's the only real case for paying, and it's a case about accountability, not information.
- **Critiques are thin and mostly anecdotal.** I found no substantive published critique of a "Ship 30 voice." What exists is scattered: short-form fatigue, a business-topic monoculture in the cohort, and imitation producing generic writers.

**Interview note.** The episode is *$30M Writer: Never write AI slop again* (Greg Isenberg, uploaded 2026-09-21, 1:09:34 long). The ~26-minute transcript used here covers only the first part of it. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YuOSyRj3sXg) (B, since Cole is speaking)

## 2. Named frameworks and exercises (verification status)

| Framework | What it is, paraphrased | Verified? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atomic Essay** | A single-idea essay of 250–300 words, published as a screenshot. Seven steps: pick a topic by answering a question, pick an audience (general / niche / industry), write the headline, outline key points, expand each with a story, research or context, format it, then publish and track results | Yes | [ship30for30.com guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-an-atomic-essay-a-beginners-guide) (B) |
| **1/3/1** | One declarative sentence, three sentences that expand on it, one declarative sentence to close. A pacing device, with variants like 1/4/1 | Yes | [Write With AI, Oct 2024](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/the-131-writing-technique) (B) |
| **4A framework** | Aspirational (it's possible), Actionable (the steps), Analytical (the numbers), Anthropological (the reason: fears, failures, human nature) | Yes | [Cole on X](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1641191079650410496) (B) |
| **Endless Idea Generator** | A grid: the 4 A's crossed with sub-types (how-to, tips, stats, trends, fears, failures, lessons, mistakes). The claim is 1–2 ideas become 12+ pieces in about 10 minutes | Yes | [ship30 frameworks post](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/online-writing-frameworks) (B) |
| **2-Year Test** | Anything you've solved in the last two years makes you an expert to your past self, so write as that person's mentor. Uses the obvious-to-you principle | Yes | [ship30 post](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-2-year-test-a-framework-for-endless-content-ideas) (B) |

## 3. Books

- **The Art and Business of Online Writing** (2020). Part 1 covers writing consistently: platform choice, form and content type, the what/who/promise headline, and a five-line intro. Part 2 finds a niche from engagement data. Part 3 covers the business side: pillar pieces and monetization. [Shortform overview](https://www.shortform.com/blog/nicolas-cole-the-art-and-business-of-online-writing/) (C); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/55189231-the-art-and-business-of-online-writing) (D). This is the core free-plus-cheap text.
- **The Art & Business of Ghostwriting** (2023, Different Publishing). Of everything here, it maps most closely onto a voice library. It includes voice-as-attributes ("voices aren't found, they're created"), the approved-language audit, the 5 archetypes, the 3 phases, and managing feedback. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Art-Business-Ghostwriting-Writing-People-ebook/dp/B0FW4PWRSH) (B)
- **Snow Leopard** (Category Pirates: Cole, Lochhead, Yoon). Writing advice seen through category design. Topics: become a "category of one," Obvious vs Non-Obvious content, the power of a point of view, and "languaging," meaning coining terms that stick. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Snow-Leopard-Legendary-Writers-Category/dp/1956934456) (B); [review](https://malikalimoekhamedov.substack.com/p/book-review-snow-leopard) (D)
- **The 22 Laws of Digital Writing** (with Bush; ebook). Sample laws: "Don't Start A Blog," "Skimmability is Readability." [ship30](https://www.ship30for30.com/the-22-laws-of-digital-writing) (B). Price not verified.

## 4. The split

**(a) Fully reproducible for free from public material:** Atomic Essays, 1/3/1, 4A, Endless Idea Generator, Content Buckets (3 subjects × general / niche / industry audience, same post), 2-Year Test, headline formula, Golden Intersection, "create your niche from data," commodity / personality / original, the five Digital Brain categories, voice dials and EDAN, the signature-line practice, and the value-horizon question. All of these come from the B sources above.

**(b) Paid or cheap-paid, but described clearly enough to reproduce:**
- The ghostwriting book's approved-language audit, 5 archetypes, 3 phases and niche formula. This costs the price of a book, not a course.
- The Ship 30 session topics ("Leveraging Your Library," "Comments-As-Content," "Data-Driven Niche Selection") are listed publicly with one-line descriptions. The session contents aren't public. I'm inferring them from the free material, and they may hold details I can't verify.
- The Digital Brain *tool*. Its mechanism is public: upload your corpus, retrieve, draft. Cole himself says the library can live anywhere, so a markdown folder plus any model is a working substitute.
- PGA's 4 Ps are named publicly, but the module detail isn't. Only relevant if you want to ghostwrite.

**(c) What you really only get by paying:**
- **A cohort clock.** Fixed dates, daily assignments, and hundreds of people shipping alongside you. The refund is tied to finishing, which is a real commitment device. [ship30](https://www.ship30for30.com/)
- **Live, personal feedback.** Monday Hot Seat with Cole (Skool) and hot seats in PGA clinics. This is scarce and can't be copied. [Skool](https://www.skool.com/ai-writing-skool/about); [PGA](http://learn.premiumghostwritingacademy.com/pga-course-community)
- **Peer reading.** Readers who comment early, before you have an audience.
- **Tools.** AI Cole answers from *his* archive, which is already free. Whether Ghostbase's G1 beats a general model plus your own library is unverified.

Caveat: a 2025 participant found the cohort skewed toward business topics ([measiwitch](https://measiwitch.substack.com/p/three-reasons-im-struggling-with), D), so peer feedback on non-business writing may be thinner than the headcount suggests.

## 5. Critiques

- **Evidence is thin.** Web, Hacker News and review-site searches found no substantive critique of a homogeneous "Ship 30 voice"; most reviews are affiliate. Plausible, unproven.
- **Short-form fatigue.** One paying participant says he "kind of loathes" the premise. His first reason is a dislike of short-form content; the rest is paywalled. [Pacio, Sep 2024](https://medium.com/@pacio49/top-four-reasons-why-i-kind-of-loathe-ship-30-for-30-0137a6aa9b27) (D)
- **Imitation.** A writer spent three years trying to *become* Cole or Bush and says it left him with nothing distinct. [Medium, 2023](https://medium.com/@fullstackwriter/youre-not-nicolas-cole-you-re-not-dickie-bush-f74ff28999b0) (D)
- **Depth ceiling.** Even an affiliate reviewer calls it a 0-to-1 program, not good-to-great, with a short format that limits depth. [grahammann.net](https://grahammann.net/blog/ship-30-for-30-review) (D, affiliate)
- **Mixed messages and recycled content.** One reviewer says *Snow Leopard* argues quality over quantity, which contradicts the volume advice elsewhere, and repackages free newsletter material. [review](https://malikalimoekhamedov.substack.com/p/book-review-snow-leopard) (D)
- **My inference, not sourced:** shared headline templates, 1/3/1 openers and the 4A grid produce structural sameness, even when the content differs. Cole's 2026 commodity / personality split reads partly as a fix for this: the formats are commodity, and the stories and stances are what differentiate.

## 6. Draft self-directed curriculum (8 weeks, built only from a and b)

**Goal:** a voice library of stories, stances, signature lines and approved language that an AI can recombine from. Audience growth is a side effect, not the goal. Budget: one book, about 30 minutes a day, one publish per weekday.

| Week | Build | Practice (publish) | Framework used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Audit | Gather everything you've published or said: posts, talks, transcripts. Run the ghostwriter's Step 1 audit on *yourself*, and mark sentences you'd happily reuse as `approved.md` | 5 Atomic Essays, each one idea | Approved language, Atomic Essay |
| 2. Life facts and the 2-Year Test | `facts.md`: 30 life facts (origin, jobs, odd skills, formative projects). List 20 problems solved in the last 2 years | 5 pieces, each answering a question with a fact (Golden Intersection) | Digital Brain categories, 2-Year Test |
| 3. Story bank | `stories/`: 15 stories, each told as setup, turn, lesson. Tag each with the 4A it fits. Transcribe yourself telling 3 of them aloud, then edit down | 5 story-led pieces | 3 Phases (self-applied), 4A |
| 4. Stance ledger | `stances.md`: for 20 commodity debates in your field, write which side you're on, why, and the story that earned the stance. This is Cole's anti-slop core | 5 opinion pieces (Opinionator archetype) | Commodity vs Personality |
| 5. Signature lines | Write 5–10 candidate signature lines (a credential, an origin, a thesis). Reuse each one in at least 3 pieces this week and watch which gets repeated back to you | 5 pieces that reuse the lines deliberately | Signature line, name & claim |
| 6. Voice dials | Pull 10 sentences of yours and 10 of a writer you admire. Log word choice, sentence length, structure and EDAN mix. Write `voice.md` with explicit settings. Try all 5 archetypes on one idea | 5 pieces, varying one dial each | Voice dials, EDAN, 5 archetypes |
| 7. Recombination test | Point any model at the library folder. Ask it to draft 5 pieces using *only* approved language, stances and stories. Grade each: did it pick the right side, reuse real lines, invent nothing? Log what failed and patch the library | Publish the best 2 after editing; 3 human-written | Personal language model (DIY) |
| 8. Horizon and niche | Tag every library entry short, medium or long term. Read 7 weeks of engagement data. Draft a 1 problem / 1 person / 1 way statement and 3 content buckets | 1 long pillar piece built mostly from long-horizon entries | Value horizon, data-driven niche, Content Buckets |

**What this replaces, and how:** the cohort clock becomes a public commitment (for example, announce the 8 weeks). Peer review becomes two or three writers swapping drafts weekly. Hot-seat feedback has no substitute. If week 4 or 5 stalls, one month of AI Writing Skool at $99 during a live cohort is the cheapest way to buy it.
