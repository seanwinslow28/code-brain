# Story Framework Library

26 narrative frameworks organized by purpose: Transformation, Strategic, Persuasive, Analytical, Emotional. Each entry: what the framework does, when to choose it, and the diagnostic questions that drive its structure.

Read this file when selecting a framework. Match the framework to the *purpose* of the narrative — not the topic.

---

## Transformation — Stories about change

### Hero's Journey
**What it does:** Classic transformation arc — protagonist crosses thresholds, faces trials, returns transformed.
**Best when:** Customer success story, founder narrative, change-management story. The audience needs to *feel* the arc.
**Driving questions:**
- Who is the hero?
- What's their ordinary world?
- What call disrupts it?
- What trials do they face?
- How are they transformed?

### Pixar Story Spine
**What it does:** Compact emotional arc using "Once upon a time / Every day / Until one day / Because of that / Until finally" framework that builds tension to resolution.
**Best when:** Short-form storytelling — pitch decks, opening narratives, social posts, anywhere brevity matters.
**Driving questions:**
- Once upon a time, what?
- Every day, what happened?
- Until one day, what changed?
- Because of that, what?
- Until finally, how was it resolved?

### Customer Journey
**What it does:** Narrative following customer transformation from pain point through solution to success.
**Best when:** Case study, testimonial, marketing site narrative. The customer is the hero; the product is the helper.
**Driving questions:**
- What was the *before* struggle?
- What discovery moment occurred?
- How did they implement?
- What transformation happened?
- What's their new reality?

### Challenge Overcome
**What it does:** Dramatic structure centered on confronting and conquering a significant obstacle.
**Best when:** Recovery stories, comeback narratives, difficult-decision stories. Stakes need to feel real.
**Driving questions:**
- What obstacle blocked progress?
- How did stakes escalate?
- What was the darkest moment?
- What breakthrough occurred?
- What was learned?

### Character Arc
**What it does:** Personal evolution story showing growth through experience and struggle.
**Best when:** Profile pieces, employee spotlights, founder stories where the *person* is the subject.
**Driving questions:**
- Who are they at the start?
- What forces change?
- What do they resist?
- What breakthrough shifts them?
- Who have they become?

## Strategic — Stories about identity, position, and direction

### Brand Story
**What it does:** Authentic narrative communicating brand values, mission, and unique market position.
**Best when:** Marketing site, brand book, "About us" page, brand-launch storytelling.
**Driving questions:**
- What sparked this brand?
- What core values drive it?
- How does it impact customers?
- What makes it different?
- Where is it heading?

### Vision Narrative
**What it does:** Future-focused story painting a vivid picture of a desired state and the path to reach it.
**Best when:** All-hands keynote, board presentation, strategic pivot announcement, fundraising vision.
**Driving questions:**
- What's the current reality?
- What opportunity emerges?
- What's the bold vision?
- What's the strategic path?
- What does the transformed future look like?

### Origin Story
**What it does:** Foundational narrative explaining how something came to be and why it matters today.
**Best when:** Company history, product origin, founder retelling. Connects the present to a meaningful beginning.
**Driving questions:**
- What was the spark moment?
- What early struggles occurred?
- What key breakthrough happened?
- How did it evolve?
- What's the current mission?

### Positioning Story
**What it does:** Narrative establishing unique market position and competitive differentiation.
**Best when:** Sales narrative, competitive deck, category-creation pitch.
**Driving questions:**
- What market gap exists?
- How are you uniquely qualified?
- What makes your approach different?
- Why should the audience care?
- What future do you enable?

### Culture Story
**What it does:** Internal narrative defining organizational values, behaviors, and identity.
**Best when:** Employee onboarding, culture deck, leadership communication during change.
**Driving questions:**
- What principles guide decisions?
- What behaviors exemplify the culture?
- What stories illustrate the values?
- How do people experience it?
- What culture are you building?

## Persuasive — Stories that move people to act

### Pitch Narrative
**What it does:** Compelling story structure designed to inspire investment, action, or partnership.
**Best when:** Investor pitch, partnership pitch, internal capital-allocation pitch.
**Driving questions:**
- What problem landscape exists?
- What's your vision for the solution?
- What proof validates the approach?
- What's the opportunity size?
- What action do you want?

### Sales Story
**What it does:** Customer-centric narrative demonstrating value and building desire for the solution.
**Best when:** B2B sales motion, demo narrative, proposal storytelling.
**Driving questions:**
- What pain do they feel?
- How do you understand it?
- What solution transforms the situation?
- What results can they expect?
- What's the path forward?

### Change Story
**What it does:** Narrative making the case for transformation and mobilizing people through transition.
**Best when:** Reorg announcement, strategy shift, behavior-change campaign.
**Driving questions:**
- Why can't we stay here?
- What does *better* look like?
- What's at stake if we don't change?
- How do we get there?
- What's in it for them?

### Fundraising Story
**What it does:** Emotionally compelling narrative connecting donor values to mission impact.
**Best when:** Nonprofit appeal, philanthropic pitch, mission-driven capital ask.
**Driving questions:**
- What problem breaks hearts?
- What solution creates hope?
- What impact will the investment make?
- Why is it urgent?
- How can they help?

### Advocacy Story
**What it does:** Story galvanizing support for a cause, movement, or policy change.
**Best when:** Campaign messaging, public advocacy, community-organizing narrative.
**Driving questions:**
- What injustice demands attention?
- Who is affected and how?
- What change is needed?
- What happens if we act?
- How can they join?

## Analytical — Stories about insight

### Data Storytelling
**What it does:** Transform data insights into a compelling narrative with clear, actionable takeaways.
**Best when:** Analytics report, research summary, executive dashboard narrative.
**Driving questions:**
- What context is needed?
- What data reveals the insight?
- What patterns explain it?
- *So what* — why does it matter?
- What actions should follow?

### Case Study
**What it does:** Detailed narrative documenting real-world application, results, and learnings.
**Best when:** Marketing case study, internal post-mortem, proof-of-concept writeup.
**Driving questions:**
- What was the situation?
- What approach was taken?
- What challenges emerged?
- What results were achieved?
- What lessons transfer?

### Research Narrative
**What it does:** Story structure presenting research findings in an accessible, engaging way.
**Best when:** Academic findings going public, research summary for non-researchers, white paper.
**Driving questions:**
- What question drove the research?
- How was it investigated?
- What did you discover?
- What does it mean?
- What are the implications?

### Insight Narrative
**What it does:** Narrative revealing a non-obvious truth or pattern that shifts understanding.
**Best when:** Thought-leadership essay, "what we learned" post, contrarian POV piece.
**Driving questions:**
- What did everyone assume?
- What did *you* notice?
- What deeper pattern emerged?
- Why does it matter?
- What should change?

### Process Story
**What it does:** Behind-the-scenes narrative showing how something was made or accomplished.
**Best when:** Build-in-public posts, lab-notebook-style content, "how we shipped X" posts.
**Driving questions:**
- What was being created?
- What approach was chosen?
- What challenges arose?
- How were they solved?
- What was learned?

## Emotional — Stories that connect

### Hook-Driven
**What it does:** Story structure maximizing emotional engagement through a powerful opening and well-placed touchpoints.
**Best when:** Newsletter open, blog hero post, anywhere the first sentence has to earn the rest.
**Driving questions:**
- What surprising fact opens it?
- What urgent question emerges?
- Where are the emotional peaks?
- What creates relatability?
- What payoff satisfies?

### Conflict Resolution
**What it does:** Narrative centered on tension building and satisfying resolution of a core conflict.
**Best when:** Drama-shaped narrative — short fiction, anecdote-heavy posts, vignettes.
**Driving questions:**
- What's the central conflict?
- Who wants what, and why?
- What prevents resolution?
- How does tension escalate?
- How is it resolved?

### Empathy Story
**What it does:** Story designed to create emotional connection and understanding of other perspectives.
**Best when:** Bridging across audiences, DEI narrative, perspective-shift content.
**Driving questions:**
- Whose perspective are we taking?
- What do they experience?
- What do they feel?
- Why should the audience care?
- What common ground exists?

### Human Interest
**What it does:** Personal story highlighting universal human experiences and emotions.
**Best when:** Magazine-style writing, profile features, broad-appeal content.
**Driving questions:**
- Who is at the center?
- What personal stakes exist?
- What universal themes emerge?
- What emotional journey occurs?
- What makes it relatable?

### Vulnerable Story
**What it does:** Authentic personal narrative sharing struggle, failure, or raw truth to build connection.
**Best when:** Founder/leader storytelling that needs trust and humanity. Use sparingly; vulnerability cheapens with overuse.
**Driving questions:**
- What truth is hard to share?
- What struggle was faced?
- What was learned?
- Why share this *now*?
- What hope does it offer?

### StoryBrand (BAB / 7-part)
**What it does:** Customer-as-hero framework where the brand is the *guide*, not the hero.
**Best when:** Marketing copy, sales narrative, anywhere the customer must be centered.
**Structure:** A character / has a problem / meets a guide / who gives them a plan / and calls them to action / that ends in success / avoiding failure.
**Driving questions:**
- Who is the customer?
- What problem do they face?
- How are you the guide (not the hero)?
- What's the plan?
- What's the call to action?
- What's the success they'll experience?
- What failure are they avoiding?

## Frameworks at a Glance

| Purpose | Quick frameworks | Long-form frameworks |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation | Pixar Spine, Before-After-Bridge | Hero's Journey, Customer Journey |
| Strategic | Origin Story, Positioning Story | Brand Story, Vision Narrative |
| Persuasive | Pitch Narrative, Sales Story | Change Story, Fundraising Story |
| Analytical | Insight Narrative, Process Story | Research Narrative, Case Study |
| Emotional | Hook-Driven, Empathy Story | Vulnerable Story, Human Interest |

When unsure, default to **Pixar Spine** for short-form and **Hero's Journey** or **StoryBrand** for long-form. They're the most universally applicable.
