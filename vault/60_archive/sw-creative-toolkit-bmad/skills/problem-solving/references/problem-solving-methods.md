# Problem-Solving Method Library

Curated methods organized by the six phases of the systematic problem-solving workflow: Diagnosis, Analysis, Synthesis (solution generation), Evaluation, Implementation, and Creative (lateral). Each entry: what the method does, when it fits, and facilitation prompts.

Read this file when selecting methods for a phase. Match the method to the problem type, not the other way around.

---

## Diagnosis — Understand the problem before you try to fix it

### Five Whys Root Cause
**What it does:** Drill down through layers of symptoms by asking *why* five times (or until you hit something irreducible).
**Best when:** Linear cause chain. One thing broke, you suspect a single root cause beneath the surface.
**Watch out for:** Questions like "why is the team underperforming?" can produce blamey answers — keep the question on systems, not people.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Why did this happen?"
- "Why is that the case?"
- "Why does that occur?"
- "What's beneath that?"
- "What's the root cause?"

### Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa)
**What it does:** Map all potential causes across categories — People, Process, Materials, Equipment, Environment — to systematically explore the cause space.
**Best when:** Multi-factor problem with no obvious single cause. Quality issues, recurring failures, "everything seems off."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What people factors contribute?"
- "What process issues?"
- "What material problems?"
- "What equipment factors?"
- "What environmental conditions?"

### Problem Statement Refinement
**What it does:** Transform vague complaints into precise, actionable problem statements that focus solution effort.
**Best when:** The problem keeps shifting shape in conversation. "It's slow." "It's broken." Refinement is the gate to everything else.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What *exactly* is wrong?"
- "Who is affected and how?"
- "When and where does it occur?"
- "What's the gap between current and desired?"
- "What makes this a problem worth solving?"

### Is/Is Not Analysis
**What it does:** Define problem boundaries by contrasting where the problem exists vs. where it doesn't, narrowing investigation.
**Best when:** Problem is intermittent or context-specific. "It only happens on Tuesdays." "It only affects power users."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Where does the problem occur? Where doesn't it?"
- "When does it happen? When doesn't it?"
- "Who experiences it? Who doesn't?"
- "What pattern emerges from those boundaries?"

### Systems Thinking
**What it does:** Map interconnected system elements, feedback loops, and leverage points to understand complex problem dynamics.
**Best when:** Problem keeps recurring despite point fixes. Symptoms in one place, causes in another. "We fix it and it comes back."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are the system components?"
- "What relationships exist?"
- "What feedback loops?"
- "What delays occur between cause and effect?"
- "Where are the leverage points?"

## Analysis — Understand forces, constraints, and dependencies

### Force Field Analysis
**What it does:** Identify driving forces (pushing toward solution) and restraining forces (blocking progress) to plan interventions.
**Best when:** Organizational or change-management problems. The technical fix is known; the *adoption* is the problem.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What forces drive toward solution? (motivation, resources, support)"
- "What forces resist change? (inertia, cost, complexity, politics)"
- "Which are strongest?"
- "Which can we influence?"
- "What's the strategy?"

### Pareto Analysis (80/20)
**What it does:** Apply the 80/20 rule to identify the vital few causes creating the majority of impact — the ones worth solving first.
**Best when:** Many causes exist, finite time. You can't fix everything; you need to know which 20% gives 80% return.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What causes exist?"
- "What's the frequency or impact of each?"
- "What's the cumulative impact?"
- "What vital few drive 80%?"
- "Where do we focus?"

### Gap Analysis
**What it does:** Compare current state to desired state across multiple dimensions to identify specific improvement needs.
**Best when:** You can clearly describe both the current and desired state but the path between them is unclear.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's the current state?"
- "What's the desired state?"
- "What gaps exist?"
- "How big are the gaps?"
- "What causes them?"
- "Priority focus?"

### Constraint Identification (Theory of Constraints)
**What it does:** Find the bottleneck limiting system performance. Optimizing anything else is wasted effort.
**Best when:** Throughput / flow / queueing problems. Pipeline issues. "Everyone's busy and nothing's shipping."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's the constraint?"
- "What limits throughput?"
- "What should we optimize?"
- "What happens if we elevate the constraint?"
- "What's the next constraint behind it?"

### Failure Mode Analysis (FMEA)
**What it does:** Anticipate how solutions could fail and engineer preventions before problems occur.
**Best when:** High-stakes implementation, regulated environments, anything where post-deployment failure is expensive.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What could go wrong?"
- "What's the likelihood?"
- "What's the impact?"
- "How do we prevent it?"
- "How do we detect early?"
- "What's the mitigation?"

## Synthesis — Generate solution options

### TRIZ Contradiction Matrix
**What it does:** Resolve technical contradictions ("if I improve X, Y gets worse") using 40 inventive principles distilled from millions of patents.
**Best when:** Engineering/technical problems with apparent trade-offs. "Make it stronger AND lighter." "Make it faster AND cheaper."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What improves?"
- "What worsens?"
- "What's the contradiction?"
- "What inventive principles apply?"
- "How do we resolve without compromise?"

### Lateral Thinking Techniques
**What it does:** Use provocative operations and random entry to break pattern-thinking and access novel solutions.
**Best when:** Stuck in local maxima. Same answers keep coming up. Need a forced re-frame.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Make a provocation."
- "Challenge an assumption."
- "Use a random stimulus."
- "Escape the dominant idea."
- "Generate alternatives."

### Morphological Analysis
**What it does:** Systematically explore all combinations of solution parameters to find non-obvious optimal configurations.
**Best when:** Solution space is combinatorial — multiple parameters, multiple options each. "We could ship in 5 form factors × 3 price tiers × 4 channels."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are the key parameters?"
- "What options exist for each?"
- "Try different combinations."
- "What patterns emerge?"
- "What's optimal?"

### Biomimicry Problem Solving
**What it does:** Learn from nature's 3.8 billion years of R&D — find biological analogues to engineering problems.
**Best when:** Optimization problems where nature has already solved a similar one (energy efficiency, structural integrity, swarm coordination).
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "How does nature solve this?"
- "What biological analogy fits?"
- "What principles transfer?"
- "How do we adapt?"

### Synectics Method
**What it does:** Make the strange familiar and the familiar strange through analogies — spark creative problem-solving breakthroughs.
**Best when:** Stuck in familiar framing. Need to defamiliarize the problem to see it fresh.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's this like?"
- "How are they similar?"
- "What metaphor fits?"
- "What does that suggest?"
- "What insight emerges?"

## Evaluation — Choose the best option rigorously

### Decision Matrix
**What it does:** Systematically evaluate solution options against weighted criteria for objective selection.
**Best when:** Multiple viable options, multiple criteria of varying importance. "We have 4 candidates and 6 things we care about."
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are the options?"
- "What criteria matter?"
- "What weights?"
- "Rate each option."
- "Calculate scores."
- "What wins?"

### Cost-Benefit Analysis
**What it does:** Quantify expected costs and benefits of solution options to support rational investment decisions.
**Best when:** Financial impact is the dominant criterion. Build vs. buy. Big capital allocations.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are the costs?"
- "What are the benefits?"
- "Quantify each."
- "What's the payback period?"
- "What's the ROI?"
- "What's recommended?"

### Risk Assessment Matrix
**What it does:** Evaluate solution risks across likelihood × impact dimensions to prioritize mitigation efforts.
**Best when:** Risk is the primary concern. Regulated industries, customer-facing changes, irreversible decisions.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What could go wrong?"
- "What's the probability?"
- "What's the impact?"
- "Plot on the matrix."
- "What's the risk score?"
- "What's the mitigation plan?"

### Pilot Testing Protocol
**What it does:** Design small-scale experiments to validate solutions before full implementation commitment.
**Best when:** Reversibility matters. You want to fail cheap before scaling. Multiple plausible solutions, unclear which works.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What will we test?"
- "What's success criteria?"
- "What's the test plan?"
- "What data to collect?"
- "What did we learn?"
- "Scale or pivot?"

### Feasibility Study
**What it does:** Assess technical, operational, financial, and schedule feasibility of solution options.
**Best when:** Decision involves new capability or significant resource commitment. "Can we actually do this?"
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Is it technically possible?"
- "Operationally viable?"
- "Financially sound?"
- "Schedule realistic?"
- "Overall feasibility?"

## Implementation — Execute with discipline and feedback loops

### PDCA Cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act)
**What it does:** Iterate Plan → Do → Check → Act with continuous learning and adjustment.
**Best when:** Solution is uncertain — you'll learn as you go. Continuous improvement contexts. Lean operations.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's the plan?"
- "Execute the plan."
- "Check the results."
- "What worked? What didn't?"
- "Adjust and repeat."

### Gantt Chart Planning
**What it does:** Visualize project timeline with tasks, dependencies, and milestones for execution clarity.
**Best when:** Multi-step implementation with real dependencies. Cross-functional coordination. Time-boxed delivery.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are the tasks?"
- "What sequence?"
- "What dependencies?"
- "What's the timeline?"
- "Who's responsible?"
- "What milestones?"

### Stakeholder Mapping
**What it does:** Identify all affected parties and plan an engagement strategy to build support and manage resistance.
**Best when:** Solution requires buy-in from people outside the immediate team. Change management. Cross-functional rollouts.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Who's affected?"
- "What's their interest?"
- "What's their influence?"
- "What's the engagement strategy?"
- "How to communicate?"

### Change Management Protocol
**What it does:** Systematically manage organizational and human dimensions of solution implementation.
**Best when:** Solution requires behavior change. Adoption is the actual problem.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's changing?"
- "Who's impacted?"
- "What resistance is expected?"
- "How to communicate?"
- "How to support transition?"
- "How to sustain?"

### Monitoring Dashboard
**What it does:** Create a visual tracking system for key metrics to ensure the solution delivers expected results.
**Best when:** Solution must be observable post-deployment. Operational rollouts. SLA-bound systems.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What metrics matter?"
- "What targets?"
- "How to measure?"
- "How to visualize?"
- "What triggers action?"
- "Review frequency?"

## Creative — Lateral methods for stuck problems

### Assumption Busting
**What it does:** Identify and challenge underlying assumptions to open new solution possibilities.
**Best when:** Solution space feels exhausted. Same five ideas keep coming up. There's an unspoken constraint nobody's named.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are we assuming?"
- "What if the opposite were true?"
- "What if that assumption were removed?"
- "What becomes possible?"

### Random Word Association
**What it does:** Use random stimuli to force the brain into unexpected connection patterns, revealing novel solutions.
**Best when:** Quick energizer to break a stuck session. Solo or group.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Pick a random word."
- "How does it relate?"
- "What connections emerge?"
- "What ideas does it spark?"
- "Make it relevant."

### Reverse Brainstorming
**What it does:** Flip the problem to "how could we *cause* or *worsen* this?" then reverse the insights to find solutions.
**Best when:** Stuck on a positive framing. Easier to list ways to fail than ways to succeed.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "How could we cause this problem?"
- "How could we make it worse?"
- "What would guarantee failure?"
- "Now reverse the insights."
- "What solutions emerge?"

### Six Thinking Hats (de Bono)
**What it does:** Explore problem from six perspectives — White (facts), Red (emotions), Yellow (benefits), Black (risks), Green (creativity), Blue (process) — for comprehensive view.
**Best when:** Group discussion that keeps collapsing into one mode (all critique, all enthusiasm). Forces structured perspective rotation.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "White hat: facts?"
- "Red hat: feelings?"
- "Yellow hat: benefits?"
- "Black hat: risks?"
- "Green hat: alternatives?"
- "Blue hat: process?"

### SCAMPER for Problems
**What it does:** Apply seven problem-solving lenses — Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Reverse.
**Best when:** You're improving an existing solution rather than designing a new one.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What to substitute?"
- "What to combine?"
- "What to adapt?"
- "What to modify?"
- "Other purposes?"
- "What to eliminate?"
- "What to reverse?"
