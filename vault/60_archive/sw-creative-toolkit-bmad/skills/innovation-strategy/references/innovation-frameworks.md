# Innovation Strategy Framework Library

Curated frameworks organized by their strategic purpose: Disruption, Business Model, Market Analysis, Strategic, Value Chain, Technology. Each entry: what the framework reveals, when it fits, and the diagnostic questions it answers.

Read this file when selecting frameworks for a phase. Match the framework to the strategic question, not the other way around.

---

## Disruption — Find market openings incumbents can't or won't defend

### Disruptive Innovation Theory (Christensen)
**What it reveals:** How new entrants use simpler, cheaper, "good-enough" solutions to overtake incumbents by serving overlooked segments.
**Best when:** Incumbent-led market with sophisticated, over-engineered offerings priced for the high end. Non-consumers exist.
**Key questions:**
- Who are the non-consumers?
- What's "good enough" for them?
- What incumbent weakness exists?
- How could simple beat sophisticated?
- What market entry point exists?

### Jobs to be Done (JTBD)
**What it reveals:** The functional, emotional, and social *jobs* customers hire products to do — surfaces unmet needs competitors miss because they're focused on features.
**Best when:** Mature category where everyone competes on features and pricing, but customer satisfaction is flat.
**Key questions:**
- What job is the customer hiring this for?
- What progress do they seek?
- What alternatives do they currently use (including non-purchase)?
- What frustrations exist in the current solution?
- What would cause them to fire it?

### Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim & Mauborgne)
**What it reveals:** Uncontested market space created by making competition irrelevant through value innovation. Find a market where you don't fight for share — you create the share.
**Best when:** Saturated red-ocean market where price war is the default competitive move.
**Key questions:**
- What factors that the industry takes for granted can we **eliminate**?
- What should we **reduce** below industry standard?
- What can we **raise** above industry standard?
- What should we **create** that the industry has never offered?

### Crossing the Chasm (Moore)
**What it reveals:** Why and how innovations stall between early adopters and the mainstream market — and the focused beachhead strategy that crosses the chasm.
**Best when:** Product has innovator/early-adopter traction but mainstream growth is stalled.
**Key questions:**
- Who are the innovators and early adopters?
- What's our beachhead market?
- What's the compelling reason to buy?
- What's our whole product (not just the core)?
- How do we cross to mainstream?

### Platform Revolution
**What it reveals:** How to transform linear value chains into exponential platform ecosystems connecting producers and consumers.
**Best when:** Multi-sided opportunities where network effects could compound. Beware: most "platform plays" aren't platforms.
**Key questions:**
- What network effects exist?
- Who are the producers? The consumers?
- What transaction do we enable?
- How do we achieve critical mass on each side?

## Business Model — Design how you create, deliver, and capture value

### Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder)
**What it reveals:** All nine building blocks of a business model on one page — customer segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue streams, key resources, activities, partnerships, cost structure.
**Best when:** Comprehensive mapping. New venture, major pivot, or first-pass business-model audit.
**Key questions:** Walk all nine blocks. The *interactions* between blocks reveal the strategic insight, not the blocks themselves.

### Value Proposition Canvas (Osterwalder)
**What it reveals:** A precise match between customer jobs/pains/gains and your products/pain-relievers/gain-creators. Product-market fit at the unit level.
**Best when:** You suspect product-market fit isn't actually there yet, or you're refining a value proposition for a specific segment.
**Key questions:**
- What are customer jobs?
- What pains do they experience?
- What gains do they desire?
- How do we relieve those pains?
- How do we create those gains?
- What products and services do that work?

### Business Model Patterns
**What it reveals:** Proven business model patterns from other industries you can apply to yours — subscription, freemium, marketplace, razor-and-blade, bait-and-hook, long-tail, etc.
**Best when:** Stuck in your industry's default model. Want to import structure from a different industry.
**Key questions:**
- What patterns could apply?
- How would each change our model?
- What second-order effects emerge?

### Revenue Model Innovation
**What it reveals:** Alternative ways to monetize value creation beyond traditional pricing.
**Best when:** The product is good but the pricing/packaging is leaving money on the table or excluding viable customers.
**Key questions:**
- How else could we charge? (usage-based, performance-based, subscription, perpetual license, freemium tiers)
- What would customers pay for differently?
- What new revenue streams could the same product unlock?

### Cost Structure Innovation
**What it reveals:** How redesigning cost structure can enable new price points or improve margins through radical efficiency.
**Best when:** Margins are squeezed AND you suspect cost structure is structurally bloated, not just inefficient.
**Key questions:**
- What are our biggest costs?
- What could we eliminate or automate?
- What could we outsource or share?
- How could we flip fixed to variable?

## Market Analysis — See the landscape clearly before betting

### TAM / SAM / SOM
**What it reveals:** Market size at three resolutions — Total Addressable, Serviceable Addressable, Serviceable Obtainable.
**Best when:** Pitching investment, deciding whether a market is worth entering, or pressure-testing growth ambitions.
**Key questions:**
- Total market size?
- What can we realistically serve?
- What can we obtain near-term?
- What assumptions underlie these numbers?
- How fast is the market growing?

### Five Forces (Porter)
**What it reveals:** Industry structure across five competitive forces — supplier power, buyer power, rivalry, threat of substitutes, threat of new entrants. Where industry profit lives.
**Best when:** Strategic positioning decisions. Choosing which industry to compete in or how to defend within one.
**Key questions:**
- Supplier power?
- Buyer power?
- Competitive rivalry?
- Threat of substitutes?
- Threat of new entrants?
- Where is the strategic opportunity?

### PESTLE
**What it reveals:** Macro environmental forces — Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental — shaping opportunities and threats.
**Best when:** Long-horizon strategy or entering an unfamiliar geography/regulated industry.
**Key questions:** Walk each axis. Most reveals come from intersections (regulatory + technological, social + legal).

### Market Timing Assessment
**What it reveals:** Whether market conditions are right for the innovation. Too early or too late both fail.
**Best when:** A solution is technically possible but it's unclear whether the market is ready.
**Key questions:**
- What needs to be true first?
- What's changing now?
- Are customers ready?
- Is technology mature enough?
- What's the window of opportunity?

### Competitive Positioning Map
**What it reveals:** A 2D plot of competitors against the dimensions that matter most — surfaces white space and differentiation opportunities.
**Best when:** You can name the two most important competitive dimensions in your market.
**Key questions:**
- What dimensions matter most?
- Where are competitors positioned?
- Where's the white space?
- What's our unique position?
- What's defensible about it?

## Strategic — Long-horizon, portfolio-level moves

### Three Horizons Framework
**What it reveals:** A balanced portfolio across current business (H1), emerging opportunities (H2), and future possibilities (H3) for sustainable growth.
**Best when:** Mature business that's healthy in H1 but lacks H2/H3 investment.
**Key questions:**
- What's our core business (H1)?
- What emerging opportunities (H2)?
- What future possibilities (H3)?
- How do we invest across horizons?
- What transitions are needed?

### Lean Startup Methodology
**What it reveals:** A build-measure-learn loop that validates assumptions in rapid cycles and pivots toward product-market fit efficiently.
**Best when:** Early-stage venture or new initiative inside an established company. High uncertainty about what will work.
**Key questions:**
- What's the riskiest assumption?
- What's the minimum viable product to test it?
- What will we measure?
- What did we learn?
- Build, pivot, or persevere?

### Innovation Ambition Matrix
**What it reveals:** Innovation portfolio balance across core, adjacent, and transformational initiatives — based on the risk and impact of each bucket.
**Best when:** You suspect the innovation portfolio is over-indexed on incremental work and under-indexed on transformational bets.
**Key questions:**
- What's core enhancement?
- What's adjacent expansion?
- What's transformational breakthrough?
- What's our portfolio balance?
- What's the right mix given our context?

### Strategic Intent Development
**What it reveals:** A bold, aspirational goal that stretches the organization beyond current capabilities and forces innovation upstream.
**Best when:** The team is tactical and incremental and needs a unifying ambition. Deliberate provocation.
**Key questions:**
- What's our audacious goal?
- What would change our industry?
- What seems impossible but valuable?
- What's our moonshot?
- What capability must we build?

### Scenario Planning
**What it reveals:** Multiple plausible futures and the strategies that work across them. Builds robust strategy under uncertainty.
**Best when:** High uncertainty across multiple critical variables. The future genuinely could go several ways.
**Key questions:**
- What critical uncertainties exist?
- What scenarios could unfold?
- How would we respond to each?
- What strategies work across all scenarios?
- What early signals would we watch?

## Value Chain — Where to play in the production-to-consumption flow

### Value Chain Analysis (Porter)
**What it reveals:** Mapping all activities from raw input to end customer to identify where value is created and captured.
**Best when:** Vertical integration / disintegration decisions, or finding where margin actually lives in a complex product.
**Key questions:**
- What's the full value chain?
- Where is value created?
- What activities are we good at?
- What could we outsource?
- Where could we disintermediate?

### Unbundling Analysis
**What it reveals:** Opportunities to break apart integrated value chains and capture specific high-value components.
**Best when:** A bundled incumbent looks vulnerable to specialists who do one piece extraordinarily well.
**Key questions:**
- What's bundled together?
- What could be separated?
- Where's the most value concentrated?
- What would customers pay for separately?
- Who else could provide which pieces?

### Platform Ecosystem Design
**What it reveals:** How to architect multi-sided platforms that create value through network effects and reduced transaction costs.
**Best when:** You've validated that real network effects exist (not just imagined ones).
**Key questions:**
- What sides exist?
- What value exchange between them?
- How do we attract each side?
- What network effects compound?
- What's our revenue model?
- How do we govern the platform?

### Make vs. Buy Analysis
**What it reveals:** Strategic decisions about vertical integration vs. outsourcing for competitive advantage.
**Best when:** Capability decisions where building, buying, and partnering all look plausible.
**Key questions:**
- What's our core competence?
- What provides competitive advantage?
- What should we own?
- What should we partner on?
- What's the risk of each path?

### Partnership Strategy
**What it reveals:** Strategic partnerships and ecosystem plays that expand capabilities and reach efficiently.
**Best when:** Speed matters and you can't build the capability in time, or the capability isn't core enough to warrant building.
**Key questions:**
- Who has complementary strengths?
- What could we achieve together?
- What's the value exchange?
- How do we structure the partnership?
- What's the governance model?

## Technology — Time and structure tech-enabled bets

### Technology Adoption Lifecycle
**What it reveals:** How innovations diffuse through society — innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards.
**Best when:** Timing market entry. Different segments demand different go-to-market.
**Key questions:**
- Who are the innovators and early adopters?
- What's our adoption strategy by segment?
- Where are we on the curve?

### S-Curve Analysis
**What it reveals:** Inflection points in technology maturity and market adoption — when to jump curves to the next technology.
**Best when:** Mature technology approaching its limits and a new alternative is emerging.
**Key questions:**
- Where are we on the S-curve?
- What's the next curve?
- When should we jump?
- What's the tipping point?

### Technology Roadmapping
**What it reveals:** Evolution of technology capabilities aligned with strategic goals and market timing.
**Best when:** Multi-year tech-product strategy. Sequencing capability development.
**Key questions:**
- What capabilities do we need?
- What's the sequence?
- What dependencies exist?
- What's the timeline?
- Where do we invest first?

### Open Innovation Strategy
**What it reveals:** How to leverage external ideas, technologies, and paths to market to accelerate beyond internal R&D.
**Best when:** Internal R&D is bottlenecked, or the relevant innovation is happening faster outside than inside.
**Key questions:**
- What could we source externally?
- Who has relevant innovation?
- How do we collaborate?
- What's our IP strategy?
- How do we integrate external innovation?

### Digital Transformation Framework
**What it reveals:** How to reimagine business models, operations, and customer experiences through digital enablers.
**Best when:** Established business with significant analog or pre-cloud operations, facing digital-native competition.
**Key questions:**
- What digital capabilities exist?
- How could they transform our model?
- What customer experience improvements?
- What operational efficiencies?
- What new business models become possible?
