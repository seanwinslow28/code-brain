# Design Thinking Method Library

Curated methods organized by the five design-thinking phases plus implementation. Each entry: what the method does, when it fits, and facilitation prompts you can drop into a session verbatim.

Read this file when selecting methods for a phase. Pick 3–5 per phase that fit the user's challenge, constraints, and access to real users.

---

## Empathize — Understand the people you're designing for

### User Interviews
**What it does:** Deep conversations to surface user needs, experiences, and pain points through active listening.
**Best when:** You can talk to 5+ real users for 30–60 minutes each. Open exploration, not validation.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What brings you here today?"
- "Walk me through a recent experience."
- "What frustrates you most?"
- "What would make this easier?"
- "Tell me more about that."

### Empathy Mapping
**What it does:** Visual artifact organizing what users *say*, *think*, *do*, and *feel* — surfaces the gap between stated and observed behavior.
**Best when:** You have raw research notes and need to synthesize them into a shared team artifact.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What did they say?"
- "What might they be thinking?"
- "What actions did they take?"
- "What emotions surfaced?"

### Shadowing
**What it does:** Observe users in their natural environment to capture unspoken behaviors, workarounds, and contextual factors.
**Best when:** Stated behavior diverges from actual behavior; you have access to the work environment.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Watch without interrupting."
- "Note their workarounds."
- "What patterns emerge?"
- "What do they not say?"

### Journey Mapping
**What it does:** Document the complete user experience across touchpoints, time, and emotional state to surface pain points and opportunities.
**Best when:** Multi-step or multi-channel experience; need to spot where the journey breaks.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's their starting point?"
- "What steps do they take?"
- "Where do they struggle?"
- "What delights them?"
- "What's the emotional arc?"

### Diary Studies
**What it does:** Users document their own experiences over days or weeks, capturing authentic moments researchers wouldn't see.
**Best when:** Behavior unfolds over time (habit formation, recurring tasks); willing-to-participate users available.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What did you experience today?"
- "How did you feel?"
- "What worked or didn't?"
- "What surprised you?"

## Define — Frame a user-centered problem statement

### Problem Framing
**What it does:** Transforms raw observations into clear, actionable problem statements that inspire (rather than constrain) ideation.
**Best when:** Empathy phase produced lots of insights but no shared sense of *the* problem yet.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's the real problem?"
- "Who experiences this?"
- "Why does it matter?"
- "What would success look like?"

### How Might We
**What it does:** Reframes problems as opportunity questions that open solution space without prescribing answers.
**Best when:** Transitioning from Define to Ideate; you want to seed multiple HMW questions to generate against.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "How might we help users…?"
- "How might we make it easier to…?"
- "How might we reduce the friction of…?"

### Point of View Statement
**What it does:** Specific user-centered problem statement capturing *who*, *what need*, and *why* (the insight).
**Best when:** You need a single anchor sentence the team can return to during ideation.
**Format:** `[User type] needs [need] because [insight].`
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's driving this need?"
- "Why does it matter to them?"

### Affinity Clustering
**What it does:** Group related observations on a wall (or board) to reveal patterns and opportunity themes.
**Best when:** You have 30+ raw insights from interviews/observation and need to find the signal.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What connects these?"
- "What themes emerge?"
- "Group similar items."
- "Name each cluster."
- "What story do they tell?"

### Jobs to be Done
**What it does:** Identify the functional, emotional, and social *jobs* users hire solutions to do.
**Best when:** You need a non-feature-centric way to describe what users actually want done.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What job are they trying to do?"
- "What progress do they want?"
- "What are they really hiring this for?"
- "What alternatives exist?"

## Ideate — Generate diverse solutions

### Brainstorming
**What it does:** Generate large quantity of diverse ideas without judgment. Quantity over quality.
**Best when:** Open solution space, group of 3–8, time-boxed 15–30 min per HMW.
**Ground rules:**
- No bad ideas. Defer judgment.
- Build on others.
- Go for quantity.
- Be visual.
- Stay on topic.

### Crazy 8s
**What it does:** Each person sketches eight solution variations in eight minutes — one minute per sketch.
**Best when:** You need to break out of obvious solutions; works solo or in groups.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Fold paper in 8."
- "1 minute per sketch."
- "No overthinking."
- "Quantity over quality."
- "Push past the obvious."

### SCAMPER Design
**What it does:** Apply seven design lenses to existing solutions: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other uses, Eliminate, Reverse.
**Best when:** You're improving or transforming an existing product/feature, not greenfield ideation.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What could we substitute?"
- "How could we combine elements?"
- "What could we adapt?"
- "How could we modify it?"
- "Other purposes?"
- "What to eliminate?"
- "What if reversed?"

### Provotype Sketching
**What it does:** Deliberately provocative, extreme prototypes to spark breakthrough thinking — push past safe answers.
**Best when:** Team is converging too fast; you want to stretch the solution space.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's the most extreme version?"
- "Make it ridiculous."
- "Push boundaries."
- "What useful insights emerge from the extreme?"

### Analogous Inspiration
**What it does:** Find inspiration from completely different domains; spark innovative connections.
**Best when:** The problem feels stuck inside its own conventions.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What other field solves this?"
- "How does nature handle this?"
- "What's an analogous problem?"
- "What can we borrow?"

## Prototype — Make ideas tangible and testable

### Paper Prototyping
**What it does:** Quick low-fidelity sketches and mockups to make ideas tangible enough to test.
**Best when:** Early concept testing; you want feedback in hours not days.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Sketch it out."
- "Make it rough."
- "Focus on the core concept."
- "Test assumptions."
- "Learn fast."

### Role Playing
**What it does:** Act out user scenarios and service interactions to test the experience flow and pain points.
**Best when:** Service design, multi-actor flows, onboarding/handoff moments.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Play the user."
- "Act out the scenario."
- "What feels awkward?"
- "Where does it break?"
- "What works?"

### Wizard of Oz
**What it does:** Simulate complex functionality manually behind the scenes — user thinks the system is real.
**Best when:** Testing whether a concept resonates *before* you build the engineering work to support it.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Fake the backend."
- "Focus on the experience, not the build."
- "What do they think is happening?"
- "Does the concept work?"

### Storyboarding
**What it does:** Visualize the user experience across time and touchpoints as a sequential illustrated narrative.
**Best when:** Multi-step or multi-channel experience; need a shared visual the team can react to.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's scene 1?"
- "How does it progress?"
- "What's the emotional journey?"
- "Where's the climax?"
- "How does it resolve?"

### Physical Mockups
**What it does:** Build tangible artifacts users can touch and interact with to test form and function.
**Best when:** Hardware, packaging, ergonomics, or any product where the physical interaction matters.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Make it 3D."
- "Use basic materials."
- "Make it interactive."
- "Test ergonomics."
- "Gather reactions."

## Test — Validate with real users

### Usability Testing
**What it does:** Watch real users attempt tasks with the prototype to identify friction points.
**Best when:** You have a prototype and 5–7 representative users available.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Try to accomplish X."
- "Think aloud, please."
- "Don't help them."
- "Where do they struggle?"
- "What surprises them?"

### Feedback Capture Grid
**What it does:** 2x2 grid organizing user feedback across *likes*, *questions*, *ideas*, and *changes*.
**Best when:** You want to convert messy session notes into actionable insights fast.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What did they like?"
- "What questions arose?"
- "What ideas did they have?"
- "What needs changing?"

### A/B Testing
**What it does:** Compare two variations to understand which approach better serves user needs.
**Best when:** You have two viable directions and a way to put them in front of users at scale.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Show version A."
- "Show version B."
- "Which works better?"
- "Why the difference?"
- "What does the data show?"

### Assumption Testing
**What it does:** Identify and validate the *riskiest* assumptions underlying your solution to reduce build risk.
**Best when:** Pre-prototype or early-prototype; you want to find what would prove you wrong before investing.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What are we assuming?"
- "How can we test this?"
- "What would prove us wrong?"
- "What's the riskiest assumption?"

### Iterate and Refine
**What it does:** Use test insights to improve the prototype through rapid cycles of refinement and re-testing.
**Best when:** Post-test; you have clear signal on what works and what doesn't.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What did we learn?"
- "What needs fixing?"
- "What stays?"
- "Make changes quickly."
- "Test again."

## Implement — Move from validated concept to scaled solution

### Pilot Programs
**What it does:** Launch small-scale real-world implementation to learn before full rollout.
**Best when:** Concept is validated; you need to learn how it behaves in production conditions.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Start small."
- "Real users, real context."
- "What breaks?"
- "What works?"
- "Scale lessons learned."

### Service Blueprinting
**What it does:** Map all service components — frontstage interactions, backstage operations, supporting systems, handoffs.
**Best when:** Service or multi-touchpoint product; you need a single map for cross-functional alignment.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What's visible to users?"
- "What happens backstage?"
- "What systems are needed?"
- "Where are the handoffs?"

### Design System Creation
**What it does:** Build consistent patterns, components, and guidelines for scalable, repeatable implementation.
**Best when:** Solution will be replicated across surfaces, products, or teams.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "What patterns repeat?"
- "Create reusable components."
- "Document the standards."
- "Enable consistency without micromanagement."

### Stakeholder Alignment
**What it does:** Bring team and stakeholders along the discovery journey to build shared understanding and commitment.
**Best when:** Implementation requires buy-in from people who weren't in the empathy/ideation phases.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "Show the research."
- "Walk through the prototypes."
- "Share user stories."
- "Build empathy."
- "Get buy-in."

### Measurement Framework
**What it does:** Define success metrics and feedback loops to track impact and inform future iterations.
**Best when:** You're handing off to operate-and-learn mode and need to know what "working" looks like in numbers.
**Facilitation prompts:**
- "How will we measure success?"
- "What are the key metrics?"
- "How do we gather feedback?"
- "When do we revisit?"
