## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[silent-decay-in-strategic-pipelines]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

### 1. Add “metric corruption,” not merely missing semantic validation

**What to add:** Campbell’s Law: once a semantic-quality measure becomes an optimization target, agents will learn to manufacture its appearance. “Novel concepts produced,” citation count, application relevance scores, and cross-vault connections can all increase while strategic value declines. The sharper sentence pattern is: **“The pipeline does not merely fail to measure value; it progressively substitutes what is measurable for what was valued.”**

**Anchor:** Donald T. Campbell, [“Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change”](https://jmde.journals.publicknowledgeproject.org/index.php/jmde_1/article/view/297). Campbell explains how quantitative indicators become distorted when used for consequential decisions.

**What this unlocks:** An **anti-Goodhart evaluation spec** for Code-Brain: every strategic metric paired with a countermetric, an unscored holdout review, and evidence of downstream use. Sean could ship a portfolio-ready one-pager called **“How I Prevented My Agent Fleet from Gaming Its Own KPIs,”** including tests such as “concept cited in a decision within 30 days” rather than “concept created.”

### 2. Add double-loop decay: the objective can expire while execution remains correct

**What to add:** Argyris’s distinction between single-loop and double-loop learning. The article currently assumes the pipeline’s goal remains valid and outputs merely stop advancing it. A worse failure occurs when agents improve execution against an obsolete governing assumption: wrong role targets, outdated definitions of “strong fit,” or research optimized for a career narrative Sean has already outgrown. Sentence pattern: **“Silent decay begins one level above output quality: the system corrects actions without reopening the assumptions that make those actions sensible.”**

**Anchor:** Chris Argyris, [“Double Loop Learning in Organizations”](https://hbr.org/1977/09/double-loop-learning-in-organizations), extended in *Knowledge for Action*.

**What this unlocks:** A **strategic-assumption retirement protocol**: each scheduled agent records the governing hypothesis it serves, disconfirming evidence, and a review date. That becomes an intent-engineering **agent spec plus executable runbook** showing how objectives themselves get revised—not just prompts, models, or thresholds.

### 3. Add a structural remedy: separate “inside-and-now” control from “outside-and-future” adaptation

**What to add:** Stafford Beer’s Viable System Model, specifically the tension between **System 3** (internal optimization) and **System 4** (environmental intelligence), plus algedonic signals that bypass routine reporting when viability is threatened. The current concept diagnoses semantic stagnation but gives no organizational topology for preventing it. Sentence pattern: **“A pipeline decays when its operational controller also owns the judgment about whether its environment has changed.”**

**Anchor:** Stafford Beer, *Brain of the Firm* and *Diagnosing the System for Organizations*; the model explicitly assigns outward adaptation to System 4 and urgent escalation to [algedonic signals](https://metaphorum.org/staffords-work/viable-system-model).

**What this unlocks:** A **fleet viability architecture**: execution agents cannot certify their own strategic relevance; a separate environment-sensing agent compares job-market evidence, portfolio positioning, and research demand against current intent charters. Sean could ship this as an **interactive governance demo or architecture diagram**—a stronger agentic-engineering artifact than another observability dashboard.
