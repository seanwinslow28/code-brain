## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[epistemic-artifacts-as-strategic-proof]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Replace “cryptographic proof” with a provenance ladder**

   - **Add:** Separate four claims the article currently collapses: *stylistic trace → process evidence → tamper-evident lineage → verified identity*. Construction lines and annotated flops occupy the first two levels; they are forgeable and therefore not cryptographic.
   - **Anchor:** The [C2PA Technical Specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html), especially its signed manifests, ingredient assertions, action history, and content bindings. Crucially, C2PA validates the integrity of provenance assertions—not whether those assertions are true, valuable, or proof of unaided human creation.
   - **Unlock:** An executable **“Provably Mine” portfolio demo**: hash each source, generation, reroll, edit, and decision note into an append-only manifest; then show what survives modification and what does not. Sentence pattern: “This artifact *suggests* human handling; this signed lineage *demonstrates* an unaltered process record; neither alone proves authorship.” That would turn a loose aesthetic claim into an agentic-engineering case study about evidence architecture.

2. **Add medium-specific authenticity using the autographic/allographic distinction**

   - **Add:** Ask whether authenticity belongs to the physical instance or to the reproducible work. Pencil grain may matter constitutively to a drawing, while identical copies of a text, score, program, or game build can remain valid instances of the same work. The present concept incorrectly treats every creative medium as though it were a painting.
   - **Anchor:** Nelson Goodman’s *[Languages of Art](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goodman-aesthetics/)*, specifically the distinction between **autographic** works, where original-versus-forgery matters, and **allographic** works, which can be correctly instantiated from a notation.
   - **Unlock:** A **medium-authenticity matrix** for Sean’s portfolio: Substack prose, pencil illustration, sprite sheet, LoRA, agent specification, and execution trace—each with a different unit of authorship and appropriate evidence. This would support a sharper essay: “A sprite’s pixels may be allographic; its selection history is autographic.” It also tells him when preserving paper grain adds signal and when it is merely decorative theater.

3. **Add an anti-intentionalist challenge: provenance cannot certify quality or meaning**

   - **Add:** Explicitly distinguish “Sean made these decisions” from “those decisions explain why the work succeeds.” A diary of rerolls can prove activity while inviting exactly the activity-proof failure named in the related concept. Models can also manufacture plausible flops, annotations, and construction marks.
   - **Anchor:** W. K. Wimsatt and Monroe Beardsley’s “[The Intentional Fallacy](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/WimsattBeardsley_Intentional.pdf),” which rejects authorial intention as the standard for judging a finished work.
   - **Unlock:** A **two-channel portfolio evaluation protocol**: reviewers first score the artifact blind, then inspect its provenance and decision trail. Publish the score delta. This yields a rigorous Substack essay or hiring one-pager demonstrating that process evidence answers “who controlled the work?” while blind evaluation answers “is the work any good?”—a distinction the current concept cannot make.
