## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[agentic-systems-across-personal-creative-and-professional-workflows]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

## 1. Add a “function allocation” layer—not one autonomy level per agent

**What to add:** Model every workflow across four stages: information acquisition, information analysis, decision selection, and action implementation. Assign each stage its own autonomy level and reversal cost. This punctures the article’s assumption that a workflow is simply “agentic” or “manual.”

**Anchor:** Raja Parasuraman, Thomas Sheridan, and Christopher Wickens, [“A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11760769/) (2000). Their central finding is that automation changes human work and coordination; it does not merely remove labor.

**Sentence pattern:** “Automate acquisition and analysis; require human choice when consequences are costly; automate execution only when reversal is cheap.”

**What it unlocks:** An **Agent Function Allocation Matrix** covering the Daily Driver, Vault Critic, sprite pipeline, and job feed. That becomes a portfolio one-pager demonstrating mature autonomy design—not another inventory of agents. It also gives intent-engineering a concrete decision procedure for setting autonomy boundaries.

## 2. Add “situated repair” as a contradiction to intent-first execution

**What to add:** Treat specifications and plans as resources agents consult, not programs that determine behavior. Record the moments where the environment invalidates the declared intent: missing context, contradictory signals, misunderstood artifacts, and improvised recovery. Make `observed_situation`, `plan_breakdown`, `repair_attempt`, and `human_intervention` first-class trace events.

**Anchor:** Lucy Suchman, [*Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Plans_and_Situated_Actions.html?id=AJ_eBJtHxmsC) (1987). Suchman directly challenges the planning model underlying AI systems: purposeful action is produced through interaction with local circumstances, not by executing a complete prior representation.

**Sentence pattern:** “The intent spec predicts the normal path; the repair trace reveals the actual system.”

**What it unlocks:** An **executable “intent under ambiguity” demo** for the intent-engineering MCP server: replay one task across changed circumstances and show when the agent repairs, escalates, or blindly obeys. It also supports a strong Substack essay—“Your Agent’s Spec Is Not Its Behavior”—where Sean currently risks sounding like another advocate for better declarative instructions.

## 3. Add “graceful extensibility” instead of equating health with successful routine execution

**What to add:** Evaluate whether the fleet can stretch safely when surprise exceeds its designed envelope. Track adaptive capacity consumed, which agent absorbed the disruption, what work was shed, and whether the system preserved human judgment. A green scheduled-run dashboard is reliability; it is not resilience.

**Anchor:** David D. Woods, [“Four Concepts for Resilience and the Implications for the Future of Resilience Engineering”](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.RESS.2015.03.018) (2015). Woods distinguishes robustness and rebound from **graceful extensibility**: maintaining the capacity to adapt when events challenge known boundaries.

**Sentence pattern:** “When capacity is exhausted, preserve X, shed Y, recruit Z, and expose the boundary breach.”

**What it unlocks:** A **fleet surprise-drill runbook** and portfolio demo: simultaneously remove the MBP route, corrupt one input, and exceed a time budget; then measure degradation, work shedding, and recovery. That demonstrates operational judgment beyond “seven agents ran overnight.”
