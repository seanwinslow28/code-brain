## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[agentic-engineering-and-ai-product-management-integration]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Boundary Object PM” as the missing translation layer.**  
Anchor it on Susan Leigh Star and James R. Griesemer’s paper **“Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects”**. The current concept says agentic engineering “supports” AI PM, but it does not explain how PM artifacts survive translation across engineers, agents, executives, users, and evaluators.

Add a mode like: **“Treat every agent spec, PRD, eval, and portfolio proof as a boundary object: plastic enough for each stakeholder to use, stable enough to preserve intent.”**

This unlocks a sharper artifact: a **portfolio one-pager or Substack essay** titled something like *The AI PM as Boundary Object Designer*. It would let Sean argue that his edge is not “PM who can code” or “builder with product taste,” but someone who designs durable coordination artifacts between humans and agent systems.

2. **Add “Joint Cognitive Systems” to contradict the automation-booster frame.**  
Anchor it on David Woods and Erik Hollnagel’s book **Joint Cognitive Systems: Foundations of Cognitive Systems Engineering**. The article currently implies agentic engineering improves PM workflows through automation and optimization. That is too thin. Woods/Hollnagel would force the missing question: **what new coordination burden does the agent fleet create?**

Add this sentence pattern: **“The PM job does not disappear into automation; it moves upward into managing the joint cognitive system: what the human monitors, what the agent notices, where authority shifts, and how recovery happens after surprise.”**

This unlocks a decision artifact the current concept cannot produce: an **agent operating model / authority map** for Code-Brain. Columns: task class, agent autonomy, human checkpoint, failure signal, recovery owner. That artifact would be strong for senior AI-PM roles because it frames Sean as someone who understands automation risk, not just automation capability.

3. **Add “Situated Action” as a critique of intent-first overconfidence.**  
Anchor it on Lucy Suchman’s book **Plans and Situated Actions**. Sean’s intent-engineering frame says agents need better specifications of “what to want.” Suchman adds the necessary contradiction: plans do not determine action; action is improvised in context. This is directly relevant to agent fleets, where the gap between written intent and runtime behavior is the whole problem.

Add a mode like: **“Intent specs are not commands; they are resources for situated action. The real product surface is the repair loop when the plan meets an unexpected file, missing permission, stale context, or ambiguous user goal.”**

This unlocks a better **runbook or executable demo**: not “here is my agent fleet,” but **“watch the fleet recover from a bad assumption.”** Demo genres: failed daily-note injection, stale vault concept, blocked MCP access, conflicting hook rule. The current article can only describe integration; this would let Sean show operational judgment under uncertainty.
