## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[ai-product-manager-and-vibe-coding-interview-integration]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “selection-validity mode,” anchored on Frank Schmidt & John Hunter’s “The Validity and Utility of Selection Methods in Personnel Psychology.”**  
   The current note treats vibe-coding interviews as a cultural signal. Add the psychometric counterframe: interviews are instruments with predictive validity, adverse impact, criterion contamination, and work-sample fidelity. Schmidt/Hunter’s meta-analysis is the canonical missing reference. Source: [Personnel selection summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personnel_selection).  
   **Unlocks:** a portfolio artifact Sean does not yet have: “How I’d Design an AI-PM Work-Sample Interview.” Genre: hiring-system teardown. It would let him evaluate Sierra/Anthropic-style loops as measurement systems, not just prep for them.

2. **Add “situated-action mode,” anchored on Lucy Suchman’s _Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication_.**  
   The concept assumes “AI PM + live coding” means strategy plus execution. Suchman gives the contradiction: real work is not plan execution; it is improvisation inside tools, breakdowns, social cues, and environmental constraints. Source: [Lucy Suchman / Plans and Situated Actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Suchman).  
   **Sentence pattern:** “The interview is not testing whether the candidate can follow a plan; it is testing whether they can recover intention when the plan collapses.”  
   **Unlocks:** a Substack essay or interview runbook: “Vibe Coding Is a Situated-Action Test.” This would make Sean sound sharper than generic “AI PMs need to code” discourse.

3. **Add “human-AI team compatibility mode,” anchored on Bansal, Nushi, Kamar, Weld, Lasecki & Horvitz’s “A Case for Backward Compatibility for Human-AI Teams.”**  
   The missing facet is that AI-PM interviews increasingly test whether a candidate can manage changing model behavior, not merely use AI fluently. Bansal et al. show that a more accurate AI update can still hurt team performance if it violates learned user expectations. Source: [arXiv summary](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01148).  
   **Unlocks:** an agentic-engineering demo: a tiny “model update compatibility evaluator” that shows before/after agent behavior, user trust breakage, and regression gates. This turns the concept into a concrete IC signal for roles where Sean must prove he can product-manage agents as unstable teammates.
