## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[ai-fluency-agentic-engineering-signal-and-portfolio-projects-for-interview-prep]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “legibility of practice” mode, anchored on Donald Schön’s _The Reflective Practitioner_**

   Sean’s concept treats portfolio projects as proof that he can use AI tools. Missing: proof that he can **think-in-action while building**. Add a section that distinguishes “AI fluency” from “reflective practice under uncertainty.”

   Sentence pattern: “Here is the situation I entered, the frame I initially used, the surprise that broke the frame, and the revised move I made.”

   This unlocks a stronger artifact than a project gallery: a **case-study teardown** of Code-Brain or 16BitFit where the signal is not “I built agents,” but “I can notice when an agent system is optimizing the wrong thing and reframe the work.” That maps directly to senior PM, AI-PM, and agentic-engineering interviews where evaluators care about judgment, not tool exposure.

2. **Add “taste as evaluation function,” anchored on Christopher Alexander’s _Notes on the Synthesis of Form_**

   The article currently says portfolio projects should showcase agentic capability, but it lacks a theory of **why one artifact is better than another**. Alexander gives Sean a missing bridge: design quality emerges from resolving concrete misfits between a form and its context.

   Add “misfit inventory” as a technique: every portfolio artifact should name the forces it resolves. For Code-Brain: cost ceiling, unattended autonomy, private/public boundary, citation collapse, launchd fragility, stale context, agent overreach.

   This unlocks a **portfolio one-pager with evaluative teeth**: not “here are my projects,” but “here are the misfits I designed against.” It also gives Sean a sharper interview answer to “why did you build it this way?” Current concept can prove activity; Alexander lets it prove taste.

3. **Add “demo as falsifiable claim,” anchored on Bret Victor’s talk _Inventing on Principle_**

   The missing contradiction: “agentic engineering signal” is not strongest when described. It is strongest when the viewer can manipulate it and see the principle hold or fail. Bret Victor’s work is the reference for making an idea inspectable rather than asserted.

   Add a rule: every portfolio project should express one principle as an executable demo. Example: “Agents should carry explicit intent, stop rules, and health metrics, not just prompts.” Then ship a tiny **intent-engineering playground** where changing the intent spec visibly changes agent behavior, escalation, or refusal.

   This unlocks an **interactive interview artifact**: a 3-minute browser demo or CLI replay that turns Sean’s agent philosophy into something falsifiable. Current concept risks sounding like “I know agent tools.” This turns it into “I can make agent behavior legible, steerable, and testable.”
