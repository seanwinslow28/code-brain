## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[cognitive-optimization-in-job-hunt-and-daily-routine]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Stress-Exposure Ladder” as the missing bridge between protected time and interview performance.**  
   Anchor it on Donald Meichenbaum’s *Stress Inoculation Training* and Albert Bandura’s paper “Self-Efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral Change.” Right now the concept implies: protect energy, do rituals, perform better. Missing is a graded exposure protocol: deliberately rehearse increasingly costly job-hunt situations before the real one.

   Pattern to add: `low-stakes rep -> simulated pressure -> live pressure -> recovery review`.  
   This unlocks a **job-hunt performance runbook**: mock recruiter screen, mock hiring-manager screen, hostile portfolio critique, live interview, postmortem. It turns “confidence ritual” from mood management into trainable self-efficacy.

2. **Add “Activation Energy Design” from behavioral design, not productivity.**  
   Anchor it on B.J. Fogg’s *Tiny Habits* and the Fogg Behavior Model: behavior happens when motivation, ability, and prompt converge. Sean’s current concept overweights cognitive energy as a scarce fuel tank. Fogg adds the contradicting frame: many failures are not energy failures, they are prompt/friction failures.

   Pattern to add: `When I finish X, I do tiny Y, because Z is already open/visible/loaded.`  
   This unlocks an **agent-assisted daily routine spec**: calendar prompt, workspace state, Claude session bootstrap, job-search tab set, “next artifact” queue. Current concept can say “protect 8:30-9:30.” This addition can specify the exact environmental trigger that makes 8:30-9:30 start without negotiation.

3. **Add “Recovery as Training Load Management,” not just burnout prevention.**  
   Anchor it on Jim Loehr and Tony Schwartz’s *The Power of Full Engagement*, plus Anders Ericsson, Ralf Krampe, and Clemens Tesch-Romer’s paper “The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance.” The missing facet is periodization: high-cognition job-hunt work should be treated like training blocks, with intensity, recovery, and adaptation tracked.

   Pattern to add: `performance block -> deliberate recovery -> adaptation note -> next-load adjustment`.  
   This unlocks a **weekly job-hunt operating review**: which activities are high-load, which are recovery, which are skill-acquisition reps, and which are fake productivity. It also gives Sean a stronger Substack angle: “I stopped treating job hunting as a to-do list and started treating it as cognitive athletics.”
