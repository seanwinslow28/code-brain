## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[deep-research-queue-integration-across-folders]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “queue triage as epistemic routing,” anchored on Herbert Simon’s _The Sciences of the Artificial_.**

   The current concept treats the queue as a logistics hub: research questions go in, LDR/Gemini come out. Missing is the design question Simon would force: what kind of problem is this before choosing a processor?

   Add a routing layer that classifies each queue item by **problem shape**: well-structured lookup, ill-structured synthesis, adversarial due diligence, taste/voice critique, speculative exploration, or decision support.

   Sentence pattern to add:  
   “This queue is not a backlog of questions; it is a problem-shape classifier that decides which kind of inquiry deserves which machine.”

   This unlocks a **research-router runbook** or **agent spec** where Sean can show judgment, not just automation. It would help him make decisions like: “Does this go to local LDR, Gemini DR, LLM Council, manual reading, or no research at all?” The current concept cannot distinguish cheap curiosity from consequential uncertainty.

2. **Add “progressive summarization with decision pressure,” anchored on Tiago Forte’s _Building a Second Brain_ plus Gary Klein’s _Sources of Power_.**

   The note says research is centralized, but it does not say how outputs become decisions. Forte gives the capture/distill/express pipeline; Klein gives recognition-primed decision-making under uncertainty. Together they expose the missing facet: research queues should not merely produce notes. They should produce **decision-ready compression**.

   Add a rule that every completed research item must end in one of four output shapes: **decision, contradiction, reusable pattern, or artifact seed**. If it cannot produce one, it was probably not worth researching.

   Sentence pattern to add:  
   “A completed research task is not done when summarized; it is done when it changes a choice, kills an assumption, or creates a shippable artifact.”

   This unlocks a **Substack essay** on “research debt,” a **daily-driver upgrade**, or a **vault lint rule** that flags dead-end research. Sean’s current concept risks becoming an elegant inbox for intellectual hoarding; this addition turns it into a pressure system for judgment.

3. **Add “citation reliability tiers,” anchored on Latanya Sweeney’s data provenance work and the W3C PROV model, specifically _PROV-DM: The PROV Data Model_.**

   Sean already knows LDR citation quality collapses on compound prompts. But the concept under review does not encode provenance as a first-class property. It names LDR and Gemini DR as processors without tracking what kind of evidence each output can safely support.

   Add provenance tiers for each research result: **primary source verified, secondary source corroborated, model-synthesized but unverified, speculative lead, rejected/fabricated**. Each queue item should carry a confidence/provenance footer, not just a checkbox.

   Sentence pattern to add:  
   “The queue should preserve evidence lineage: which claims came from primary sources, which came from synthesis, and which are only leads for future verification.”

   This unlocks an **evidence-grade research artifact**, useful for job-hunt portfolio claims, public essays, and agent-fleet credibility. It also enables a concrete **knowledge-lint check**: reject or quarantine research outputs that cite weak or fabricated sources. The current concept can route work; this addition lets Sean defend why the resulting knowledge should be trusted.
