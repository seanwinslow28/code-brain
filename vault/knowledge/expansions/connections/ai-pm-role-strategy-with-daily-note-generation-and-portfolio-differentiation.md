## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

## 1. Add a “costly-signal” test for portfolio work

**What:** Separate *capability claims* from signals that are difficult to fake. Score every portfolio artifact on relevance, verifiability, and imitation cost. A 250-agent configuration count is descriptive; a replayable failure investigation with traces, trade-offs, and a shipped correction is a signal.

**Anchor:** Michael Spence, [“Job Market Signaling”](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/87/3/355/1909092) (1973), especially the distinction between observable signals and attributes that merely function as indices.

**Sentence pattern:** “An agent violated **X constraint** under **Y condition**; I diagnosed **Z mechanism**, rejected **A alternative**, and changed **B measurable outcome**.”

**Unlocks:** A **portfolio evidence matrix** mapping each target-role competency to one hard-to-fake artifact: executable demo, trace, decision record, benchmark, or incident report. This corrects the current concept’s assumption that “having unusual infrastructure” automatically communicates “AI-PM judgment.”

## 2. Replace generic daily-note synthesis with Critical Incident capture

**What:** Add a structured **Critical Incident mode** to Daily Note Generation:

`trigger → intended outcome → decision under uncertainty → rejected alternative → action → observable consequence → reusable principle`

Only promote entries containing a consequential choice or disconfirming evidence. Routine summaries should remain logs, not career evidence.

**Anchor:** John C. Flanagan, [“The Critical Incident Technique”](https://www.apa.org/pubs/databases/psycinfo/critical.html) (1954). Flanagan’s method collects specific consequential behaviors rather than opinions or generalized self-assessments.

**Sentence pattern:** “When **specific condition** occurred, I chose **action** over **alternative** because **constraint**; the result was **evidence**, which changed my operating rule to **principle**.”

**Unlocks:** An automatically maintained **behavioral-interview story bank**, plus stronger Substack postmortems and portfolio case studies. Sean could produce evidence of prioritization, recovery, governance, and technical judgment—not merely claim that daily notes support synthesis.

## 3. Turn the job search into an Opportunity Solution Tree

**What:** Reframe “AI PM > Tech PM > Creative PM” as an untested solution hierarchy. Build:

`measurable hiring outcome → employer pains → positioning hypotheses → portfolio/interview experiments → riskiest assumptions → evidence`

For example: “Fleet observability is the differentiator” becomes a hypothesis tested through recruiter comprehension, interview conversion, and which artifacts interviewers actually discuss.

**Anchor:** Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*, operationalized in her [Opportunity Solution Tree](https://www.producttalk.org/2016/08/opportunity-solution-tree/): begin with an outcome, discover opportunities through concrete stories, compare multiple solutions, then test their riskiest assumptions.

**Sentence pattern:** “We believe **employer archetype** struggles with **observed pain**; showing **artifact** should produce **behavioral signal**; we will revise the thesis if **test result** fails.”

**Unlocks:** A **weekly job-search experiment board**, role-archetype decision rubric, and publishable essay such as “I Ran My Job Search Like Product Discovery.” The current concept joins three nouns; this addition supplies causal hypotheses, competing strategies, and kill criteria.
