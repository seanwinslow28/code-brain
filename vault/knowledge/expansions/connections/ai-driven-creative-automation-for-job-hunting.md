## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[ai-driven-creative-automation-for-job-hunting]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “show-the-workflow artifact critique” anchored on Bret Victor’s essay _Inventing on Principle_.**  
   Exemplifier: Bret Victor, [_Inventing on Principle_](https://worrydream.com/#!/InventingOnPrinciple).  
   Current concept says “Jitter helps portfolio/job hunting.” Missing piece: the portfolio artifact should reveal Sean’s principle, not just output polish. Add a mode that asks: “What creative decision became inspectable, manipulable, or faster because of the automation?”  
   This unlocks a **portfolio teardown essay/demo**: before/after workflow, control surface, iteration trace, and decision log. Without this, Sean risks sounding like “I use AI tools to make assets faster,” which is generic creator-tool adoption.

2. **Add “programmable media system” anchored on Alan Kay’s _Doing with Images Makes Symbols_.**  
   Exemplifier: Alan Kay, [_Doing with Images Makes Symbols_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p2LZLYcu_JY).  
   The concept treats Jitter as a production accelerator. Kay’s missing frame is stronger: media tools are not just for making artifacts; they are environments for thinking. Add the idea that Sean’s creative automation should become a **small, user-modifiable medium**: prompts, presets, export routines, variants, and constraints as manipulable primitives.  
   This unlocks an **agentic creative-tool spec** or **interactive demo** where the valuable thing is not a motion graphic, but a reusable “job-hunt creative cockpit” for producing tailored proof-of-work assets.

3. **Add “quality gates over generation” anchored on Donald Norman’s _The Design of Everyday Things_.**  
   Exemplifier: Don Norman, [_The Design of Everyday Things_](https://jnd.org/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded-edition/).  
   The article assumes more generated motion equals better interview material. Norman gives the contradicting frame: tools fail when feedback, constraints, affordances, and error recovery are weak. Add a “creative automation QA loop”: brand-fit check, legibility check, export-size check, motion-distraction check, recruiter-skim check.  
   This unlocks a **runbook/eval harness** for portfolio automation: “Generate 12 variants, reject 9 by explicit criteria, ship 3.” That lets Sean show senior product judgment instead of mere AI fluency.
