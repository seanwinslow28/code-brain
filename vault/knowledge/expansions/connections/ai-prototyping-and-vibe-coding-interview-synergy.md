## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[ai-prototyping-and-vibe-coding-interview-synergy]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “specimen interview” mode anchored on Michael Polanyi’s _The Tacit Dimension_**

   **What to add:** A missing facet: interviews do not just test output quality; they test whether the candidate can make tacit judgment visible under pressure. Add a “specimen interview” pattern: Sean brings a small working prototype, then narrates the invisible moves behind it: what he noticed, what he ignored, what he constrained, what he delegated to the model, and where he overrode it.

   **Exemplar:** Michael Polanyi, _The Tacit Dimension_ (1966), especially the idea that “we know more than we can tell.”

   **Unlocks:** This turns the concept from “use AI tools in interviews” into a **live judgment-demonstration format**. Artifact: a reusable interview runbook titled “How I Use AI Without Outsourcing Judgment,” with a 10-minute demo script, decision log, and red-team section. Current failure mode: Sean sounds like he is showing tool fluency; this lets him show professional taste.

2. **Add “pairing etiquette with a non-human pair” anchored on Llewellyn Falco + Woody Zuill’s Strong-Style Pairing**

   **What to add:** A contradicting framework: vibe-coding is often framed as solo speed plus narration, but strong-style pairing says, “for an idea to go from your head into the computer, it must go through someone else’s hands.” Recast the LLM as the “hands,” while Sean remains the navigator responsible for intent, decomposition, and acceptance.

   **Exemplar:** Llewellyn Falco and Woody Zuill, “Strong-Style Pairing” talks/workshops; also Zuill’s mob programming writings and talks.

   **Unlocks:** This gives Sean a cleaner interview posture: not “I asked Cursor to build it,” but “I navigated; the model drove; here is how I controlled the loop.” Artifact: an **AI-pairing interview playbook** with roles, allowed delegation, checkpoint prompts, and phrases for explaining model use without sounding dependent. Current failure mode: generic “AI fluency”; this produces a defensible collaboration model.

3. **Add “prototype as boundary object” anchored on Susan Leigh Star and James Griesemer’s boundary-object paper**

   **What to add:** The concept treats prototypes as proof of coding ability. Missing: prototypes in PM and agentic-engineering interviews are often boundary objects: artifacts that let product, engineering, design, and hiring managers negotiate meaning without sharing the same mental model. Add a “boundary-object prototype” mode: every demo must include what each audience can inspect.

   **Exemplar:** Susan Leigh Star and James R. Griesemer, “Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects” (1989).

   **Unlocks:** This shifts Sean from candidate-as-builder to candidate-as-cross-functional-translator. Artifact: a **portfolio one-pager template** for each prototype: user promise, system behavior, agent boundaries, failure cases, PM tradeoffs, and next implementation bet. Current failure mode: the article only reaches “prototype link for take-home”; this reaches “prototype as organizational argument.”
