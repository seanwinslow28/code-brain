## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[agentic-engineering-signal-in-product-development]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “commander’s intent / backbrief mode”**
   - **Anchor:** Stephen Bungay, *The Art of Action*.
   - **What to add:** Treat intent engineering less like a richer prompt spec and more like a command system: intent, constraints, delegated freedom, and backbriefs where agents restate what they believe matters before acting.
   - **Unlocks:** A stronger **agent spec / portfolio demo**: “How I turn ambiguous product goals into executable agent intent.” Current concept says Sean blends PM and automation; this would show a named operating doctrine for doing it.

2. **Add “levels of automation” as the missing autonomy ladder**
   - **Anchor:** Parasuraman, Sheridan, and Wickens, “A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation,” *IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics*, 2000.
   - **What to add:** Classify agents by what they automate: information acquisition, analysis, decision selection, or action execution. Then assign autonomy levels per risk surface.
   - **Unlocks:** A concrete **runbook / governance artifact** for Sean’s fleet: which agents may suggest, decide, write, commit, notify, or escalate. This moves the concept from “agentic signal” to an actual decision framework for product teams adopting agents.

3. **Add “plans are resources, not instructions” as the contradiction**
   - **Anchor:** Lucy Suchman, *Plans and Situated Actions*.
   - **What to add:** A situated-action critique of intent engineering: specs do not determine behavior; agents act inside messy local context, tool failures, stale data, permissions, and hidden user expectations. Add a “situated drift” section: where agents correctly follow intent but still produce bad product outcomes.
   - **Unlocks:** A sharper **Substack essay or postmortem template**: “Why agent specs fail even when the prompt is good.” This gives Sean a more original voice than generic agent optimism and lets him critique his own fleet with intellectual teeth.
