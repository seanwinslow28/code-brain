## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[automation-and-daily-routine-interdependence]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “repair loop” mode, not “efficiency” mode**

Anchor it on Donald Schön’s *The Reflective Practitioner*, especially reflection-in-action. The missing idea is that routines are not just throughput machinery; they are instruments for noticing breakdowns, reframing the situation, and changing the operator.

Sentence pattern to add: “When the daily note fails, the question is not ‘how do I restore automation?’ but ‘what did the failure reveal about the job-hunt model I was implicitly running?’”

This unlocks a **job-hunt operating review artifact**: a weekly “breakdown log” where failed feeds, empty fits, stale notes, or ignored tasks become evidence about strategy quality. Right now the concept can only say “automation improves efficiency.” Schön lets it ask whether the routine is teaching Sean anything.

2. **Add “situated action” as a contradiction to planned workflow fantasy**

Anchor it on Lucy Suchman’s *Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication*. The missing contradiction: daily routines do not execute clean plans; they collide with contingent realities. “0 new fits” is not just a data point. It may mean feed decay, market mismatch, bad role taxonomy, emotional avoidance, or a search space that needs redesign.

Sentence pattern to add: “The daily note is not the plan; it is a coordination surface between yesterday’s assumptions and today’s discovered situation.”

This unlocks a **portfolio one-pager or Substack essay** on “agentic routines as coordination surfaces,” which is much stronger than another automation writeup. It gives Sean language for AI-PM interviews: agents don’t replace product judgment; they expose where product judgment must update.

3. **Add “calm technology / peripheral awareness” to define what the routine should not demand**

Anchor it on Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown’s essay *The Coming Age of Calm Technology*. The missing facet is attention design. A daily automation loop can become another dashboard that asks to be managed. The useful question is: what should stay in the periphery until it matters?

Sentence pattern to add: “A good daily routine should move job-search state into peripheral awareness, surfacing only exceptions: feed silence, neglected follow-ups, decaying portfolio freshness, or strategy drift.”

This unlocks an **agent spec or runbook** for the Daily Driver: normal state stays quiet; anomaly state escalates. It would let Sean define notification thresholds, exception classes, and escalation rules instead of producing daily summaries that merely prove the machine ran.
