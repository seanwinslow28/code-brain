## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[agentic-engineering-signal-ai-fluency-and-portfolio-projects-for-interview-prep]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “demonstration over declaration” as the missing signal standard.**  
   Anchor it on Bret Victor’s essay **“Inventing on Principle”** and demo talk of the same name. The missing idea is not “show AI fluency,” but “make the evaluator watch the principle operate.” Sentence pattern: *“I believe agents should X, so here is a working system where X is visible in the trace, failure mode, and recovery path.”*  
   This unlocks a **portfolio one-pager + live demo script** where Code-Brain is not described as a large agent fleet, but staged as an inspectable argument: nightly loop, manifest, critic output, lint gate, cost cap, and a before/after concept diff. Current concept says “build portfolio projects”; Victor gives Sean the stronger bar: the artifact must embody the claim.

2. **Add “capability maturity model for agent work” to replace vague AI-fluency signaling.**  
   Anchor it on Watts Humphrey’s **“Managing the Software Process”** and the **Capability Maturity Model** lineage, but translate it into agentic engineering levels: ad hoc prompting → repeatable workflows → measured agent traces → managed evals → optimizing governance loops.  
   This unlocks a **recruiter-facing rubric / interview artifact**: “Agentic Engineering Signal Levels 1-5.” Sean can place Code-Brain at Level 4 in some dimensions, Level 2-3 in others, and show judgment instead of bravado. The current concept collapses Bolt/v0/Cursor familiarity, MCP usage, and autonomous fleet design into one bucket called “AI Fluency.” A maturity model lets him distinguish toy fluency from operational competence.

3. **Add “negative capability / failure literacy” as a counter-signal to portfolio polish.**  
   Anchor it on John Allspaw’s essay/talk **“The Infinite Hows”** and Sidney Dekker’s **“The Field Guide to Understanding Human Error.”** The missing facet: serious systems people are impressed by what you can say about failure, not just what you shipped. Sentence pattern: *“This system fails in these three ways; here is how I detect, bound, and recover from each.”*  
   This unlocks a **runbook, postmortem, or interview story bank** around real agent incidents: 2026-06-20 headless OAuth 401, fabricated LDR citations, v3.15.0 auto-commit conflicts, MBP-awake dependency, launchd PATH failures. The current concept points Sean toward “visible signs of capability”; failure literacy turns those signs into senior-engineering evidence.
