## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[vibe-coding-interview-ecosystem]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Work-Sample Ecology” anchored on Laszlo Bock’s *Work Rules!* and Schmidt & Hunter’s 1998 paper “The Validity and Utility of Selection Methods in Personnel Psychology”**

   Current concept treats vibe-coding as a format. It is missing the hiring-science frame: structured work samples beat charisma-heavy interviews because they simulate job tasks and reduce interviewer theater.

   Add a section that distinguishes:
   - **performance sample**: “Can Sean ship a working slice under ambiguity?”
   - **collaboration sample**: “Can Sean narrate tradeoffs without performing panic?”
   - **artifact sample**: “Does the repo left behind prove judgment?”

   This unlocks a **vibe-coding interview design rubric**: a one-page scorecard Sean can bring into prep or even publish as “How AI-Native Startups Should Evaluate Agentic Engineers.” Right now the concept says “prepare for the format”; this would let him critique the format and show hiring-system literacy.

2. **Add “Interview as Pairing Protocol” anchored on Llewellyn Falco’s talk/writing on strong-style pairing and Woody Zuill’s mob programming work**

   The missing facet is that vibe-coding is not just coding live; it is a compressed collaboration protocol. Sean’s portfolio may prove he can build alone with agents, but the interview tests whether he can make another human feel usefully included while the machine is in the loop.

   Add a named mode: **strong-style agentic pairing**.

   Sentence pattern:
   > “For the next five minutes, I’ll drive through the agent, but I’ll keep the architectural decisions human-readable: first I’ll state the intent, then the constraint, then the verification signal.”

   This unlocks a **live-interview runbook**: exact narration moves, handoff phrases, when to ask permission before invoking an agent, and when to stop coding to re-align. Current concept risks optimizing for output; this adds the interpersonal choreography that senior interviewers actually score.

3. **Add “Critique of Vibes” anchored on Christopher Alexander’s *Notes on the Synthesis of Form* and Donald Schön’s *The Reflective Practitioner***

   The concept accepts “vibe-coding” too literally. It needs a contradiction: good AI-native work is not vibe, it is reflective conversation with a partially reliable material. Alexander gives the design-problem frame; Schön gives the “reflection-in-action” frame.

   Add this counter-position:
   > “The point is not to vibe faster. The point is to maintain a better conversation with the situation than the agent can maintain alone.”

   This unlocks a **Substack essay and portfolio walkthrough voice** where Sean stops sounding like another AI-tools maximalist and instead frames Code-Brain, 16BitFit, and intent-engineering as evidence of reflective control under uncertainty. Current concept reaches “I use agents in interviews”; this reaches “I know when agentic acceleration becomes epistemic debt.”
