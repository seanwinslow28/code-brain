## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[automation-and-agentic-tooling-synergy]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “promise-to-proof automation” anchored on Michael Polanyi’s *The Tacit Dimension*.**  
   **What to add:** A distinction between automation that executes explicit procedures and agentic systems that preserve tacit judgment. Polanyi’s core problem is that “we know more than we can tell”; your current concept treats Skills + fleets + pipelines as additive capability, but not as a transfer problem between tacit human practice and formalized machine action.  
   **Exemplar:** Michael Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (1966).  
   **Unlock:** A sharper Substack or portfolio essay: **“The Automation Trap: Agents Don’t Replace Judgment Until You Encode the Unspoken Parts.”** Artifact-wise, this lets you produce an **intent-capture runbook** for each automation: what was explicit, what stayed tacit, what failure signals reveal missing judgment. Right now the concept says “skills integrated into fleets increase impact”; Polanyi lets you say which kinds of work cannot safely scale until tacit constraints become observable.

2. **Add “joint cognitive systems” anchored on David Woods & Erik Hollnagel’s *Joint Cognitive Systems: Patterns in Cognitive Systems Engineering*.**  
   **What to add:** Reframe the fleet not as tools executing workflows, but as a human-agent joint system where reliability depends on coordination, observability, handoff, and graceful degradation. Your article has synergy, but not coordination cost. It misses the question: when agents become teammates, what new work does Sean have to do to stay in control?  
   **Exemplar:** David D. Woods and Erik Hollnagel, *Joint Cognitive Systems: Patterns in Cognitive Systems Engineering* (2006).  
   **Unlock:** A concrete **agent fleet observability spec**: baton files, escalation boundaries, confidence displays, “who owns the next move,” and post-incident review templates. This would strengthen Code-Brain beyond “I have many agents” into “I understand the control architecture of semi-autonomous work.” It also gives you hiring language for AI-PM roles: designing the cockpit, not just wiring the automations.

3. **Add “ladder of automation / irony of automation” as the contradicting frame, anchored on Lisanne Bainbridge’s paper “Ironies of Automation.”**  
   **What to add:** A counterweight to the concept’s optimistic integration story: as automation improves, humans get worse positioned to intervene because they lose practice, context, and situational awareness. This is the missing critique. Agent fleets can make Sean more capable day to day while making him more brittle during exceptions.  
   **Exemplar:** Lisanne Bainbridge, “Ironies of Automation” (1983), *Automatica*. Optional adjacent anchor: Parasuraman, Sheridan, and Wickens, “A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation” (2000).  
   **Unlock:** A decision artifact the current concept cannot produce: an **automation level matrix** for Code-Brain. Columns: task, autonomy level, human monitoring burden, skill atrophy risk, intervention trigger, rollback path. This turns the article from “automation plus agents equals leverage” into a usable governance framework: where to automate, where to keep deliberate manual friction, and where Sean should rehearse failure recovery.
