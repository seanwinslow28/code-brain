## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[prj-job-hunt-2026]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Search-Firm Operating System” as a missing job-hunt lens**

   Anchor it on **Steve Dalton, _The 2-Hour Job Search_**.

   The current concept treats `prj-job-hunt-2026` as a personal automation hub: routines, synthesis, daily notes, portfolio refreshes. What is missing is a canonical outbound-search operating model: target list, prioritization, contact sequencing, informational-interview conversion, and follow-up discipline.

   Add a facet like:

   > **Search-Firm Mode** — the job hunt is not a reflection exercise; it is a pipeline business with ranked targets, warm-path discovery, conversion metrics, and weekly pipeline review.

   This would unlock a **job-search pipeline artifact** Sean does not currently have: a one-page “target company operating board” with ranked employers, relationship paths, next action, hypothesis, and stage. It would also make the concept less self-referential. Right now it says “Sean is optimizing his job hunt”; Dalton’s framework would force the question: “Optimizing which funnel?”


2. **Add “Positioning by Category Design,” not just portfolio narration**

   Anchor it on **Al Ries and Jack Trout, _Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind_**, plus the sharper modern variant **Christopher Lochhead, _Play Bigger_**.

   Sean’s concept says the project manages a job search and integrates automation, synthesis, and portfolio work. Missing: a theory of category. Is Sean presenting as AI PM, senior PM, agentic engineer, PM-who-codes, or autonomous-systems product lead? Those are not interchangeable markets.

   Add a facet like:

   > **Category Claim** — every artifact must make one buyer category obvious before it explains the system: “agentic-engineering IC,” “AI-native product operator,” or “autonomous workflow systems builder.”

   This unlocks a **portfolio positioning memo** and **homepage decision framework**. The useful artifact is not another summary of Code-Brain; it is a ruthless category choice: headline, proof stack, disqualifying signals, target roles, and what to omit. Without this, Sean risks sounding impressive but hard to route: “smart AI tools person” instead of “obvious hire for this category.”


3. **Add “Evidence Portfolio / Case-Based Hiring” as the contradiction to credential storytelling**

   Anchor it on **Patrick McKenzie, “Don’t Call Yourself A Programmer”** and **David C. Baker, _The Business of Expertise_**.

   The concept currently frames the job hunt as managed by automation and knowledge synthesis. Missing: the outside buyer does not care that the vault is sophisticated until it reduces hiring risk. The concept needs a facet that converts internal systems into external proof.

   Add a facet like:

   > **Evidence Portfolio Mode** — every claim must collapse into a case: problem, constraint, shipped artifact, judgment call, measurable result, and what Sean would do differently.

   This unlocks a **case-study packet**: 3 to 5 tight artifacts such as “How I built a $0/run nightly knowledge loop,” “How I turned Claude Code into a personal operating system,” and “How 16BitFit uses agents to bridge product, game design, and asset pipelines.” The current concept can produce dashboards and summaries; this facet produces hiring evidence that survives a skeptical staff engineer, head of product, or founder screen.
