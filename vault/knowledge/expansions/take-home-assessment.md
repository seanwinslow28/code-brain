## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[take-home-assessment]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “work-sample contract” as the missing negotiation frame**

   Anchor it on **Mike Monteiro, _Design Is a Job_**, especially his argument that professional work needs explicit terms, scope, and payment norms. The concept currently treats take-homes as an assessment format; it misses the candidate-side contract logic.

   Add a section like: “A take-home is not just a task; it is a proposed work contract with unclear compensation, IP ownership, time budget, evaluation rubric, and feedback obligation.”

   This unlocks a **candidate decision runbook** Sean can actually use before accepting an assessment: when to say yes, when to cap time, when to request rubric/payment, when to offer a portfolio substitute. It also gives him a sharper Substack angle: “The Take-Home Is a Contract, Not a Vibe Check.”


2. **Add “signal extraction under asymmetric information”**

   Anchor it on **Michael Spence, “Job Market Signaling” (1973)**. The missing facet is that take-homes are not just evaluations of ability; they are costly signals in a market where companies and candidates both lack information.

   Add: “The take-home’s value depends on whether the costly signal is diagnostic. A 6-hour polished demo may signal free time, compliance, and taste more than job performance. A constrained 90-minute trace may signal judgment better.”

   This unlocks a **rubric for judging the assessment itself**: is this task measuring PM/product judgment, agentic engineering skill, speed under ambiguity, polish, or unpaid labor tolerance? Sean could produce an **interview debrief template** that ranks each take-home by diagnosticity, cost, and transferability to his portfolio.


3. **Add “artifact-first substitution strategy”**

   Anchor it on **Patrick McKenzie, “Don’t Call Yourself A Programmer, And Other Career Advice”**. McKenzie’s core move is market positioning through legible artifacts, not relying on standard hiring filters to understand you.

   Add: “For candidates with unusual leverage, the optimal response may not be completing the assigned take-home exactly. It may be substituting a stronger public artifact: a teardown, design memo, working demo, or agent trace that proves the same ability with reusable upside.”

   This unlocks Sean’s missing **portfolio conversion pattern**: every take-home becomes either a shipped demo, a public teardown, a private memo, or a reusable agent spec. Current concept stops at “companies use this format.” This addition turns it into a strategy: “How do I make the assessment compound even if they say no?”
