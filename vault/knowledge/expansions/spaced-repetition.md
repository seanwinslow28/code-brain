## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[spaced-repetition]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “desirable difficulty” as the missing counterweight to easy recall.**  
   Anchor it on Robert A. Bjork and Elizabeth L. Bjork’s paper/chapter **“Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning.”**  
   Current concept says “review at increasing intervals,” which risks turning Sean’s interview prep into recognition fluency: seeing familiar AI-PM vocabulary and mistaking it for usable command. Add the rule: spaced repetition only counts when retrieval is effortful enough to expose a gap.  
   Sentence pattern to add: “If a card feels fluent, make it harder: remove cues, change context, force production, or interleave it with a neighboring concept.”  
   This unlocks an **interview-prep runbook** where Sean is not just rereading `eval-vocab.md`, but escalating cards from definition → comparison → concrete story → adversarial follow-up. It also gives his agents a critique criterion: “Did this review create productive struggle, or just familiarity?”

2. **Add “retrieval practice beats review” as the core mechanism.**  
   Anchor it on Henry L. Roediger III and Jeffrey D. Karpicke’s paper **“Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention.”**  
   The missing facet is that spaced repetition is not primarily about timing. Timing helps, but the engine is retrieval. Sean’s current examples are too passive: “Repeating” and “rewriting thin examples” could mean exposure, not recall.  
   Sentence pattern to add: “Every repetition must require closed-book production before feedback: answer first, inspect second, revise third.”  
   This unlocks an **executable agent spec** for interview drills: an agent can prompt Sean with a scenario, hide the source, score the answer against the canon, then write a delta note. The artifact becomes a retrieval loop, not a study checklist. It would also improve the job-hunt vault by generating evidence of readiness: missed distinctions, repaired examples, and answer evolution over time.

3. **Add “knowledge-in-pieces / transfer failure” to contradict the idea that retained vocabulary equals usable judgment.**  
   Anchor it on Andrea A. diSessa’s book **“Changing Minds: Computers, Learning, and Literacy.”**  
   The current concept assumes the unit being retained is “information.” For Sean, the real unit is often a judgment move: when to mention evals, when to challenge a metric, when to describe agent autonomy boundaries, when to translate PM language into engineering constraints. diSessa’s frame helps name why someone can remember terms and still fail to transfer them under pressure.  
   Sentence pattern to add: “Track not just whether I remember the concept, but which situation cues make me use it.”  
   This unlocks a **portfolio/interview decision matrix**: concepts become indexed by trigger conditions, not just definitions. Example: “If interviewer asks about AI reliability, route to eval design + stop rules; if they ask about team adoption, route to intent charter + autonomy boundaries.” That produces a stronger artifact than the current note: a situational playbook for AI-PM and agentic-engineering interviews.
