## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[cultural-fit-assessment]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Mutual Fit Due Diligence,” not cultural fit**

   Anchor it on **Edgar H. Schein, _Organizational Culture and Leadership_**, especially the artifacts / espoused values / basic assumptions model.

   Current concept frames fit as “do I align with them?” That is too candidate-compliance-coded. Add a reverse-interview diagnostic: distinguish visible startup artifacts from actual operating assumptions.

   Sentence pattern to add:

   > Cultural fit is not whether I match the company’s stated values; it is whether my working assumptions survive contact with the company’s real decision habits.

   This unlocks a **company-fit scorecard / interview runbook** Sean can actually use: questions like “What gets rewarded when speed and quality conflict?” or “Who can stop a bad launch?” It turns the concept from vibes into an artifact for choosing between AI-PM roles, agentic-engineering IC roles, and founder-led chaos shops.

2. **Add “Culture Add” as a contradiction to “fit”**

   Anchor it on **Patty McCord, _Powerful: Building a Culture of Freedom and Responsibility_**, plus the **Netflix Culture Deck** as the canonical operating artifact.

   “Cultural fit” is often a sanitizing phrase for sameness. Sean’s edge is not blending in; it is bringing a working agent fleet, local-model cost discipline, critique loops, and PM judgment into teams that may only talk about “AI-native” work. Add a facet that asks: “What cultural capability do I increase here?”

   Sentence pattern to add:

   > The question is not only whether I fit the culture, but whether my operating system increases the culture’s range.

   This unlocks a **portfolio one-pager / interview narrative** where Sean names his additive value: “I bring agent observability, critique infrastructure, and production-minded autonomy boundaries to teams that are still using agents as demos.” Without this, the concept risks training him to sound agreeable instead of differentiated.

3. **Add “Founder Mode / Manager Mode Fit” as a startup-specific discriminator**

   Anchor it on **Paul Graham, “Founder Mode”** and contrast it with **Andy Grove, _High Output Management_**.

   AI-native startups often claim they want autonomy, but some are founder-mode shops where decisions route through taste, urgency, and direct intervention. Others want Grove-style managerial leverage: systems, metrics, one-on-ones, operating cadence. Sean needs to know which game he is entering.

   Sentence pattern to add:

   > Cultural fit in an AI-native company is partly a fit with its control topology: founder taste, managerial leverage, or agent-mediated operating rhythm.

   This unlocks a **decision memo / role-risk assessment** for interviews: “Can I operate under founder taste without becoming a prompt monkey?” “Does this team value systems builders or heroic closers?” “Will agentic-engineering work be treated as infrastructure or as novelty?” Current concept cannot distinguish a good AI startup from a charismatic, exhausting one.
