## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[ciia-keyword-sanity-checks]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “ATS Reality Check” anchored on Jon Shields’ Jobscan writing**
   - **What to add:** A distinction between *keyword matching as table-stakes retrieval* and *resume persuasion as evidence design*. Current concept treats “JD-keyword mirror” as if matching language is the whole game. Add a rule: keywords are only valid when attached to proof verbs, scope, and outcome.
   - **Exemplar:** Jon Shields, Jobscan essay “How to Write an ATS Resume” / Jobscan’s ATS optimization guides.
   - **Unlocks:** A **resume QA runbook** that separates `ATS-pass`, `recruiter-skim`, and `hiring-manager-belief`. Sean could ship a one-page “resume validation protocol” instead of another generic keyword checklist. Sentence pattern: “Mirror the noun, prove the verb, quantify the consequence.”

2. **Add “Confidentiality Boundary Redaction” anchored on Amy Gallo’s HBR piece on talking about confidential work**
   - **What to add:** CIIA should not just mean “don’t leak.” It needs a reusable transformation pattern for public artifacts: replace employer-sensitive nouns with capability claims, replace private metrics with ranges, replace internal architecture with decision shape.
   - **Exemplar:** Amy Gallo, Harvard Business Review, “How to Talk About Your Work Without Revealing Confidential Information.”
   - **Unlocks:** A **portfolio-safe artifact pattern** for Sean’s public job-hunt materials. This lets him show The Block and Code-Brain sophistication without sounding evasive or over-redacted. Example pattern: “I cannot name SYSTEM, but I can name the decision: reduced reviewer load by moving subjective judgment from hook to subagent.”

3. **Add “Evidence-Centered Resume Claims” anchored on Mislevy, Steinberg, and Almond’s Evidence-Centered Design**
   - **What to add:** Treat each resume bullet as an assessment claim: claim → evidence → task/context → inference. Current concept checks whether keywords appear; it does not check whether the bullet gives the reader enough evidence to infer seniority.
   - **Exemplar:** Robert Mislevy, Linda Steinberg, and Russell Almond, “Evidence-Centered Assessment Design” / *Evidence-Centered Design for Educational Assessment*.
   - **Unlocks:** A **resume/case-study scoring rubric** for AI-PM and agentic-engineering roles. It would catch bullets that are keyword-rich but epistemically weak. Sentence pattern: “If the claim is `agentic engineering`, the observable evidence must be `agent boundary designed`, `failure mode handled`, `cost/safety constraint enforced`, and `artifact shipped`.”
