## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[friction-killing-wedge]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “switching-forces mode,” not just friction naming.**  
   Anchor it on Bob Moesta’s *Demand-Side Sales 101* and Christensen et al.’s *Competing Against Luck* / JTBD. Current concept says “kill one friction,” but not **why someone finally switches now**. Add pattern: “User abandons CURRENT HABIT when push-of-situation + pull-of-new-solution beats anxiety-of-switching + habit-of-present.” This unlocks a **wedge interview script** and **portfolio case study** where Sean can show causal demand, not just sharper positioning.

2. **Add “commodity drift map.”**  
   Anchor it on Simon Wardley’s *An Introduction to Wardley ‘Value Chain’ Mapping* / Wardley Maps. The missing contradiction: “the thing nobody else does” is temporary. A wedge is defensible only if Sean knows which components are user-visible and still novel, versus hidden commodities. Add pattern: “Map the killed friction as a value-chain node; defend only what is high-visibility and left-of-market.” This unlocks a **strategy one-pager** for Code-Brain / intent-engineering that explains what to productize, what to open-source, and what to treat as table stakes.

3. **Add “assumption ledger before artifact.”**  
   Anchor it on Rita McGrath and Ian MacMillan’s 1995 HBR article *Discovery-Driven Planning*. Current concept risks becoming a slogan generator: “works-with-no-corpus,” “learns-from-rejection,” etc. Add pattern: “For each wedge claim, name the make-or-break assumption, cheapest test, pass/fail threshold, and next funding step.” This unlocks an **executable validation runbook**: five tiny demos or Substack experiments that prove whether the wedge is real before Sean turns it into a product narrative.
