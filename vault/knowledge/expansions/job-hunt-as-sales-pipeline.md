## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[job-hunt-as-sales-pipeline]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “constraint-first pipeline” mode.** A pipeline is not a feed; it is a sequence of conversions with one limiting stage. Anchor this on Eliyahu Goldratt’s *The Goal* and its [Five Focusing Steps](https://northriverpress.com/5fs-lp/): identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate other activity, elevate it, then repeat. Track `qualified lead → warm contact → recruiter screen → hiring manager → final → offer`, including conversion rate, cycle time, and work-in-progress. Sentence pattern: “The fleet is healthy only if it increases throughput at the current constraint.” This would unlock a **weekly job-hunt operating review**, a bottleneck dashboard, and an agent spec that reallocates effort—portfolio production when screens are scarce, interview drills when finals fail—instead of celebrating job-feed uptime.

2. **Add “costly-signal portfolio” mode.** The sales metaphor assumes persuasion drives conversion; Michael Spence’s [“Job Market Signaling”](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/87/3/355/1909092) says hiring is also an asymmetric-information problem. Generic applications, AI-generated prose, and sprawling tool inventories are cheap signals because weak candidates can imitate them. Sean needs evidence that is difficult to produce without the claimed capability: a replayable agent failure incident, an eval suite with before/after results, a governance demo with audit traces, or a working intent-engineering reference implementation. Sentence pattern: “Do not claim the capability; publish the residue only that capability would leave.” This unlocks **proof-of-work case studies, technical portfolio one-pagers, and interview narratives** that convert Code-Brain from an impressive inventory into credible evidence of senior agentic-engineering judgment.

3. **Replace “keep the network aware” with a “moderately weak-tie activation” model.** Awareness is a broadcast metric, not a referral mechanism. Karthik Rajkumar, Guillaume Saint-Jacques, Iavor Bojinov, Erik Brynjolfsson, and Sinan Aral’s *Science* paper [“A Causal Test of the Strength of Weak Ties”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107999/) found an inverted-U relationship: weaker ties improved job transmission, but only to a point. Add a contact-routing policy that favors former collaborators, second-degree specialists, conference acquaintances, and dormant peers—not intimate contacts or indiscriminate LinkedIn volume. Every outreach should carry a relevant artifact and one bounded ask. This unlocks an **executable networking campaign**: a tie-strength ledger, five-contact weekly experiment, referral-conversion cohorts, and outreach templates such as “I built X because your team faces Y; what assumption would you challenge?”
