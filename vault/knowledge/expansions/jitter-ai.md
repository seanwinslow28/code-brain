## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[jitter-ai]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “end-user programming, not AI magic” as the missing frame.**  
   Anchor it on Bonnie Nardi’s *A Small Matter of Programming: Perspectives on End User Computing* and Alan Cypher’s edited volume *Watch What I Do: Programming by Demonstration*. Jitter AI should not just be filed as “prompt-to-motion effect”; it belongs in the lineage of tools where non-programmers create reusable computational behavior.

   Unlock: a sharper Substack essay or portfolio one-pager: **“Prompted Tools Are Macros With Taste.”** Sean can argue that Jitter AI’s real product move is not generation, but compressing motion-design scripting into an end-user programming interface. That gives him a PM/agentic-engineering bridge: how to design authorable tools, presets, and guardrails for creative operators.

2. **Add “software instrument design” as the creative-tool facet.**  
   Anchor it on Bret Victor’s talk *Inventing on Principle* and essay *Magic Ink*. The missing distinction: Jitter AI is not just producing animations; it is generating little instruments for seeing, manipulating, and iterating on motion. Victor’s lens asks whether the tool preserves immediacy, feedback, and direct manipulation, or hides the medium behind language.

   Unlock: an executable critique artifact: **a Jitter AI evaluation rubric** for creative AI tools. Columns could include immediacy, parameter legibility, reversibility, inspectability, composability, and whether the generated tool teaches the user anything about motion. This would let Sean critique Jitter, Adobe Firefly-style tools, Remotion workflows, and his own 16BitFit asset pipeline with one reusable frame.

3. **Add “promptable effect as procedural texture / shader culture.”**  
   Anchor it on Patricio Gonzalez Vivo and Jen Lowe’s *The Book of Shaders*, plus Casey Reas and Ben Fry’s *Processing: A Programming Handbook for Visual Designers and Artists*. The concept currently says “ripple, glitch, dithering, pixelated effect” as examples, but misses that these are part of a named procedural-graphics tradition: small parameterized systems that generate visual behavior.

   Unlock: a concrete demo genre Sean does not yet get from this note: **a motion-effect cookbook or agent spec** where each Jitter-style prompt maps to a procedural primitive: noise, displacement, feedback, quantization, temporal easing, palette reduction, masking. For 16BitFit, this becomes more than “AI-assisted animation”; it becomes a bridge from natural-language creative tools to deterministic pixel-art post-processing and reusable Remotion/Jitter effect recipes.
