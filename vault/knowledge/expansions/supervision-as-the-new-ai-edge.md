## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[supervision-as-the-new-ai-edge]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “commander’s intent” as the missing supervision primitive.**  
   Anchor it on Stephen Bungay’s *The Art of Action* and the military doctrine of “commander’s intent,” not generic “alignment.” The current concept says supervision is valuable, but it does not yet distinguish **telling agents what to do** from **specifying the outcome, constraints, and initiative boundary when plans break**.

   Sentence pattern to add: “The supervisor’s job is not to approve every move; it is to state intent clearly enough that useful initiative survives contact with reality.”

   This unlocks a sharper **agent spec / portfolio governance runbook**: intent statement, boundaries, delegated decisions, exception triggers, review cadence. It would make Sean’s “intent engineering” work feel less like a coined category and more like a lineage with an older operational doctrine behind it.

2. **Add “management by exception” as the economic model of agent supervision.**  
   Anchor it on Stafford Beer’s *Brain of the Firm*, especially viable system thinking and exception-based control. The concept currently frames supervision as judgment over outputs, but it is missing the cybernetic question: **what must be escalated, and what should disappear into routine?**

   Sentence pattern to add: “A mature agent fleet should not make the human busier; it should compress attention onto exceptions worth human judgment.”

   This unlocks a concrete **fleet observability artifact** Sean can ship: dashboards and review loops organized by variance, anomaly, cost drift, stale outputs, contradiction, and unresolved escalations. It also gives him a decision rule for his own fleet: which agents deserve autonomy, which need tighter gates, and which are creating supervision debt instead of leverage.

3. **Add “streetlight effect / automation bias” as the contradiction to supervision optimism.**  
   Anchor it on Lisanne Bainbridge’s paper “Ironies of Automation” and Moshe Bar-Hillel’s work on the base-rate fallacy, rather than vague “AI risks.” The current concept assumes senior value moves cleanly into curation and verification. Bainbridge complicates that: the more automation handles routine work, the less practiced the human becomes at intervening when it fails.

   Sentence pattern to add: “The danger is not that supervisors stop building; it is that they supervise systems whose failure modes they can no longer personally reproduce.”

   This unlocks a stronger **Substack essay or interview answer**: “The New Non-Builder Is the Person Who Can’t Audit the Machine.” It also gives Sean a portfolio demo angle: not just agents that produce artifacts, but agents with rehearsal tests, adversarial review, trace inspection, rollback paths, and human skill-maintenance loops.
