## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[complexity-override]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “probe-sense-respond instrumentation,” not just “override.”**  
   Anchor it on Dave Snowden & Mary Boone, **“A Leader’s Framework for Decision Making”** (*Harvard Business Review*, 2007).

   Current concept names Cynefin, but only as a permission slip to suspend linear routing. Missing piece: in complex domains, the move is not “turn constraints off,” it is **run bounded probes that make the system disclose information**.

   Sentence pattern to add:  
   > Complexity Override does not relax discipline; it changes the unit of discipline from routing compliance to probe design: safe-to-fail experiment, observable signal, amplification/dampening rule.

   This unlocks a **runbook / agent spec** Sean cannot currently produce: a “Complexity Override Protocol” where a research topic triggers 3-5 parallel probes, each with a hypothesis, timebox, expected signal, and kill/amplify rule. Without this, the concept risks becoming “messy topic gets special handling,” which is too vague to operationalize.

2. **Add “premature convergence as failure mode.”**  
   Anchor it on Donald A. Schön, **The Reflective Practitioner** (1983), especially reflection-in-action.

   The concept correctly worries about premature synthesis, but it frames the problem structurally: routing rules force bad compression. Schön adds the missing practitioner lens: in uncertain domains, expertise often consists of **having a conversation with the material situation** rather than applying a known procedure.

   Sentence pattern to add:  
   > Complexity Override protects the reflective phase where the agent is not yet solving the problem, but learning what kind of problem it is by acting, observing back-talk, and reframing.

   This unlocks a **Substack essay / portfolio one-pager** Sean cannot currently write well: “Why My Agent Fleet Needs Reflection, Not Just Routing.” It would let him explain agentic-engineering judgment in human terms, rather than sounding like another orchestration diagram. It also gives him a sharper critique of his own fleet: the system may be good at execution and weak at reframing.

3. **Add “divergent/convergent gates” as the missing lifecycle.**  
   Anchor it on Alex F. Osborn, **Applied Imagination** (1953), plus the Design Council’s **Double Diamond** model.

   “Complexity Override” currently says: suspend single-shape constraints, probe, then synthesize. Missing is the gate logic between **divergence** and **convergence**. Without that, override mode can sprawl forever or collapse back into synthesis too early.

   Sentence pattern to add:  
   > Override mode has two explicit gates: divergence is allowed until the system has produced materially different frames; convergence is allowed only after those frames can be compared by decision-relevant criteria.

   This unlocks an **executable demo / evaluation harness**: feed the same messy research prompt into normal routing vs. Complexity Override, then score whether the override produced multiple distinct frames before recommending one. That artifact would make the concept demonstrable to AI-PM or agentic-engineering audiences, instead of remaining an internal vocabulary note.
