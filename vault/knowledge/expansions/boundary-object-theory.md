## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[boundary-object-theory]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Interpretive Flexibility vs. Infrastructure”**
   - **What to add:** Boundary objects are not just ambiguous documents; they are maintained by infrastructure, routines, repositories, classifications, and work practices. Add a facet that asks: *what social or technical infrastructure keeps this artifact usable despite divergent meanings?*
   - **Anchor:** Susan Leigh Star and Geoffrey C. Bowker, **_Sorting Things Out: Classification and Its Consequences_**.
   - **Unlock for Sean:** This turns the concept from “AI summaries erase productive ambiguity” into an **agent-system design pattern**: preserve not only the PRD text, but the classification system around it. Artifact to ship: a **Boundary Object Preservation Runbook** for agents that summarizes PRDs, RFCs, roadmaps, or metric docs while retaining owner roles, unresolved tensions, glossary variants, and decision provenance.

2. **Add “Trading Zone” as the Contradicting Frame**
   - **What to add:** Boundary objects allow coordination without shared meaning; **trading zones** explain cases where groups develop a partial shared language because coordination pressure is high enough. This is the missing counterweight: sometimes ambiguity should be preserved, sometimes it should be converted into a pidgin/creole/shared protocol.
   - **Anchor:** Peter Galison, **_Image and Logic: A Material Culture of Microphysics_**, especially his account of physicists, engineers, and instrument-makers forming trading zones.
   - **Unlock for Sean:** This gives him a decision rule the current concept lacks: **preserve ambiguity or force translation?** Artifact to ship: an **AI-PM decision matrix** for agent workflows:
     - boundary-object mode for strategy docs, roadmaps, PRDs before alignment;
     - trading-zone mode for API contracts, launch checklists, incident runbooks, eval specs.
     This would sharpen his intent-engineering work because it distinguishes “semantic pluralism is useful” from “semantic pluralism is now operational risk.”

3. **Add “Documents as Coordination Mechanisms, Not Knowledge Containers”**
   - **What to add:** A document’s job is often not to represent truth, but to coordinate action under uncertainty. Add a mode that treats PRDs, RFCs, roadmaps, and architecture diagrams as **performative artifacts**: they create commitments, permissions, dependencies, and escalation paths.
   - **Anchor:** Lucy Suchman, **_Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication_**.
   - **Unlock for Sean:** This gives him a stronger critique of AI summarization: summaries fail not only because they remove ambiguity, but because they mistake plans for instructions. Artifact to ship: a **Substack essay or portfolio one-pager** titled something like “AI Summaries Destroy the Work Documents Actually Do,” with a concrete agent spec: summarize the commitments, open contingencies, affected roles, and situated exceptions instead of extracting “key points.”
