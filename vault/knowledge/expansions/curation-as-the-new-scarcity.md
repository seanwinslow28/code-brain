## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[curation-as-the-new-scarcity]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “costly signaling curation,” not just trust curation**

   Anchor it on Michael Spence’s paper **“Job Market Signaling”** and Rory Sutherland’s book **Alchemy**.

   Current concept says curation matters because users lack attention. Missing facet: users also need to know the curator has *skin in the game*. A list of “best MCP servers” is cheap. A curator who publishes failures, rejected tools, eval traces, before/after workflows, and decision criteria is emitting costly signals.

   Add sentence pattern: “A curator is trusted less by what they recommend than by what they visibly refuse, test, and risk being wrong about.”

   This unlocks a **portfolio artifact** Sean does not yet have: a “curation ledger” for Code-Brain or VoicePrint showing accepted/rejected tools, tests run, failure notes, and why each tool earned cognitive load. That is stronger than another recommendation essay because it demonstrates judgment as labor.

2. **Add “the editor as product surface”**

   Anchor it on Tina Brown’s **The Vanity Fair Diaries** and Harold Ross / William Shawn as described in Ben Yagoda’s **About Town: The New Yorker and the World It Made**.

   The concept frames intermediaries as validators. Missing facet: great curators are not neutral filters; they create a recognizable editorial world. The scarcity is not only “which tools are good?” but “which tools belong in *this taste system*?” The New Yorker was not valuable because it summarized culture efficiently. It made selection feel like membership in a sensibility.

   Add sentence pattern: “Curation becomes defensible when the filter has a house style: exclusions feel authored, not merely ranked.”

   This unlocks a **Substack series format** Sean cannot reach with the current concept: “The Code-Brain Canon,” where tools, agents, workflows, and references are reviewed through a consistent editorial rubric. That moves him away from generic AI-tool punditry and toward a named curatorial voice: PM judgment plus builder receipts plus gonzo distrust of hype.

3. **Add “anti-curation: legibility can destroy discovery”**

   Anchor it on James C. Scott’s **Seeing Like a State** and Venkatesh Rao’s essay **“A Big Little Idea Called Legibility.”**

   Current concept assumes curation improves abundance by reducing noise. Missing contradiction: aggressive curation can flatten weirdness into legible categories, killing the serendipity that made the ecosystem valuable. A perfect ranked directory often becomes a bureaucratic map of yesterday’s consensus.

   Add sentence pattern: “The curator’s failure mode is not bad taste; it is over-legibility, where the map becomes clean enough to erase the frontier.”

   This unlocks an **agent spec / eval runbook**: a curation agent that must preserve “productive weirdness.” Instead of ranking tools only by utility, it would tag items as canonical, risky, weird-but-promising, obsolete, or personally resonant. That gives Sean a sharper artifact than “trusted filter”: a discovery system that balances adoption confidence with frontier optionality.
