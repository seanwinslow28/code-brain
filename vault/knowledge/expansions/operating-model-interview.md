## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[operating-model-interview]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add a “work-system interview protocol,” not just an evaluation label.**  
   Anchor it on Lucy Suchman’s *Plans and Situated Actions* and Edgar Schein’s *Humble Inquiry*. The missing move is: interview the operating model as a situated practice, not as a dashboard of artifacts.

   Sentence pattern to add: “When the plan says X, what actually happens at 8:37 AM when attention, tools, and incentives collide?”

   This unlocks a **field-interview artifact** Sean can ship: a repeatable `operating-model-interview.md` runbook with prompts, failure probes, and transcript tags. Right now the concept says “structured evaluation,” but it does not define the interrogation method. Suchman gives him the anti-plan critique; Schein gives him the question discipline.

2. **Add “double-loop learning” as the contradiction engine.**  
   Anchor it on Chris Argyris and Donald Schön’s *Organizational Learning: A Theory of Action Perspective*. The current concept evaluates whether the Daily-driver helps the job hunt; it does not ask whether the job-hunt operating model is optimizing the wrong premise.

   Sentence pattern to add: “If this agent succeeds, what governing variable remains unchallenged?”

   This unlocks a **decision review / agent critique template** where the fleet distinguishes:
   - single-loop: “Did the Daily-driver execute the morning plan?”
   - double-loop: “Is the morning plan still the right governing theory?”
   - deutero-learning: “Is Sean getting better at redesigning the system?”

   That gives Sean a sharper portfolio artifact: “How I Use Agents to Challenge My Own Operating Assumptions,” instead of another personal productivity note.

3. **Add “after-action review plus pre-mortem” as the temporal structure.**  
   Anchor it on Gary Klein’s “Performing a Project Premortem” in *Harvard Business Review* and the U.S. Army’s *A Leader’s Guide to After-Action Reviews*. The concept currently has no time axis: before the day, during the day, after the day, after the week.

   Sentence pattern to add: “Assume this week’s job-hunt operating model failed by Friday; what caused it, what signal would appear by Tuesday, and what intervention would still be cheap?”

   This unlocks a **daily/weekly operating cadence artifact**:
   - Monday premortem: forecast failure modes.
   - Daily Driver: observe live drift.
   - Friday AAR: compare intent, action, result, lesson.
   - Fleet critic: convert lessons into changed rules.

   This would let Sean produce an executable **agent spec** for the Daily-driver that critiques plans before executing them, instead of merely summarizing overnight context and today’s tasks.
