## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[boundary-setting-as-political-act]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

### 1. Add “boundary critique,” not merely boundary selection

Anchor it in Werner Ulrich’s *Critical Heuristics of Social Planning* and Ulrich and Martin Reynolds’s “[Critical Systems Heuristics](https://www.wulrich.com/downloads/ulrich_reynolds_2010_prepub.pdf).” CSH supplies twelve paired **is/ought** questions covering beneficiaries, decision-makers, expertise, success measures, and—crucially—people affected but not involved.

Sentence pattern: **“The boundary privileges X as beneficiary, grants Y control, treats Z as expert, and leaves A affected without representation.”**

This would turn “political” from a metaphor into an inspectable distribution of authority. It unlocks an **Intent Charter boundary-audit annex** or **agent-spec review rubric** that can expose whose interests an objective, health metric, stop rule, or escalation policy structurally excludes.

### 2. Add an “excluded-actor response repertoire”

Anchor it in Albert O. Hirschman’s *[Exit, Voice, and Loyalty: Responses to Decline in Firms, Organizations, and States](https://books.google.com/books/about/Exit_Voice_and_Loyalty.html?id=4gcPEAAAQBAJ)*. The Unity example currently treats retaliation as an omitted feedback loop. Hirschman makes the sharper claim: excluded actors possess distinct political responses—**exit**, **voice**, and loyalty-mediated delay—and system design changes the relative cost and effectiveness of each.

Sentence pattern: **“This boundary does not remove the actor; it changes whether their cheapest response is voice, exit, sabotage, or silent degradation.”**

This unlocks an **ecosystem-policy pre-mortem** or **agent-fleet dissent runbook**: for every affected actor, specify available voice channels, observable exit signals, switching costs, and the point at which loyalty becomes concealed failure. That reaches decisions about API pricing, agent deprecation, human override, and contributor governance that the current inside/outside binary cannot.

### 3. Replace “too wide/too narrow” with an executable boundary-adequacy test

Anchor it in John Sterman’s *Business Dynamics*, especially Chapter 21’s **boundary adequacy, extreme-condition, behavior-anomaly, surprise-behavior, and system-improvement tests**, listed in his [MIT faculty book outline](https://mitmgmtfaculty.mit.edu/jsterman/business-dynamics/).

Sentence pattern: **“A variable belongs inside the boundary if adding its feedback structure materially changes the reference behavior or reverses the preferred intervention.”**

This contradicts the article’s currently rhetorical trade-off: breadth is not inherently worse for persuasion, nor narrowness inherently more predictive. Adequacy is purpose-relative and testable. It unlocks an **executable boundary-sensitivity demo**: replay a fleet incident under progressively expanded models—agent logic only, host availability, provider limits, schedule coupling—and record where the diagnosis or recommended control changes. That becomes a strong portfolio artifact because it demonstrates model criticism through code rather than repeating systems-thinking doctrine.
