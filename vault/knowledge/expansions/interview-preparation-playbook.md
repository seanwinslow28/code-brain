## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[interview-preparation-playbook]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “case interview issue-tree mode” anchored on Victor Cheng’s _Case Interview Secrets_**

   The current concept says “phase-based heuristics,” but it does not give Sean a pressure-tested structure for live problem decomposition. Add an interview-prep mode based on Cheng’s hypothesis-driven issue tree: state the objective, split the problem into mutually exclusive buckets, pick the highest-leverage branch, then test assumptions aloud.

   Sentence pattern: “I’d frame this as three possible failure points: user intent, system capability, and operational rollout. My first hypothesis is the constraint sits in X, because Y. I’d test that by Z.”

   This unlocks a **live problem-solving runbook** for AI-PM and agentic-engineering interviews where the interviewer throws an ambiguous product/system question at him. Without it, Sean risks sounding like he has prepared stories; with it, he demonstrates executive decomposition under uncertainty.

2. **Add “interaction repair mode” anchored on Erving Goffman’s _Interaction Ritual_**

   The concept mentions “repair, redirection, and reframing,” but it lacks a sociological grammar for what is actually happening when an interview goes sideways. Goffman’s face-work gives Sean a named lens for preserving competence while acknowledging mismatch, confusion, or pushback.

   Sentence pattern: “Let me correct how I framed that. The stronger version is not that I automated everything; it’s that I learned where automation needs explicit stop rules.”

   This unlocks a **misalignment-response artifact**: a compact table of interviewer signals, likely interpretation, and repair move. Example rows: “skeptical eyebrow after agent fleet claim → sounds like toy automation → answer with launchd schedule, cost cap, failure mode, rollback.” The current concept prepares for ideal execution; Goffman would prepare Sean for social recovery.

3. **Add “performance evidence packet” anchored on Patrick McKenzie’s essay _Don’t Call Yourself A Programmer, And Other Career Advice_**

   Sean’s playbook is optimized for what to say during the interview, but it under-specifies portable proof. McKenzie’s core move is career positioning through business-legible evidence, not identity claims. For Sean, this means translating “I built Code-Brain” into quantified, inspectable artifacts an interviewer can evaluate quickly.

   Sentence pattern: “The artifact I’d show is not the repo; it’s the operating loop: scheduled agents, failure budget, manifests, and the decision records showing how the system improved.”

   This unlocks a **portfolio one-pager / interview leave-behind**: “Agentic Engineering Operating System: 7 agents, $0 nightly loop, local-model routing, critic manifest, failure modes, artifacts produced.” The current concept helps Sean answer well in the room; this addition helps him leave behind evidence that keeps working after the call ends.
