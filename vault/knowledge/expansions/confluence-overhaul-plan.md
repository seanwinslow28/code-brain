## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[confluence-overhaul-plan]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Document Ecology” as the missing unit of analysis**
   - **What to add:** Treat the overhaul as an ecology of document types, not a generic “knowledge base improvement.” Add a taxonomy: policy docs, decision records, runbooks, onboarding paths, project briefs, meeting residue, canonical indexes, and dead-document retirement rules.
   - **Anchor:** Richard Saul Wurman, *Information Anxiety*; Abby Covert, *How to Make Sense of Any Mess*.
   - **Unlocks for Sean:** A **Confluence audit runbook** or **portfolio one-pager** where he can show how he diagnoses knowledge rot by artifact class. Current concept says “improve documentation”; this would let him ship a concrete “docs operating model” with named failure modes: orphaned docs, duplicate truth, ambiguous ownership, stale onboarding, and decision amnesia.

2. **Add “Knowledge-Centered Service” instead of top-down documentation cleanup**
   - **What to add:** KCS mode: docs are created and improved in the flow of work, especially support, PM, engineering, and incident workflows. The key move is: don’t assign documentation as a cleanup project; wire it into demand loops.
   - **Anchor:** Consortium for Service Innovation, *KCS v6 Practices Guide*.
   - **Unlocks for Sean:** An **agent spec** for a Confluence-maintenance agent that watches repeated questions, stale links, unresolved tickets, Slack/meeting residue, and support escalations, then proposes doc updates with provenance. This contradicts the current “overhaul plan” framing, which implies a periodic cleanup. KCS gives him a sharper claim: documentation quality is an operational byproduct, not a documentation-team virtue.

3. **Add “Boundary Object” theory to explain why Confluence docs fail across teams**
   - **What to add:** Docs are not just information containers; they are boundary objects that different teams use differently while pretending they mean the same thing. Add explicit handling for translation artifacts: PRDs, metric definitions, launch checklists, architecture diagrams, roadmap narratives, and glossary entries.
   - **Anchor:** Susan Leigh Star and James R. Griesemer, “Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects” (*Social Studies of Science*, 1989).
   - **Unlocks for Sean:** A **Substack essay** or **AI-PM interview artifact** arguing that AI knowledge systems fail when they summarize documents without modeling who needs the document to mean what. This would help him sound less like “PM improves Confluence” and more like “agentic systems need boundary-object governance so Sales, Eng, Product, and Support don’t optimize from incompatible truths.”
