## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[context-management-as-a-bottleneck]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Context as Boundary Object,” anchored on Susan Leigh Star & James R. Griesemer, “Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects” (1989).**  
   Current concept treats context as fuel for agents. Add the missing social layer: context is also a negotiated artifact that lets different actors coordinate without fully agreeing. Sentence pattern: “A context packet is successful when it is plastic enough for each role to use locally, but stable enough to preserve identity across handoffs.”  
   This unlocks a **portfolio essay or agent spec** on why Sean’s fleet needs different context objects for writer, critic, researcher, daily-driver, and job-hunt modes. Without this, the concept stays inside “better summaries”; with it, Sean can argue for **context products**, not just context compression.

2. **Add “Information Foraging / Scent,” anchored on Peter Pirolli & Stuart Card, “Information Foraging in Information Access Environments” (1999).**  
   The article says agents need relevant injected context, but it does not name the mechanism by which relevance is discovered. Add “information scent”: agents should follow cues, not ingest everything. Sentence pattern: “Good context management is not recall maximization; it is scent design, where every injected fragment carries enough signal to justify the next retrieval step.”  
   This unlocks an **executable demo or runbook** for Sean’s vault agents: context packets with scent markers, retrieval trails, stop rules, and decay logic. It also gives him a stronger critique of his own fleet: if the nightly critic is descriptive, it may be receiving high-volume context with weak scent.

3. **Add a contradicting “Forgetting as Infrastructure” mode, anchored on Jorge Luis Borges, “Funes the Memorious” (1942) plus Viktor Mayer-Schönberger, *Delete: The Virtue of Forgetting in the Digital Age* (2009).**  
   The concept assumes better context management means more coherent, up-to-date memory. The missing counter-framework is that intelligence often depends on deletion, lossy abstraction, and refusal to carry forward stale detail. Sentence pattern: “The bottleneck is not only what the agent cannot fit into context; it is what the system lacks the courage to forget.”  
   This unlocks a **knowledge-lint artifact or vault policy**: expiration dates, demotion rules, contradiction retirement, and “do-not-inject” registers. It would let Sean ship a sharper Substack piece: not “agents need memory,” but “agentic systems need institutional forgetting.”
