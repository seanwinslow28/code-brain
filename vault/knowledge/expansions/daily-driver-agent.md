## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[daily-driver-agent]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Situated Daily Ops” as a correction to the assistant fantasy**

   **What to add:** A mode that treats the daily-driver agent as a *situated action partner*, not a planner that can know the day in advance. Sentence pattern: “The agent proposes a plan only as a disposable orientation device; the real job is detecting when the situation has changed.”

   **Anchor:** Lucy Suchman, *Plans and Situated Actions: The Problem of Human-Machine Communication*.

   **Unlocks:** A stronger agent spec and Substack essay: “Why personal AI assistants fail when they worship the calendar.” This would let Sean define interruption handling, ambiguity escalation, and plan-repair behavior instead of merely saying the agent “manages daily operations.” Current concept is too task-list shaped; Suchman gives him the contradiction.

2. **Add “Attention Budgeting” instead of task automation**

   **What to add:** Frame the daily-driver as an *attention allocation system*: it should decide what deserves Sean’s cognition today, what can be batched, what should be ignored, and what should become a ticket. Sentence pattern: “The agent does not optimize for completed tasks; it optimizes for protecting scarce executive attention.”

   **Anchor:** Herbert A. Simon, “Designing Organizations for an Information-Rich World” in *Computers, Communications, and the Public Interest*.

   **Unlocks:** A portfolio one-pager or runbook showing the daily driver as an executive-function layer for job hunting: inbox triage, interview prep, outbound follow-up, fleet health, and deferral rules. This gives Sean a sharper AI-PM artifact than “daily assistant,” because it names the product principle: abundance of information creates poverty of attention.

3. **Add “Commitment Ledger” for promises, not reminders**

   **What to add:** A facet where the daily-driver tracks commitments as social contracts: who is waiting on Sean, what Sean implicitly owes, what can be renegotiated, and what would damage trust if missed. Sentence pattern: “Every reminder is downgraded unless it represents a commitment between people.”

   **Anchor:** Fernando Flores and Terry Winograd, *Understanding Computers and Cognition: A New Foundation for Design*.

   **Unlocks:** An executable demo or agent schema for job-hunt follow-through: hiring-manager replies, recruiter loops, thank-you notes, referral asks, “waiting on them” states, and renegotiation prompts. The current concept talks about routines and automation; this would let Sean build a daily-driver agent that understands requests, promises, declarations, and breakdowns. That is much closer to PM-grade operating leverage.
