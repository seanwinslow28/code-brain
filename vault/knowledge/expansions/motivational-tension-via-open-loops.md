## What this is

Critiques from two external reasoners (gpt-5.5 via Codex CLI, Gemini 3 via Anti-Gravity CLI) of [[motivational-tension-via-open-loops]]. The synthesizer describes what the concept is; this expansion proposes what's missing.

## From Codex (gpt-5.5)

1. **Add “Sternberg tension taxonomy,” not generic open loops.**  
   Anchor it on Meir Sternberg’s *[Expositional Modes and Temporal Ordering in Fiction](https://iupress.org/9780253207913/expositional-modes-and-temporal-ordering-in-fiction/)*. The missing distinction: **curiosity = what happened? suspense = what will happen? surprise = I was wrong about what happened.** Your concept currently collapses all reader pull into “motivational tension,” which makes the Substack skill unable to diagnose loop type.  
   Sentence pattern to add: “This paragraph is not a cliffhanger; it is a past-tense curiosity gap / future-tense suspense bet / surprise setup.”  
   Unlocks: a **Substack tension-audit rubric** or **critic agent spec** that can mark each section by tension mode and catch the generic failure mode: “interesting premise, no temporal engine.”

2. **Add Loewenstein’s “priming dose” as the operational version of closeable curiosity.**  
   Anchor it on George Loewenstein’s 1994 paper, *[The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation](https://philpapers.org/rec/LOWTPO-6)*, summarized in this neuroscience review as information-gap curiosity triggered by a small amount of partial knowledge. Your concept has “small and closeable,” but not the craft lever: the reader needs **just enough partial answer to feel ownership of the missing piece**.  
   Pattern to add: “Give the reader 70% of the answer, then withhold the decisive 30%.”  
   Unlocks: an **executable draft-revision checklist** for Sean’s Substack skill: every open loop must include known-state, missing-state, why-now, and payoff horizon. Without this, the concept stays psychological; with it, it becomes an editing tool.

3. **Add Brooks’s “narrative desire” to move from hook mechanics to essay-scale architecture.**  
   Anchor it on Peter Brooks’s *[Reading for the Plot: Design and Intention in Narrative](https://books.google.com/books/about/Reading_for_the_Plot.html?id=bh4QV-uRvuwC)*. Brooks treats plot as desire stretched across time, not merely a question awaiting answer. The missing facet: some loops should not resolve quickly; they should become the essay’s propulsion system through delay, repetition, and retrospective meaning.  
   Pattern to add: “The opening question returns changed, not merely answered.”  
   Unlocks: a **job-hunt narrative arc / Substack season map** where “Will Sean become an agentic-engineering IC?” is not a recurring update but a structured long-form plot. Current concept can improve paragraphs; Brooks unlocks serialized essays with accumulating stakes.
