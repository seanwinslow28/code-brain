## Synthesis

There is a direct tension between the technical capability of a model to generate plausible text and the legal liability incurred when that text induces user action. When epistemic uncertainty (not knowing what it doesn't know) is not explicitly surfaced, the system creates an 'illusion of competence' that users rely on, thereby routing legal liability back to the organization. This means that technical calibration is not just a performance metric but a legal risk control mechanism; failing to distinguish between aleatoric noise and epistemic ignorance creates a structural vulnerability where the company is bound by the model's confident falsehoods.

## Implications

- Product teams must prioritize 'refusal behavior' and 'confidence surfacing' as primary safety features, not just accuracy metrics.
- Legal disclaimers are insufficient for liability protection; the system architecture itself must prevent confident hallucination in high-stakes domains.
