## Synthesis

The tension lies between the infinite variability of creative output and the finite need for brand consistency in Sean's job hunt and product projects. By treating illustration styles and design tokens as 'boundary objects' rather than artistic choices, Sean can offload the mechanical execution to agents while retaining supervisory control over the aesthetic outcome. The consequence is that 'taste' becomes a configurable parameter in the automation pipeline, reducing the cognitive load of reviewing every generated asset.

## Implications

- Sean must define 'taste' as a set of negative constraints (what NOT to do) rather than positive instructions, which are harder for agents to follow consistently.
- The agent fleet's output quality will degrade if the style guide is treated as optional context rather than a hard schema in the prompt engineering.
