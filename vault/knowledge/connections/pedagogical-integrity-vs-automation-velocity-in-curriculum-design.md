## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the velocity of agentic automation and the pedagogical requirement for 'faded scaffolding' in skill acquisition. High-velocity agents naturally push toward 'worked examples' (full solutions) to maximize throughput, but this accelerates the 'Illusion of Competence,' where the learner mistakes the agent's output for their own understanding. To preserve the curriculum's integrity, Sean must deliberately slow down the automation loop, forcing the agent to provide 'scaffolding' and 'explanations' rather than just outputs, thereby maintaining the 'Taste-Fidelity' link by keeping Sean's cognitive engagement high during the drafting phase.

## Implications

- Sean must design the Golden Loop curriculum with explicit 'friction points' where the agent is forbidden from drafting, forcing him to generate the initial structure himself to prevent skill atrophy.
- The evaluation metrics for the curriculum should not just measure the quality of the final output, but the depth of Sean's reasoning in the decision log, ensuring he is actively steering rather than passively reviewing.
