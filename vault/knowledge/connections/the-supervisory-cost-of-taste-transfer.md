## Synthesis

This connection reveals a fundamental tension between the desire for automated creative production and the finite capacity of human taste to encode itself into machine-readable constraints. As Sean scales his agent fleet, the cost of supervision does not decrease proportionally; instead, it increases because the rubric must become more complex to prevent drift. This creates a 'taste-fidelity decoupling' where the agent produces volume efficiently but loses the specific aesthetic signal that defines Sean's unique value. The consequence is that automation becomes a liability if the supervisory overhead exceeds the efficiency gains, forcing Sean to choose between generic scale and high-fidelity niche.
