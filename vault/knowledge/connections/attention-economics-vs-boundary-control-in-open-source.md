## Synthesis

The tension between maintaining control over a platform's integrity and managing the finite attention of maintainers creates a critical bottleneck for open-source synergy. While boundary resources allow Sean to shape external contributions, they simultaneously generate queues, social obligations, and support drag that compete for his limited cognitive bandwidth. This dynamic means that open source is not an automatic compounding asset but an attention market where merge rights must be strategically allocated to prevent reputation pressure from overwhelming core development.

## Threads

### [[Boundary Object Theory]]

> Open source is not merely proof of generosity or visibility; it is a control surface.

### [[Demonstration Over Declaration]]

> The missing move is to turn each claim into a refutable public test: can another person run the MCP server, feed it a bad intent spec, see the audit fail, fix it, and observe a better agent outcome?

### [[Agentic Engineering]]

> Open source does not automatically compound. It creates queues, social obligations, support drag, vague feature requests, and reputation pressure.

## Implications

- Sean must implement a maintainer runbook that explicitly defines what gets accepted, closed, or delegated to prevent attention drain.
- Portfolio artifacts should prioritize falsifiable demos over broad contribution metrics to signal quality without inviting excessive support requests.
- The design of boundary resources must include explicit failure modes and evals to reduce the cognitive load on maintainers reviewing external contributions.
