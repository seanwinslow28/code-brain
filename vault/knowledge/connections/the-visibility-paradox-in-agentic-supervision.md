## Synthesis

There is a critical tension between the efficiency gains of automated execution and the necessity of making human supervision visible for career advancement. As Sean delegates more technical work to his agent fleet, the value of his labor shifts from the artifact itself to the tacit judgment required to direct that artifact. However, this tacit judgment is invisible by nature, creating a paradox where the more effective his automation becomes, the harder it is to demonstrate his professional worth to hiring managers who rely on visible artifacts for evaluation.

## Implications

- Sean must treat his interview performance as a separate discipline from his coding output, focusing on narrating the invisible navigation decisions he makes.
- His portfolio projects should be framed not just as technical achievements but as boundary objects that demonstrate his ability to translate intent into executable plans for diverse stakeholders.
