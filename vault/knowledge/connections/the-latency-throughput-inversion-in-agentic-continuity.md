## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between measuring creative output by volume (throughput) and measuring it by the speed of recovery from failure (latency). When Sean focuses on maximizing concepts written, he risks ignoring the 'restart latency' that determines whether those concepts are actually usable after an interruption. This inversion means that high-throughput runs with poor resumption packets are less valuable than lower-throughput runs with robust continuity artifacts, because the latter preserve the 'active goal' and 'unresolved tension' needed for Sean to re-engage effectively.

## Implications

- Sean should evaluate his fleet's performance based on 'minutes until Sean produces the next meaningful edit' rather than concept count, forcing a shift in how he defines success.
- The fleet must implement 'resumption packets' as a mandatory contract for every degraded run, ensuring that partial output is always executable and not just a record of activity.
- Sean needs to deliberately incapacitate agents to test if the fleet can change tactics, revealing whether they are truly extensible or just brittle under load.
