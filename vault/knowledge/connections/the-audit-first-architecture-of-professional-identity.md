## Synthesis

Sean is constructing a professional identity that is fundamentally 'audit-first', where the value of his work is derived not from the volume of output but from the rigor of the evidence grading mechanism. This creates a tension with the 'Director-Intern Invariant' because the Intern (agent) must produce enough volume to be useful, but the Director (Sean) must retain the ability to audit every piece of evidence without being overwhelmed. The consequence is a 'sequential pipeline' design where auditing is not an afterthought but a structural component that determines the flow of work. This shifts the cost center from 'creation' to 'verification', making Sean's role more of a 'judge' than a 'maker'.

## Implications

- Sean must design his agent fleet to output 'auditable artifacts' rather than just 'finished products', increasing the initial cost of automation but reducing long-term supervision fatigue.
- The 'Insights & Analytics' seat in his Productcraft bench is not a creative role but a quality assurance role, suggesting that his professional value is tied to his ability to grade evidence, not generate it.
