## Synthesis

The fundamental tension lies in the agent fleet's inability to distinguish between 'declared routines' (static, pre-defined plans) and 'situated actions' (dynamic, context-dependent improvisation). This misalignment causes the system to optimize for compliance with an imaginary workflow, while the actual value is generated in the 'residue' of deviations that remain invisible to the fleet's observability. The consequence is a systemic blind spot where the most important adaptive behaviors are logged as errors or ignored entirely, leading to a false sense of operational health.

## Implications

- Sean must redesign his agent fleet's observability to capture 'deviation logs' as first-class data, allowing him to analyze the true shape of his creative process rather than just its idealized form.
- The fleet's routing logic needs to be flexible enough to allow for improvisation without flagging it as a failure, enabling a feedback loop where the system learns from Sean's adaptations.
