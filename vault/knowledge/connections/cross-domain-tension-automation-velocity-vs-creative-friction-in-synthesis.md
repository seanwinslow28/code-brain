## Synthesis

This connection reveals a critical tension between the desire for high-velocity automation and the need for creative friction to maintain taste fidelity. As Sean's synthesizer scales up, the volume of rejected clusters increases, but the quality of connections does not necessarily improve proportionally. The consequence is a trust deficit where the user feels the output is becoming generic, leading to a potential abandonment of the tool if the 'taste' is not preserved.

## Implications

- Sean must tune the synthesizer to prioritize fewer, higher-quality connections over sheer volume to preserve taste.
- He should monitor rejection rates as a proxy for creative friction, ensuring they are not too low (indicating lack of scrutiny) or too high (indicating noise).
