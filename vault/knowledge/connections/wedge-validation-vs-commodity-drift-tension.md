## Synthesis

The tension lies in the temporal decay of defensibility: a wedge is only valuable if it captures demand before the component becomes a commodity, yet validation requires time that allows competitors to catch up. This creates a race where Sean must identify high-visibility nodes to defend while treating hidden components as table stakes, forcing a strategic choice between productizing novelty or open-sourcing infrastructure. The consequence is that without a commodity drift map, any wedge strategy will inevitably fail as the market standardizes around the very friction he sought to kill.

## Implications

- Sean must prioritize building only the high-visibility layer of his stack while treating underlying infrastructure as undifferentiated work to avoid wasting resources on non-defensible moats.
- The validation timeline for any new wedge must be compressed to outpace the rate of commodity drift in the specific value chain node being targeted.
