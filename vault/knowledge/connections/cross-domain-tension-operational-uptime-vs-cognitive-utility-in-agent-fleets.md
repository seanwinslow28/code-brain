## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the operational uptime of Sean's agent fleet and the cognitive utility of its output, where high availability creates an illusion of productivity while masking semantic decay. As the fleet scales to maintain constant sampling rates (e.g., daily runs at 02:30), the volume of generated artifacts outpaces Sean's capacity for verification, leading to a 'trust deficit' where he must manually correct errors rather than trusting the system. This pattern reveals that scaling automation without scaling human oversight capacity results in diminishing returns, as the cost of judgment exceeds the value of the generated concepts.
