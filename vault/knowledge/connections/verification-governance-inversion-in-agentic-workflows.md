## Synthesis

The tension lies between the operational visibility of agent fleets and the semantic value of their output. As Sean scales his fleet to increase throughput, the verification capacity does not scale linearly, creating a governance gap where the system appears healthy (high uptime, high concept count) but is semantically decaying. This inversion forces Sean to rely on manual taste checks rather than automated quality gates, effectively making human supervision the bottleneck for creative authority instead of computational power.

## Implications

- Sean must decouple production scaling from verification expectations, accepting that more agents do not equal better insight without proportional taste infrastructure.
- The current fleet configuration is masking semantic decay behind high operational metrics, requiring a shift in how success is measured from volume to connection density.
