## Synthesis

The tension lies between expanding an agent's capability through more tools and the exponential increase in maintenance complexity required to keep it honest. As the harness grows—adding data feeds, review screens, and logs—the probability of silent failure increases unless inspection habits are rigorously maintained. This pattern suggests that 'mostly' correct systems rely on margin and care, not just model accuracy, meaning Sean should prioritize reducing his agent's toolset to ensure failures remain local and retrievable.

## Implications

- Sean should aggressively prune tools from his agent fleet to reduce the maintenance surface, even if it temporarily reduces capability.
- Investing in 'inspection' and 'skepticism' protocols is more valuable than buying larger models for long-term reliability.
