## Synthesis

The core tension lies in the trade-off between agent autonomy and human oversight: as agents gain the ability to pursue goals over longer stretches of time and use tools independently, the risk of 'rogue' behavior increases, necessitating a new form of human supervision. This creates a dependency where the human must act as a 'dither' or quality filter, not because the agent is incompetent, but because the stochastic nature of agentic systems requires constant curation. The consequence is a shift in professional value from execution to supervision, where the ability to manage and correct agent behavior becomes the primary skill.
