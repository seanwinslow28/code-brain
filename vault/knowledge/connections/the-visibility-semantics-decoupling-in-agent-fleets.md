## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between operational visibility (uptime, latency, error rates) and semantic value (correctness, relevance, intent alignment). As Sean's fleet scales, monitoring focuses on the former because it is easy to measure, while the latter decouples and degrades silently. This creates a 'health illusion' where the system appears robust but fails to deliver strategic value, forcing Sean into a role of constant manual verification rather than strategic oversight.

## Implications

- Sean must redesign monitoring to include semantic validation steps, not just process health checks, to prevent silent drift in creative and professional outputs.
- The cost of manual verification for 'healthy' but semantically flawed agents may exceed the cost of building more robust, self-correcting agent architectures.
