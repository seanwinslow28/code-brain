## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the automation system's drive for high-fidelity execution and the user's need for semantic authenticity. The system interprets 'faithfulness' as adherence to explicit stylistic constraints, while the user requires 'faithfulness' to their internal, often unarticulated, identity. This creates a trap where increasing the precision of the prompt leads to more accurate but less authentic outputs, because the system is optimizing for the wrong metric: structural compliance rather than personal resonance.

## Implications

- Sean must stop trying to automate his voice through style mimicry and instead focus on automating the *constraints* that force him to write from his own experience.
- Any future creative automation must include a 'tacit knowledge layer' that explicitly defines what the system should NOT do, not just what it should do.
