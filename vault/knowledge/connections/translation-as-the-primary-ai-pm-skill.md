## Synthesis

The tension between technical mechanism and stakeholder risk creates a 'Cross-domain bridging' challenge that defines the AI PM role. The consequence is that technical competence is insufficient; the PM must act as a translator who maps a single technical constraint to five distinct stakeholder languages. This insight reveals that the 'Cross-Functional Translation' skill is not soft-skill fluff but a hard requirement for product survival, as evidenced by the failure mode of pitches dying when engineers explain RAG to lawyers without addressing liability. For Sean, this means his portfolio must demonstrate this translation explicitly, not just the technical build.

## Threads

### [[Cross-domain bridging]]

> The same sentence about hallucination rates has to land as five different promises. That translation is the whole job.

### [[Accountability Gap]]

> Every AI product pitch I've watched die in a room died the same way: an engineer explained RAG to a lawyer, the lawyer heard 'the machine writes things and we hope they're true,' and the meeting was over before the coffee got cold.

### [[Two Audiences]]

> The editor wants her voice protected, the strategist wants throughput, the SEO lead wants to not get delisted by Google, the lawyer wants to not get sued, and the executive wants a number she can say to a board.

## Implications

- Sean's portfolio artifacts must explicitly document the 'five languages' of a stakeholder group to prove his translation capability, rather than just showing the technical output.
- Job hunt messaging should frame his experience as 'bridging the gap between engineering constraints and executive risk' to align with the 'Cross-Functional Translation' skill gap in Tier-1 JDs.
- Product requirements documents should prioritize the 'sober' discovery phase where these translations are defined, as this is the load-bearing section of the PRD.
