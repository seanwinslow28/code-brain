## Synthesis

Sean faces a structural tension between the need for 'legibility' to pass institutional filters and the need for 'boundary objects' to maintain differentiation. Legibility requires simplifying his work into recognizable keywords, which erases the local complexity that makes him unique. Boundary objects require preserving that complexity so different audiences can interpret it differently. The consequence is that optimizing for one mechanism actively undermines the other: a highly legible portfolio becomes indistinguishable from competitors, while a highly differentiated boundary object may fail to register with recruiters.

## Implications

- Sean must design portfolio artifacts that are simultaneously simple enough for recruiters to parse and complex enough for engineers to respect, requiring a dual-layer narrative structure.
- He should avoid 'sand down' keywords in favor of specific value-chain positions that prove advantage in a market transition, accepting lower legibility for higher differentiation.
