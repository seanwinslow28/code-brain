## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the technological promise of open, frictionless agentic networks and the economic reality that trust and settlement require centralized chokepoints. While agents remove transaction costs, they do not remove coordination costs; instead, these costs migrate to identity and verification layers. This creates a paradox where the most valuable position in the agentic economy is not at the edge (the open mesh) but at the center (the aggregator), forcing creators and startups to choose between building an open protocol that captures no value or integrating with centralized distributors that capture most of it.
