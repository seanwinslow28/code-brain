## Synthesis

There is a critical tension between the operational health of individual agents and the strategic value they provide to Sean's broader goals. Agents like job-feed and deep-researcher report 'success' or 'degraded' statuses based on their internal execution loops, but their outputs (or lack thereof) have no direct impact on the strategic outcomes of his job hunt or creative work. This decoupling means that Sean can have a fleet that is technically 'healthy' while being strategically useless, as the agents are not producing the necessary signals for decision-making. The consequence is a false sense of progress, where the infrastructure appears to be working hard, but the user is receiving no actionable insights.

## Implications

- Sean must define success metrics for his agents based on output quality and strategic relevance, not just execution status.
- The fleet dashboard needs to be enhanced to highlight agents that are technically running but producing zero value, rather than just those that have crashed.
- A failure in the input layer (e.g., job feed API) should trigger a higher-level alert than a simple 'degraded' status, as it represents a strategic blockage.
