## Synthesis

The economic literature on autonomous agents reveals that while they can reduce search and contracting costs, their adoption is critically limited by human perceptions of autonomy loss. This creates a structural tension where the most efficient agents (fully autonomous) are the least trusted, forcing a design pattern of 'bounded delegation' that preserves human oversight. For Sean, this means his agent fleet must be engineered not just for speed, but for transparency and control, ensuring that the 'auditable mandates' and 'escalation thresholds' are visible to him, thereby maintaining his creative authority while leveraging agentic efficiency.

## Implications

- Sean should design his synthesizer with explicit 'escalation thresholds' that trigger human review for any concept or connection that exceeds a certain complexity or novelty score.
- The job hunt agents must preserve Sean's 'decision autonomy' by presenting options rather than executing applications, preventing the 'loss of choice' that depresses adoption.
