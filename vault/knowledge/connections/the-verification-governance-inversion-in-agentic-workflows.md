## Synthesis

Sean faces a structural inversion where the governance of his workflow (evals, protocols) becomes more complex and rigid than the actual creative or strategic work being performed. This tension arises because he attempts to apply rigorous, deterministic evaluation frameworks to inherently ambiguous human tasks like job hunting or creative direction. The consequence is that the system optimizes for measurable compliance rather than meaningful outcome, leading to high-volume but low-signal outputs that mask the underlying epistemic blindness.

- Sean must decouple his creative judgment from his automation metrics to prevent the system from optimizing for compliance over quality.
- The cost of maintaining rigorous evals for low-complexity tasks outweighs the benefit, suggesting a need for simpler, heuristic-based checks.
