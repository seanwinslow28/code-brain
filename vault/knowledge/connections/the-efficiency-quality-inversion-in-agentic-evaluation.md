## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the operational efficiency of using a single model family for evaluation and the epistemic integrity required for genuine discovery. When Sean relies on 'activity proof' (high run counts) as a metric, he falls into the trap of the Illusion of Competence, where the system appears robust because it is consistent with itself, not because it is correct. This inversion leads to a false sense of progress, masking the fact that the underlying taste and judgment mechanisms are failing to filter out low-value outputs.
