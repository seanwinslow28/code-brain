## Synthesis

The tension between principal-agent trust deficits and mental transaction costs reveals that agent adoption is limited by both ethical and cognitive barriers. While Szabo highlights the inability to verify agent intent as a fundamental trust issue, Shirky and Odlyzko emphasize the cognitive load of managing small transactions. Together, they show that agents fail not just because they are untrustworthy, but because they increase the mental burden on users who cannot easily audit their behavior.

## Implications

- Sean must prioritize transparency and local control in agent design to mitigate trust deficits, rather than relying on model accuracy alone.
- Automation strategies should aggregate tasks to reduce mental transaction costs, avoiding frequent low-value interactions that overwhelm users.
