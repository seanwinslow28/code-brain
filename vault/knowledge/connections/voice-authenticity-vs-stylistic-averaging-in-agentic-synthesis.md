## Synthesis

When agents are instructed to emulate multiple distinct creative voices simultaneously, they tend to converge on a homogenized 'average' style that lacks the friction and specificity of any single source. This tension reveals that stylistic emulation is not additive but subtractive; the agent must discard the unique, non-transferable traits of each author to find a common denominator. The consequence is that 'cramped' multi-author prompts produce technically correct but emotionally inert content, forcing a pivot from explicit instruction to implicit demonstration via personal evidence.

## Implications

- Multi-voice prompting strategies should be avoided in favor of single-voice deep emulation or evidence-based style transfer to prevent stylistic dilution.
- Creative agents require access to raw, unstructured personal artifacts (drafts, notes) rather than just high-level stylistic instructions to maintain authentic voice.
