## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the increasing efficiency of automated execution and the decreasing legibility of human value in career advancement. As Sean delegates more work to his agent fleet, his role shifts from artifact creation to tacit supervision, which is inherently invisible to external evaluators. This creates a paradox where greater technical effectiveness leads to lower professional visibility, requiring Sean to actively construct boundary objects and perform tacit judgment explicitly during interviews to bridge the gap between his actual work and perceived value.

## Implications

- Sean must treat his interview performance as a separate discipline from his coding output, focusing on narrating the invisible navigation decisions he makes.
- His portfolio projects should be framed not just as technical achievements but as boundary objects that demonstrate his ability to translate intent into executable plans for diverse stakeholders.
