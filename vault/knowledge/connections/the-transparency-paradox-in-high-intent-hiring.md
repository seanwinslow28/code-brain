## Synthesis

There is a critical tension between the candidate's need for concrete data (salary, location) to make rational decisions and the employer's tendency to withhold this information to maintain negotiation leverage or filter for 'culture fit' through later-stage interviews. This creates a paradox where the most relevant opportunities (Anthropic, Mercury) are also the most opaque, forcing Sean to rely on proxy signals like 'remote-friendly culture' rather than hard constraints. The consequence is that his decision-making process shifts from objective filtering to subjective risk assessment, increasing cognitive load and potential for error.

## Implications

- Sean must develop a heuristic for evaluating 'undisclosed salary' roles that goes beyond standard market rates, perhaps focusing on equity structures or total compensation bands disclosed in later stages.
- The reliance on 'remote-friendly culture' as a proxy for flexibility suggests a need to explicitly verify remote work policies before investing time in applications.
