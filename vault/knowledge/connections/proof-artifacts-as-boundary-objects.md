## Synthesis

The tension lies in treating portfolio projects as self-expression versus treating them as boundary objects that are legible to different audiences. When projects are designed as boundary objects, they must be both flexible enough to be interpreted by different stakeholders and robust enough to maintain their core identity across those interpretations. This requires a taxonomy of proof objects that allows reviewers to inspect specific outcomes, shifting the focus from the builder's narrative to the inspectable reality of the work. The consequence is that the portfolio becomes a set of verifiable proof objects that survive tool churn and demonstrate concrete observations and lessons learned, rather than just a list of tools built.
