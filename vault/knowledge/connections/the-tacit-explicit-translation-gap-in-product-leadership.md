## Synthesis

A critical tension exists between Sean's high-fidelity operational competence, built on tacit instinctual knowledge, and the explicit, canonical language required for product leadership roles. This gap creates a 'translation failure' where his actual value is invisible to hiring managers who rely on formal vocabulary to assess skill, forcing him to retroactively name his practices to demonstrate strategic value. The consequence is that without deliberate articulation exercises, his sophisticated fleet architecture is misinterpreted as simple automation rather than engineered control.

## Implications

- Sean must prioritize 'retrospective articulation' exercises that force him to name his instinctual practices before attempting to teach them, preventing the expertise-reversal effect.
- Job hunt materials should focus on mapping his specific fleet mechanisms (e.g., 'fail-closed cost guards') to canonical product management frameworks (e.g., 'risk mitigation strategies').
