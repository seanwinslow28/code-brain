## Synthesis

Sean's 'Tool-Shipping Playbook' relies on a rigid, repeatable engine to manage the chaos of solo creative production, yet this very rigidity creates a tension with the regulatory ambiguity of AI tooling. The playbook demands that every build produce a 'shipped plugin/MCP,' which requires strict protocol adherence (MCP standards), while simultaneously demanding 'honest-loop' narratives that expose the messy, unregulated reality of building in public. This tension forces Sean to navigate the gap between the clean, instrumented output required by the marketplace and the ambiguous, evolving nature of the AI tools themselves, risking credibility if the 'friction-killing' claim is undermined by the tool's own instability.

## Implications

- Sean must prioritize 'MCP Server Hardening' not just for technical stability, but to protect the credibility of his 'honest-loop' narrative against the risk of silent failures.
