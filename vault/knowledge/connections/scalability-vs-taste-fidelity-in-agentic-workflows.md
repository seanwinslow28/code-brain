## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the scalability of Sean's agent fleet and the specificity of his creative taste. As the fleet scales (sampling hundreds of clusters), the probability of homogenization increases because the context window cannot hold both operational instructions and nuanced aesthetic preferences simultaneously. This forces a choice: either reduce throughput to preserve taste, or accept that scaled output will drift toward statistical averages, losing Sean's unique voice.

## Implications

- Sean must implement dynamic pruning strategies to prevent taste dilution as cluster volume increases, rather than relying on larger context windows which only accelerate data accumulation.
- The fleet's scaling strategy requires a hard cap on daily cluster sampling to maintain aesthetic fidelity, or a shift to external taste-memory stores that do not compete for context window space.
