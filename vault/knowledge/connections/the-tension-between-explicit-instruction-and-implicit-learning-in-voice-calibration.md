## Synthesis

Sean's workflow reveals a fundamental tension between the desire for immediate control (explicit prompts) and the reality of complex style transfer (implicit learning). Explicit instructions fail because they rely on Sean's ability to articulate his taste, which he cannot do. Implicit learning succeeds by accumulating evidence over time, but it requires patience and volume. The consequence is that voice calibration shifts from a design problem to a data problem: the quality of the output depends not on the cleverness of the prompt, but on the density and specificity of the accumulated evidence.

## Implications

- Sean must accept a longer initial investment in data collection before seeing high-quality outputs, shifting his expectation from instant gratification to gradual refinement.
- The success of his voiceprint depends on the specificity of his cultural references, forcing him to dig deeper into his own tastes rather than settling for generic categories.
