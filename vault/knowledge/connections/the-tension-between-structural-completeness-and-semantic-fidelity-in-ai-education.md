## Synthesis

This connection reveals a critical tension where the automated system's drive for structural completeness (high concept counts, full curricula) directly conflicts with the semantic fidelity required for genuine learning and transfer. The consequence is that Sean may accumulate vast amounts of 'complete' artifacts that are useless in novel contexts, creating an illusion of competence while his actual ability to apply knowledge erodes. This pattern is particularly dangerous in educational and creative domains where the value lies not in the volume of information but in the depth of understanding and transferability.

## Implications

- Curriculum design must prioritize 'transferability' exercises that force application in unfamiliar contexts, rather than just reinforcing knowledge within a familiar system.
- The definition of 'success' for an AI-generated educational artifact should be measured by the learner's ability to apply concepts in new domains, not by the completeness of the generated content.
