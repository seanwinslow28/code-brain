## Synthesis

When boundary setting excludes exogenous forces like model-provider pricing or competitor moves, delay marks become invisible because the system treats these variables as constants rather than dynamic inputs. This combination creates a structural blind spot where interventions appear successful in the short term but fail catastrophically when delayed effects from outside the boundary manifest. The consequence is a false sense of control where the map's completeness is mistaken for reality's predictability, leading to overconfidence in automated systems that cannot account for external shocks.

## Implications

- Fleet monitoring dashboards must explicitly model exogenous forces as dynamic variables rather than static constants to avoid false stability signals.
- Boundary decisions should be revisited periodically as external dependencies evolve, preventing the map from becoming obsolete while still appearing accurate.
