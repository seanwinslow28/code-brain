## Synthesis

This connection reveals a fundamental tension between the theoretical scalability of agent fleets and the biological limits of human supervision. As Sean increases concept volume, the cognitive load of verifying taste fidelity grows non-linearly, creating a hard cap on effective output regardless of computational power. This pattern implies that scaling the fleet without reducing the granularity of supervision will lead to diminishing returns and potential burnout, necessitating a shift toward coarser-grained evaluation or automated taste-checking mechanisms.

## Implications

- Sean should cap concept generation at a level that allows for deep, high-fidelity supervision rather than maximizing raw output volume.
- Investment in automated taste-checking tools may yield higher ROI than scaling model size or cluster count.
