## Synthesis

There is a fundamental tension between the engineer's need for rigorous, automated evaluation and the creative's need for nuanced, intent-based governance. Engineers build tools that measure correctness against fixed specs, while creatives require tools that measure alignment with evolving intent. This gap creates an opportunity for Sean to build 'intent engineering' as a distinct discipline, bridging the two worlds by making the audit of intent the primary metric of success.

## Threads

### [[Supervision as the New AI Edge]]

> the audit IS the eval — it scores a spec against the framework before that spec ships to a coding agent

### [[Context Management as a Bottleneck]]

> the creative slice is still re-aimed from an engineer-skewed corpus — that skew held across all three passes, which is itself a finding

### [[Intent Engineering]]

> most teams do not need research-grade evals first — i.e. the gap is not 'no eval tools' but 'eval tools built for the wrong audience'

## Implications

- Sean should position his services as 'intent governance' rather than just 'AI implementation,' targeting creative teams who are frustrated by misaligned outputs.
- Developing a lightweight, creative-focused eval framework that prioritizes intent alignment over technical correctness could capture an underserved market.
