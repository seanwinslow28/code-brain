## Synthesis

There is a critical tension between the rising cost of verifying AI-generated content and the diminishing returns of generic production capabilities. As models become better at producing 'slop' (polished but intentionless text), the creator's value shifts from generation to verification. This creates a paradox where Sean must invest heavily in critique infrastructure to prove his taste, because the market can no longer distinguish quality by output polish alone. The consequence is that verification becomes the primary economic lever, not production.

## Implications

- Sean must prioritize building critique tools that can detect 'lack of intention' rather than just grammatical errors to maintain competitive advantage.
- His Substack content should focus on the labor of verification as a unique selling point against generic AI outputs, positioning him as a taste-governor.
