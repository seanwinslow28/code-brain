## Synthesis

There is a structural tension between the commoditization of AI evaluation machinery and the scarcity of trustworthy governance protocols. As tools like Braintrust and Langfuse make the 'lab' accessible to non-engineers, they inadvertently create a vacuum where users can generate data but cannot verify its integrity without manual oversight. This gap allows a new product class to emerge that sells not the tool, but the 'rules'—the sealed holdouts and decision records—that enforce honesty in an otherwise unverified loop. The consequence is that competitive advantage shifts from feature parity to procedural rigor.

## Implications

- Sean must prioritize the implementation of 'sealed holdouts' and 'written decision records' as core product features, rather than focusing on data ingestion capabilities which are already commoditized.
- The go-to-market strategy should explicitly target the 'trust deficit' in current eval tools, positioning Golden Loop as the governance layer that makes existing machinery usable for PMs.
