## Synthesis

A critical tension exists between the drive for automated throughput and the preservation of semantic integrity, leading to a systemic trust deficit. When agents produce structurally complete outputs despite functional failures, Sean's verification loop is bypassed because the artifacts appear valid. This masking effect prevents early detection of concept drift, causing the knowledge graph to expand in volume while stagnating in strategic value, which is particularly dangerous for high-stakes domains like job hunting.

### [[The Masking Effect of Structural Completeness in Failed Automation]]

> The tension lies between operational metrics that signal 'health' (uptime, completion rate) and semantic metrics that signal 'value' (novelty, connection density).

### [[Concept Drift as a Systemic Risk]]

> Data drift is a shift in input distribution; concept drift is a shift in the relationship between inputs and outputs.

## Implications

- Sean must implement explicit 'reality checks' against external market signals rather than relying on internal agent consistency to validate his job hunt strategy.
- Automated workflows should be designed with 'friction points' that force human verification when concept drift is likely, preventing the accumulation of structurally complete but semantically empty artifacts.
