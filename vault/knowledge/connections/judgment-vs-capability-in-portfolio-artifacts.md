## Synthesis

The tension lies between presenting a portfolio as a list of completed tasks (capability) versus a series of reasoned decisions (judgment). When a candidate focuses only on capability, they risk being seen as a technician who can execute but not strategize. By shifting to judgment evidence, the candidate demonstrates the ability to navigate ambiguity and make trade-offs, which is the core of senior product management. This shift transforms the portfolio from a static record of work into a dynamic demonstration of thinking. The consequence is that the candidate becomes a peer in the interview rather than a subordinate seeking validation.

## Implications

- Sean should structure his interview stories around the 'why' and 'why not' rather than just the 'what'.
- He must explicitly articulate the rejected alternatives to demonstrate the rigor of his decision-making process.
- The portfolio should highlight the systemic mismatches he identified, not just the solutions he built.
- Interviewers will evaluate his strategic thinking over his technical execution.
