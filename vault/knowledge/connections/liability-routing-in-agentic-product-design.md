## Synthesis

The tension between autonomous agent capability and organizational risk tolerance creates a 'liability routing' problem that defines the boundary of viable agentic products. When agents operate autonomously, the traditional product management model breaks down because the 'who absorbs the residual responsibility' question becomes unanswerable within existing corporate structures. This forces a design pattern where human-in-the-loop checkpoints are not just best practices but legal necessities, creating a specific type of friction that Sean must engineer around. The consequence is that successful agentic products are not defined by their autonomy level, but by their ability to clearly assign blame and accountability back to the human architect.
