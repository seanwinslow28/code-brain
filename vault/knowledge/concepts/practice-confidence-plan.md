## Definition

A structured training protocol that shifts interview preparation from passive content consumption to active performance calibration. It integrates Ericsson’s deliberate practice for skill isolation, Roediger and Karpicke’s retrieval practice for memory durability, and Johnstone’s improvisational techniques for status management. This approach treats confidence not as an internal state but as a measurable output of repeated, constrained execution under varying social stances. The mechanism requires Sean to produce artifacts like voice-calibration essays and mock-interview prompt packs to externalize his competence.

## Context

Sean is preparing for senior AI-PM and agentic-engineering roles where technical depth must be paired with executive presence. Current plans risk producing 'familiarity' without the ability to deploy knowledge under pressure or control the narrative frame of an interview. This concept addresses the gap between knowing the material and performing it effectively to hiring managers.

## Evidence

> The plan currently includes feeds, vocab rotation, and targeted consumption. That risks becoming sophisticated rereading.

> Confidence in interviews is partly performative range: status, timing, offer/accept, recovery after a bad answer.

> Without this, the plan trains content recall but not the live-room problem where he can sound overbuilt, underfocused, or too inward-facing.

## Examples

- One rep = prompt -> timed answer -> transcript -> scored rubric -> rewritten answer -> second timed answer
- No source open. Answer from memory first. Then compare against canon. Then patch only the missing move.
- Tell the same story three ways: calm senior PM, precise IC builder, candid postmortem owner.
