## Definition

This mechanism describes the process of defining aesthetic boundaries through exclusion rather than inclusion, where taste is established by identifying what is 'not' desired rather than prescribing what is. It relies on reactive feedback loops ('No.' 'Gross.') to sharpen judgment, contrasting with proactive instruction which often fails to capture nuance. This approach allows for the preservation of unique voice by actively filtering out generic or low-fidelity outputs generated by automated systems.

## Context

Sean uses this method to find his writing voice by reacting to what it isn't, acknowledging that positive prompts are insufficient for capturing his specific style. This is essential in his current setup where agents generate bulk content, requiring him to act as a filter rather than a director.

## Evidence

> I want to find my writing voice by reacting to what it ISN'T. two words is fine. "No." "Gross." "Never."

> The tension lies in the fact that the mechanisms ensuring operational reliability (explicit instructions) are ineffective for creative work, forcing Sean to maintain separate mental models.

## Examples

- Using short, negative feedback ('No.') to calibrate agent output
- Identifying 'gross' elements in automated text to refine taste
