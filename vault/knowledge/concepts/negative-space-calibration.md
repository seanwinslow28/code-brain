## Definition

Negative space calibration refers to the practice of treating gaps in knowledge as areas for investigation rather than opportunities for immediate solutioning. This mechanism requires agents to state gaps as needs or pains, not solutions, ensuring that the focus remains on understanding the underlying problem before proposing any fixes. It serves as a critical counterbalance to the tendency toward 'solutioneering' and helps maintain epistemic hygiene.

## Context

Sean should enforce negative space calibration in his gap maps to prevent agents from jumping to conclusions. This approach ensures that his automated workflows are grounded in genuine needs rather than speculative opportunities, leading to more reliable and actionable insights.

## Evidence

> Every strong gap presentation does the same three things to each gap: (1) states it as a need/pain, not a solution.

> The synthesis pipeline should prioritize investigation prompts over solution generation when gaps are identified to maintain epistemic hygiene.

## Examples

- Agents stating gaps as needs rather than solutions leads to more accurate gap analysis.
- Prioritizing investigation prompts helps maintain epistemic hygiene and prevents 'slop'.
