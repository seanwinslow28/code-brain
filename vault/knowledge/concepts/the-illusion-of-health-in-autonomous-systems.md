## Definition

Autonomous systems generate a false signal of health by reporting successful task completion, even when the underlying infrastructure or data quality is failing. This illusion arises because the monitoring metrics focus on process execution (did the agent run?) rather than outcome validity (was the output useful?). The system appears robust because it does not crash, but it is actually brittle because it cannot self-correct when the context it relies on becomes invalid.

## Context

Sean uses fleet status to gauge the health of his personal knowledge infrastructure. If he trusts the 'healthy' status without verifying the semantic integrity of the outputs, he may miss critical failures in his job hunt tracking or creative project management until they cause a visible disruption.

## Evidence

> The daily-driver morning agent successfully generated a summary and created a daily note, indicating that the immediate operational loop is functioning as expected despite underlying infrastructure gaps.

> Sean's agentic infrastructure creates a dangerous feedback loop where operational health metrics mask semantic stagnation. Agents report success based on process execution, while knowledge integrity depends on successful validation.

## Examples

- The fleet reports 'healthy' status based on process execution, while knowledge integrity depends on successful validation of the synthesized content.
- When key infrastructure components fail silently, the system continues to operate but produces outputs that are increasingly disconnected from reality.
