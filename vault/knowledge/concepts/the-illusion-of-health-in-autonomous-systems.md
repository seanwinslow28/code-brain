## Definition

This pattern describes a state where an automated system appears stable because its internal metrics (such as output volume or low rejection rates) remain within acceptable bounds, while the underlying quality of its filtering mechanisms degrades. The system's health is measured by throughput rather than fidelity, creating a false sense of operational success that masks the loss of strategic rigor. As the system prioritizes velocity, it bypasses the necessary friction required to validate high-value opportunities, leading to a dilution of signal that only becomes apparent when the user manually re-applies judgment.

## Context

Sean's job hunt automation has shifted from a high-friction, high-rejection model (qwen3-14b) to a low-friction, high-throughput model (qwen3.6-35b). This shift creates a dangerous illusion where the system feels 'healthy' because it is producing results, even though those results are less strategically aligned with his actual career goals.
