## Definition

Autonomous systems create an illusion of health when their internal metrics (uptime, task completion) are decoupled from external validity (accuracy, relevance). The system is 'healthy' because it is doing what it was told to do, but it is 'unhealthy' because the instructions or data are no longer aligned with the desired outcome. This illusion persists until a human operator manually verifies the output against reality.

## Context

Sean's daily notes and summaries give him a sense of progress and control. However, if these are generated from stale data, they create a comforting but false narrative of productivity, masking the need for actual strategic intervention.

## Evidence

> The daily-driver morning agent successfully generated a summary and created a daily note, indicating that the immediate operational loop is functioning as expected despite underlying infrastructure gaps.

> This creates a dangerous feedback loop where Sean receives daily summaries that feel complete but are actually based on outdated information, leading to strategic errors in his job hunt and creative projects.

## Examples

- The `vault-critic` has not run since 2026-08-31, leaving no recent assessment of the quality or coherence of the synthesized concepts and connections.
- Sean must implement a 'semantic health' metric that measures the freshness and coherence of outputs, not just the uptime of agents.
