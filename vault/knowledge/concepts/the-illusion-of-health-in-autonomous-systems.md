## Definition

Autonomous systems often signal health through the successful completion of routine tasks, even when their underlying data or context is compromised. This illusion arises because the system's monitoring focuses on process execution rather than content validity. The agent appears healthy because it is doing what it was told to do, but the task itself may be based on incorrect premises, leading to a dangerous feedback loop where errors are amplified by continued automation.

## Context

Sean's morning briefs and daily notes give him a sense of control and continuity. However, if these outputs are generated from stale data, they create a false sense of security, causing him to overlook critical issues in his job hunt or creative projects that require immediate attention.

## Evidence

> The daily-driver morning agent successfully generated a summary and created a daily note, indicating that the immediate operational loop is functioning as expected despite underlying infrastructure gaps.

> This creates a dangerous feedback loop where Sean receives daily summaries that feel complete but are actually based on outdated information, leading to strategic errors in his job hunt and creative projects.

## Examples

- The `vault-critic` has not run since 2026-08-31, leaving no recent assessment of the quality or coherence of the synthesized concepts and connections.
- Sean must implement a 'semantic health' metric that measures the freshness and coherence of outputs, not just the uptime of agents.
