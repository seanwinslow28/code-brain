## Definition

This invariant defines the structural necessity of separating the 'maker' role from the 'auditor' role within a single individual's workflow. The 'Director' must maintain enough distance from the 'Intern's' output to judge it objectively, yet remain close enough to understand the context. If the Director becomes too involved in the Intern's tasks, they lose their supervisory edge; if they become too detached, they lose control over quality standards.

## Context

Sean is struggling with this invariant as he attempts to scale his 'Productcraft bench'. He is trying to act as both the high-volume producer and the rigorous auditor simultaneously, which leads to 'supervision fatigue' because he cannot switch contexts fast enough to maintain both roles effectively.

## Evidence

> The bench is not an org chart with seven headcount; it is one PM's toolset, split into artifact-owning stages so each stage can be grounded in its own canon and audited by a different one.

> Sean must design his agent fleet to output 'auditable artifacts' rather than just 'finished products', increasing the initial cost of automation but reducing long-term supervision fatigue.

## Examples

- The 'Insights & Analytics' seat is redefined not as a creative role but as a quality assurance role, shifting Sean's professional value from generation to grading.
- Running seats as a sequential pipeline with two closed audit cycles forces the Director to pause and verify before allowing the Intern to proceed to the next stage.
