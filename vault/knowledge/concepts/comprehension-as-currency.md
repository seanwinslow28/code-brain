## Definition

In media markets, measurement data functions not merely as a tool for optimization but as a hard substance that transforms ambiguous human attention into a calculable object suitable for transaction. This transformation requires an intermediary to certify the accuracy of the count, creating a trust-based monopoly where the value of the metric is derived from its universal acceptance rather than its technical sophistication. When this certification becomes standardized, it acts as a boundary object that allows disparate actors (advertisers and publishers) to coordinate on price without needing to agree on the underlying quality of the content.
