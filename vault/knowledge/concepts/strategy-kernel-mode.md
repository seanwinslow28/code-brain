## Definition

This mechanism functions as a diagnostic filter that forces strategic choices to follow a strict sequence: diagnosis of the primary constraint, formulation of a guiding policy to address it, and derivation of coherent actions. It explicitly contradicts the assumption that integration or activity volume constitutes strategy, requiring Sean to articulate the 'why' behind technical work in terms of strategic advantage rather than mere execution. This mode unlocks role-selection criteria by ensuring that effort is allocated only where it resolves a specific bottleneck, such as insufficient externally legible IC evidence.

## Context

Sean often conflates activity with strategy, leading to high-volume but low-signal output during his job hunt. By applying this kernel, he can stop treating all technical work as equal and instead prioritize high-signal demos that showcase architectural judgment, making his career progression more defensible and focused on external perception rather than internal complexity.

## Evidence

> It contradicts the assumption that integration itself is strategy by forcing a clear diagnosis of the constraint (e.g., insufficient externally legible IC evidence) and a guiding policy to address it.

> This mode unlocks role-selection criteria and defensible explanations for why specific technical work deserves the next week of effort.

## Examples

- Diagnosis: 'Recruiters cannot assess my seniority from code alone.' Guiding Policy: 'Prioritize high-signal, low-volume demos that showcase architectural judgment over implementation speed.'
