## Definition

An accountability gap emerges when the complexity of an automated system exceeds the cognitive load required for a human to verify its outputs, creating a zone where errors can propagate without detection. In this gap, the agent is technically 'responsible' for executing a task, but the human lacks the necessary context or time to audit the result, leading to a diffusion of responsibility. This gap widens as automation scales, because the cost of manual verification eventually exceeds the perceived value of the automated output, causing users to accept errors by default.

## Context

Sean is building a high-velocity job hunt and creative studio workflow. If he cannot verify the quality of his agent-generated artifacts (resumes, emails, code) due to this gap, he risks submitting substandard work or missing critical opportunities, undermining the very efficiency the automation was meant to provide.
