## Definition

An Accountability Gap is the deliberate exposure of non-conformance in a system card to establish credibility with expert reviewers. It functions as a signaling mechanism where the author admits to missing regulatory artifacts (like Annex IV templates) to prove they understand the gap's existence. This transparency transforms a potential failure into a demonstration of model-risk fluency, showing the hiring manager that the candidate can run the accounting and name the materiality. The gap is not hidden; it is the primary artifact proving the candidate's ability to distinguish between operational reality and compliance theater.

## Context

Sean is using this concept to differentiate his job-hunt portfolio from generic 'vibe-coding' projects. By explicitly naming the lack of post-market monitoring, he signals to fintech hiring managers that he understands the difference between a personal tool and a regulated product. This builds trust by avoiding the 'performing rigor' trap, where a candidate claims compliance they cannot substantiate.

## Evidence

> The gaps are named on purpose. They're the credibility.

> The point isn't to claim conformance. It's to prove I can run the accounting, name the materiality, and tell you exactly where the controls stop.

> No formal post-market-monitoring report cadence; no Annex IV-conformant template; no Article 13 user instructions. Name each plainly.

## Examples

- Explicitly stating 'No model is trained or fine-tuned here' to collapse training-data governance obligations to 'not applicable'.
- Marking lines where the system 'wouldn't pass' an audit in plain sight rather than hiding them in footnotes.
