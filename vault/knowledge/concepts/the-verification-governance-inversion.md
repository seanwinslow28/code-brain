## Definition

This concept describes a structural shift in value creation where the primary bottleneck moves from content generation to content validation. As generative models increase in capability, the cost of human verification rises because errors become more plausible and harder to detect without rigorous audit trails. The economic leverage therefore shifts toward tools that reduce the cognitive load of verifying AI output rather than those that merely accelerate its production.

## Context

Sean is building an agentic fleet for his job hunt and creative studio. If he focuses only on output speed, he risks accumulating 'legibility debt' where the system appears healthy but contains subtle errors that require disproportionate time to fix later. By prioritizing verification governance, he protects his most scarce resource: his own attention during the review phase.

## Evidence

> This connection reveals a critical inversion where the value of agentic infrastructure shifts from content creation to content validation.

> As models become more capable, the 'plausibility' of errors increases, making human verification more expensive and difficult.

## Examples

- Marketing teams report that 'AI-generated content often requires more editing time than writing from scratch,' which inverts the expected efficiency gain of agentic workflows.
- Legal teams in marketing organizations are 'hesitant to approve AI-generated content without extensive review,' citing potential copyright and defamation risks.
