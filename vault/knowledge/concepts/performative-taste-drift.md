## Definition

This mechanism describes the endogenous change in an evaluator's standards caused by repeated exposure to generated outputs. The fleet does not merely overwhelm pre-existing standards; it actively reshapes what feels normal and acceptable over time. This creates a feedback loop where acceptance rates serve as both quality metrics and interventions on future taste, effectively lowering the baseline for critical scrutiny.

## Context

Sean risks unknowingly lowering his own critical thresholds as he becomes accustomed to the style and depth of AI-generated content. This drift makes it harder to detect subtle degradations in reasoning or originality, requiring explicit protocols to maintain baseline standards.

## Evidence

> Treat taste memory as endogenous. Generated concepts do not merely overwhelm Sean’s pre-existing standards; repeated exposure changes what feels normal, what receives links, and what becomes future retrieval context.

> Every accepted synthesis modifies tomorrow’s evaluator; therefore acceptance rate is both a quality measurement and an intervention on future taste.

## Examples

- Blind-ranking old human work against new fleet work to detect shifts in preference.
- Tracking whether judgments change after exposure to automation-shaped vault content.
