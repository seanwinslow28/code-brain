## Definition

This tension defines the dual nature of 'gaps' in knowledge systems: they are either voids to be filled with data (hygiene) or structural absences that signal new market opportunities (hunting). When treated as hygiene, gaps are errors to be corrected; when treated as hunting grounds, they are unmet needs. The mechanism fails when the system conflates the two, leading to 'solution-side' bias where the agent searches for answers rather than validating the existence of the problem.
