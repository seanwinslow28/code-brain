## Definition

This mechanism describes a governance dynamic where technical adoption is determined by the speed of implementation and user adoption ('running code') rather than formal voting or expert consensus. In committee-based systems, structural gridlock arises because every trivial nuance becomes a point of contention among competing stakeholders, leading to paralysis. In contrast, decentralized ecosystems thrive on 'rough consensus,' where working implementations validate standards faster than documents can be written, rendering formal governance debates obsolete.

## Context

Sean's agent fleet architecture relies on MCP and A2A protocols. If these protocols are governed by strict committees or require perfect alignment before adoption, they will lose to simpler, messier alternatives that just work. The lesson from OSI vs. TCP/IP is that 'kings, presidents and voting' must be rejected in favor of practical interoperability.

## Examples

- OSI failed because representatives from ten competing corporations could not agree on even trivial language nuances.
- IBM 'played them like a violin' by gaming the OSI committee process, demonstrating that formal governance is vulnerable to strategic manipulation.
- The IETF's cultural counter-model to OSI was its rejection of hierarchical authority in favor of practical validation.
