## Definition

This concept defines the shift from prompt engineering to continuous, iterative calibration where the value is generated not by the initial instruction but by the sustained human oversight required to mold an LLM's output toward a specific, nuanced intent. It posits that because an LLM's 'mind' is molded by thousands of other users rather than the individual owner, one-shotting taste is impossible; instead, the user must engage in a prolonged process of raising the model through back-and-forth interaction, treating the AI as a dependent entity that requires guidance rather than a tool to be commanded. The mechanism relies on the tension between the machine's generalist training and the user's specific stylistic requirements, resolved only through persistent, evidence-based correction over time.

## Context

Sean is currently building a 'Voiceprint Plugin' and writing a Substack series called 'Raising Claude' to document this exact struggle. He has moved away from trying to one-shot his voice into an LLM and is now focusing on the 'Cheese Gauntlet' method—using raw, unfiltered material over hours of interaction to achieve fluency. This concept captures the core lesson that his competitive advantage in job hunting and creative work lies not in finding the perfect prompt, but in mastering the supervision loop that turns generic AI output into a unique personal artifact.

## Evidence

> You can't prompt your taste into a machine. Expecting an LLM to think like you from a description is like expecting a kid to share your taste because you told them to.

> One-shotting gets you decent; the thing that thinks like ME takes hours of back-and-forth, not a clever prompt.
