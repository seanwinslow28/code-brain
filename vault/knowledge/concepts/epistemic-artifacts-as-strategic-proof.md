## Definition

An epistemic artifact functions as a tangible, low-cost verification mechanism that shifts value creation from theoretical promise to demonstrable operational integrity. By generating verifiable proof of underlying system capabilities—such as distinguishing truth from hallucination in real-time—the artifact serves as the primary bridge between technical competence and buyer trust. This structure allows providers to pre-sell based on demonstrated proof rather than abstract claims, effectively turning a diagnostic output into a strategic sales instrument that validates technical claims against ground truth.

## Context

This concept is critical for Sean's agentic-web-startup pilot because it redefines how he must package his service. Instead of selling abstract 'AI accuracy,' he is selling a concrete report that proves his system can find errors others miss. This artifact serves as the bridge between technical capability and buyer trust, allowing him to pre-sell based on demonstrated proof rather than theoretical promise.

## Evidence

> The artifact to pre-sell is the claim-level accuracy report (here are the untrue things assistants tell your buyers), not the journey grade.

> What it proves: the artifact is producible in ~2 hours at $0 and the errors it finds are real, receipted, and buyer-harming.
