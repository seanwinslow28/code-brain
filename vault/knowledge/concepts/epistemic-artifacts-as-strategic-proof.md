## Definition

This concept describes the strategic deployment of verification artifacts—such as purchase sheets, reachability checks, and format badges—not merely as data collection tools, but as tangible proof of competence and operational rigor. In contexts where automated access is blocked or unreliable, the artifact itself becomes the primary signal of trustworthiness to stakeholders or future selves. The value lies not in the raw data point, but in the documented effort to distinguish between 'verified' facts and 'expected' inferences, thereby creating a defensible boundary around what is known versus what is assumed.

## Context

Sean is building Productcraft and needs to demonstrate rigorous due diligence for tier-1 titles. By explicitly documenting why certain data points are 'expected' rather than 'verified,' he creates an epistemic artifact that protects against future failure if the assumption proves wrong, while simultaneously showcasing his methodological discipline to any reviewer or investor.
