## Definition

This mechanism functions as a tangible, low-cost verification layer that shifts value creation from theoretical promise to demonstrable operational integrity. By explicitly documenting the distinction between 'verified' facts and 'expected' inferences, it creates a defensible boundary around what is known versus what is assumed. The artifact itself becomes the primary signal of trustworthiness when automated access is blocked or unreliable, protecting against future failure if assumptions prove wrong.

## Context

Sean is building Productcraft and needs to demonstrate rigorous due diligence for tier-1 titles. By generating these artifacts, he showcases methodological discipline to reviewers or investors while creating a record that protects him from the liability of unverified claims in high-stakes purchasing decisions.
