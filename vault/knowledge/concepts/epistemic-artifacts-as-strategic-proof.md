## Definition

An epistemic artifact is a tangible output generated by an automated system that serves not merely as information delivery, but as verifiable proof of underlying operational integrity. In the context of agentic services, the artifact itself becomes the primary value proposition because it demonstrates the system's ability to distinguish truth from hallucination in real-time. This shifts the sales dynamic from promising future capability to demonstrating present competence through a low-cost, high-fidelity test case that validates the provider's technical claims against ground truth.

## Context

This concept is critical for Sean's agentic-web-startup pilot because it redefines how he must package his service. Instead of selling abstract 'AI accuracy,' he is selling a concrete report that proves his system can find errors others miss. This artifact serves as the bridge between technical capability and buyer trust, allowing him to pre-sell based on demonstrated proof rather than theoretical promise.

## Evidence

> The artifact to pre-sell is the claim-level accuracy report (here are the untrue things assistants tell your buyers), not the journey grade.

> What it proves: the artifact is producible in ~2 hours at $0 and the errors it finds are real, receipted, and buyer-harming.

## Examples

- The pilot produced a scorecard showing Perplexity incorrectly stating a bookstore was open on Mondays despite the site saying closed.
- Claude incorrectly stated a bakery was taking pre-orders when every item showed SOLD OUT.
