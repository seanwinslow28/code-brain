## Definition

The ladder of inference walkthrough is a mechanism for demonstrating epistemic hygiene by explicitly mapping the transition from raw observation to revised belief. It requires the candidate to expose the assumptions and selected meanings that bridge data and action, preventing the hiding of cognitive shortcuts. This structure validates the reliability of the agent's decision-making by showing how it inspects automation failures and converts incidents into learning. It serves as a rubric for each project, ensuring that the narrative includes the validation signal and the resulting belief revision. This makes the agent's internal logic auditable and trustworthy to external stakeholders.

## Context

Sean's work involves complex agent fleets where silent failures or self-congratulatory dashboards can mask underlying issues. By explicitly showing how he debugs his own agent fleet, he demonstrates a maturity in handling uncertainty and failure that is rare in AI product roles. This is particularly valuable for IC roles where the ability to inspect and correct automated systems is paramount.

## Evidence

> Portfolio walkthroughs should show how Sean moves from observation to interpretation to action without hiding the assumptions.

> Add a rubric for each project: observed data → selected meaning → assumption → action → validation signal → revised belief.

> It is especially useful for agentic-engineering IC roles because it shows he can inspect automation failures, avoid self-congratulatory dashboards, and convert incident data into learning.

## Examples

- Mapping the observed data of a failed agent run to the assumption of a timeout.
- Showing the action taken to increase retry limits and the validation signal of success.
- Revising the belief that the system was robust to the new understanding of its fragility.
- Documenting the decision to add a fallback mechanism based on the revised belief.
