## Definition

Strong imitation requires a distortion rule that makes influence survivable, preventing the writer from becoming a mere echo of their precursors. This mechanism involves capturing three distinct fields for each reference voice: what to steal, what to deform, and what to forbid because it becomes cosplay. It operationalizes Harold Bloom’s concept of misreading precursors productively by forcing active deformation rather than passive averaging.
