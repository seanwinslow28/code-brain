## Definition

This concept defines the structural friction that arises when probabilistic computational engines are deployed within interfaces designed for deterministic logic. It requires the product architect to actively construct boundary conditions that mask the inherent variance of model outputs, transforming unpredictable data flows into stable user experiences. The mechanism relies on 'hiding' variability behind robust constraints rather than exposing it as a feature, thereby preventing the collapse of user trust when edge cases trigger unexpected system behaviors.

## Context

Sean is positioning himself as an AI Product Manager who understands that technical uncertainty must be managed through architectural constraints rather than just model tuning. This insight is critical for his job hunt because it demonstrates he can bridge the gap between engineering reality and product reliability, a key differentiator in senior roles.

## Evidence

> The core tension in AI product management lies in the mismatch between the probabilistic nature of AI models and the deterministic expectations users have for software interfaces.

> An AI PM does not merely decide where to insert AI; they decide which AI properties should become part of the user experience and which must be hidden behind product constraints.

## Examples

- Designing a search interface that returns consistent formatting even when the underlying retrieval model varies in confidence scores.
- Implementing fallback logic that silently corrects minor hallucinations before they reach the user, rather than flagging them as errors.
