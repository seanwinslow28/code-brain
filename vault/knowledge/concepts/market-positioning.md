## Definition

Market positioning is the strategic act of defining a unique, specific value proposition that distinguishes a professional from the generic baseline of their peer group. It requires replacing vague, self-congratulatory adjectives with concrete, verifiable claims about specific domains or outcomes. This mechanism functions as a filter for hiring managers, allowing them to instantly identify relevance rather than forcing them to search for signal in noise. The core tension lies between the desire to appear broadly competent and the need to appear specifically indispensable.

## Context

Sean is actively job hunting for AI Product Manager and Agentic Engineering roles. His portfolio must cut through the noise of generic 'AI-fluent' candidates by explicitly stating his specific domain expertise (e.g., commerce, payments, or agentic workflows) rather than relying on broad claims of innovation.

## Evidence

> Hiring managers do not skim to find out that you are a senior designer who cares about impact. They assume that. They skim to find out what makes you worth a call.

> Instead of “innovative designer who drives impact,” say what you actually mean. “I have deep experience in commerce and retail” or “I transformed the world’s best checkout into the world’s first agentic checkout.”

## Examples

- Replacing the generic intro 'Innovative, impactful designer' with the specific claim 'I transformed the world’s best checkout into the world’s first agentic checkout.'
- Identifying that the top two scrolls of a portfolio are the most valuable real estate and must be used for specific differentiation rather than category description.
