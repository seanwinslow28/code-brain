## Definition

This mechanism describes a structural asymmetry where the cost of detecting misinformation has collapsed due to commoditized passive monitoring, while the cost of correcting it remains high due to the syndication trap. The core tension relies on the decoupling of detection from resolution: aggregators resell corrupted feeds, meaning that fixing a single source node fails to propagate corrections downstream. This creates a market gap for upstream provider correction services that bypass the noise of downstream monitoring dashboards.

## Context

Sean must position his product not as another monitoring dashboard but as an upstream data correction service to avoid the commoditization trap. The business model must account for the high friction of dealing with data providers, which may require manual intervention or specialized APIs.

## Evidence

> fixing the original listing fixes nothing when an aggregator resold the feed to dozens of sites; the working move is upstream to the one or two data providers per vertical.

> passive AI-visibility monitoring is crowded AND commoditizing. Last-30-day chatter names AIclicks, AirOps, Qwairy, Gauge, Peec, Otterly, Scrunch, Ahrefs Brand Radar, SE Ranking, Semrush, Rank Prompt, plus a free open-source 6-model tracker (Bright Data).
