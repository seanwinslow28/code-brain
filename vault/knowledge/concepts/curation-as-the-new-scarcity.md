## Definition

This mechanism defines an economic shift where value is derived not from generating content, but from the rigorous filtering of information abundance through costly signaling. Authority is established by visibly refusing, testing, and risking error on specific items, thereby demonstrating judgment as labor rather than mere opinion. This approach transforms exclusions into authored signals that distinguish high-fidelity curation from generic ranking algorithms.

## Context

Sean must differentiate his output (Code-Brain, VoicePrint) from generic AI-tool punditry by focusing on the editorial rubric and the 'curation ledger' of rejected tools. This strategy serves as a stronger proof of competence than simple recommendations, addressing the trust deficit caused by information overload.

## Evidence

> A curator is trusted less by what they recommend than by what they visibly refuse, test, and risk being wrong about.

> Curation becomes defensible when the filter has a house style: exclusions feel authored, not merely ranked.

## Examples

- Publishing a 'curation ledger' for Code-Brain showing accepted/rejected tools, tests run, failure notes, and why each tool earned cognitive load.
- Creating a Substack series format called 'The Code-Brain Canon' where tools are reviewed through a consistent editorial rubric.
