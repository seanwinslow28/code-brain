## Definition

This mechanism treats the deliberate introduction of constraints or errors not as bugs to be fixed, but as generative forces that disrupt automated complacency. By injecting 'productive obstruction' into a pipeline, the system prevents the agent from optimizing for speed at the expense of taste, forcing a re-evaluation of the output against aesthetic standards rather than technical throughput. This creates a tension where the agent must navigate ambiguity and incomplete information, mirroring Keats' concept of remaining in uncertainties without reaching for fact or reason.

## Context

Sean's creative automation pipeline risks becoming a 'slop' generator if it only optimizes for throughput. By integrating Oblique Strategies as a primitive, he ensures that his agents retain a distinct creative voice rather than converging on generic, high-probability outputs. This is critical for his Substack and portfolio work, where differentiation relies on the ability to disturb the obvious.

## Evidence

> Eno mode gives Sean a distinct creative-automation thesis: agents should not only execute taste; they should disturb taste on schedule.

> Before rerolling, draw/choose one obstruction: remove the obvious subject, change the production material, preserve the error, invert the palette, slow the motion, make the background tell the story.

## Examples

- Inverting the palette of a generated image to force a new compositional focus.
- Preserving a rendering error as a stylistic element rather than retrying the generation.
