## Definition

This mechanism describes how narrative structures sustain attention by leveraging the Ovsiankina effect, where an unresolved task creates a psychological urge to complete it. The surviving element is not the memory of the claim but the motivational version that drives resumption. By opening loops and delaying closure, the communicator creates a tension that the audience feels compelled to resolve, thereby maintaining engagement over longer periods.

## Context

Sean's job hunt and creative projects require sustained attention from recruiters and audiences. Using open loops allows him to structure his narratives (resumes, interviews, stories) in a way that keeps the listener engaged until the final payoff, making his technical arguments more memorable and persuasive.

## Evidence

> What survives is the motivational version (the Ovsiankina resumption tendency), not the memory claim.

> This balance is maintained through open loops that generate motivational tension, ensuring the narrative remains intellectually rigorous yet emotionally accessible.

## Examples

- Introducing a complex problem early in a narrative and resolving it only at the end.
- Using unresolved questions to drive the reader through dense technical sections.
