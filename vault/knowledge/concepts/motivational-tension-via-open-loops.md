## Definition

Open loops function not by enhancing memory retention, but by generating motivational tension that compels the reader to seek resolution. This mechanism relies on the Ovsiankina resumption tendency, where an interrupted task creates a psychological need to complete it, rather than the Zeigarnik effect's claim of superior recall. The intensity of this tension scales with the perceived proximity of the gap between what the reader knows and what they want to know, requiring the gap to feel small and closeable rather than a vast chasm.

## Context

Sean is building a Substack skill to maintain reader engagement during a job hunt. Understanding that open loops drive motivation rather than memory allows him to structure his narrative arcs to keep readers returning, rather than relying on gimmicks that fail replication studies.

## Evidence

> What survives is the motivational version (the Ovsiankina resumption tendency), not the memory claim. So: open loops work because they create motivational tension the reader wants to resolve, not because they magically stick in memory.

> Curiosity intensity scales with proximity: the gap must feel small and closeable, not a vast chasm.

## Examples

- Structuring a story beat so the reader feels the gap is closeable, creating a specific type of curiosity.
- Avoiding the justification of techniques with 'the Zeigarnik effect proves...' because the memory claim fails replication.
