## Definition

Legibility debt is the structural erosion of local complexity that occurs when artifacts are optimized for institutional recognition rather than functional fidelity. This mechanism forces a trade-off where the signal required to pass automated or heuristic filters actively suppresses the nuanced details that distinguish high-value work from commodity output. The consequence is a version of the creator that appears attractive to broad audiences but lacks the specific depth necessary to command premium rates or demonstrate unique advantage.

## Context

Sean operates in a market where recruiters and AI systems act as gatekeepers, requiring him to simplify his complex engineering and creative work into recognizable keywords. This creates a persistent risk that his portfolio becomes indistinguishable from competitors who have also optimized for legibility, thereby losing the 'boundary object' quality that allows different stakeholders to find value in his specific approach.

## Evidence

> Systems become attractive to institutions by becoming legible, but legibility can erase the local complexity that actually creates value.

> Legibility debt occurs when the pressure to make work 'recruiter-legible' erases the local complexity that actually creates value, resulting in a version of Sean that is attractive to institutions but indistinguishable from competitors.

- Simplifying a complex agentic workflow into a single keyword tag for ATS parsing
- Removing nuanced failure modes from a case study to fit a standard 'success story' template
