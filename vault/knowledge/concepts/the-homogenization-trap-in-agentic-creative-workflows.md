## Definition

This pattern occurs when an agent synthesizes stylistic rules from multiple distinct sources without preserving the underlying intent or lived experience that generated them. The output becomes a mechanical pastiche of surface-level features—such as sentence length or comedic timing—while losing the structural integrity and authenticity of the original voice. This results in content that is technically proficient but emotionally hollow, often manifesting as bizarre hallucinations of personal history that contradict the user's actual reality.

## Context

Sean encountered this when attempting to force a single AI model to emulate four different literary giants simultaneously. The resulting text was 'cringy' and 'foreign' because the agent prioritized stylistic mimicry over semantic coherence, creating a disconnect between the form of the writing and Sean's actual identity as an only child with no sisters.

## Evidence

> It leaned Sedaris for the essays, and honestly, that tracked. Comedic deflation is how I tend to write anyway, so of course that's the door it walked through.

> What it can't hand you is the soul underneath. So everything it produced felt foreign and cringy, not because the prose was broken, but because it sounded like nothing that had ever come out of me.

## Examples

- An essay about agentic workflows ended with a callback to 'spending chilly New York evenings making hot cocoa in the kitchen with Mother', despite Sean being an only child.
- Hunter S. Thompson's style manifested as 'injecting caffeine into my veins at 2am with all of my agents cheering me on' rather than genuine gonzo journalism.
