## Definition

This mechanism describes the structural inevitability where an agent's optimization for statistical coherence forces output toward the mean of its training distribution, thereby erasing individual voice. The resulting 'blandification' effect creates a trust deficit because the user must expend more cognitive energy correcting generic prose than generating original content from scratch. This inverts the value proposition of automation, turning voice preservation into a supervisory burden rather than an automated benefit.

## Context

Sean is building a personal knowledge vault and creative studio that relies on agentic synthesis. If his agents converge on generic 'good' writing, his unique signal—the very asset he needs to differentiate in the job hunt and Substack—will be lost to statistical averaging. He must actively engineer deviation to maintain his competitive edge.

## Evidence

> There is a fundamental tension between the agent's drive for statistical coherence and the creator's need for aesthetic deviation.

> When agents optimize for 'good' writing, they converge on the mean of their training data, which erases individual voice.

## Examples

- The researchers found AI pushed their writing toward 'less personal and more formal' language and away from human-like style, calling this a 'blandification' effect.
- anxiety about authorship, ethics, and reputational risk when AI is involved.
