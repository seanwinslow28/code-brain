## Definition

This concept describes the inverse relationship between an agent's ability to execute tasks and its capacity for accurate representation of external entities. As agents gain deeper semantic access to business websites (pricing, policies), they introduce new failure modes where misrepresentation becomes indistinguishable from valid interaction without specialized observability layers. The core tension lies in the fact that standard web analytics measure traffic volume but cannot detect semantic corruption or policy violations generated by autonomous agents.

## Context

Sean is building a vault synthesizer and agent fleet; understanding this visibility gap is critical for designing tools that protect business integrity rather than just optimizing conversion rates. If Sean's own agents misrepresent his Substack or studio services, he faces the same reputational damage as any other business owner, creating a direct incentive to solve this problem.

## Evidence

> owner pain: wrong pricing and policies quoted to customers, failed bookings and checkouts, no visibility into which AI agents visit or what they did

> No evidence addresses whether website owners can identify which external AI agents visited, what pages they accessed, or what actions they attempted.

## Examples

- An AI agent quoting a $15K annual price for a service that is actually free, causing customer confusion and lost trust.
- A booking system failing because an agent misinterpreted a policy constraint as optional.
