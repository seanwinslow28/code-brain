## Definition

This mechanism describes an inverse relationship where an agent's capacity for accurate semantic representation degrades as its ability to execute tasks deepens, creating a blind spot for business owners. Standard analytics measure traffic volume but cannot detect semantic corruption or policy violations generated by autonomous agents, meaning misrepresentation becomes indistinguishable from valid interaction without specialized observability layers. The core tension lies in the fact that while agents gain deeper access to pricing and policies, they introduce new failure modes where the owner has no visibility into which AI agents visited or what actions they attempted.

## Context

Sean is building a vault synthesizer and agent fleet; understanding this visibility gap is critical for designing tools that protect business integrity rather than just optimizing conversion rates. If Sean's own agents misrepresent his Substack or studio services, he faces the same reputational damage as any other business owner, creating a direct incentive to solve this problem before it scales.

## Evidence

> owner pain: wrong pricing and policies quoted to customers, failed bookings and checkouts, no visibility into which AI agents visit or what they did

> No evidence addresses whether website owners can identify which external AI agents visited, what pages they accessed, or what actions they attempted.

## Examples

- An AI agent quoting a $15K annual price for a service that is actually free, causing customer confusion and lost trust.
- A booking system failing because an agent misinterpreted a policy constraint as optional.
