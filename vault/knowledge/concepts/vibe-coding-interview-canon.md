## Definition

This concept describes the psychometric framework through which technical competence is evaluated in modern AI hiring, specifically highlighting the tension between cultural signaling and predictive validity. It argues that 'vibe-coding' interviews function as instruments with specific biases, including adverse impact and criterion contamination, which often prioritize stylistic fluency over actual engineering capability. The underlying pattern reveals that these interviews are less about assessing technical depth and more about verifying cultural fit within a specific coding subculture.

## Context

Sean needs to critically evaluate the validity of the interview processes he encounters, recognizing that 'vibe' is often a proxy for conformity rather than competence. This awareness allows him to prepare for interviews by focusing on demonstrating substantive engineering judgment rather than just matching the interviewer's stylistic preferences.

## Evidence

> The current note treats vibe-coding interviews as a cultural signal. Add the psychometric counterframe: interviews are instruments with predictive validity, adverse impact, criterion contamination, and work-sample fidelity.

> There is a fundamental tension between the psychometric requirement for predictive validity in hiring and the situated nature of actual AI product work.

## Examples

- An interview question that asks candidates to write code in a specific style without testing its functional correctness
- A hiring process that rejects candidates who cannot match the interviewer's casual tone despite strong technical answers
