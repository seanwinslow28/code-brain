## Definition

This concept defines a dual-layered analytical framework where macro-level shifts in the basis of competition are detected alongside micro-level mappings of reinforcing operational activities. It posits that sustainable advantage arises not from static positioning but from the compounding defensibility of an activity system that makes imitation structurally difficult. The mechanism requires distinguishing between transient market noise and fundamental inflection points that alter the rules of engagement, ensuring that strategic responses address the underlying structural changes rather than superficial symptoms.

## Context

Sean must frame his career transition as a response to a strategic inflection point rather than a personal reinvention. His portfolio must demonstrate the compounding nature of his activity system to show defensible advantage.

## Evidence

> Competitive analysis should not only map rivals; it should identify when the basis of competition is changing.

> Which operating activities reinforce each other, and which tradeoffs make imitation hard?
