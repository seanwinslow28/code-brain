## Definition

This concept defines a fundamental boundary in agentic interaction where deterministic protocols fail to capture aesthetic qualities because they rely on explicit parameterization rather than iterative emergence. Descriptive prompts attempt to define an outcome through static inputs, which is insufficient for abstract traits like taste that require resolution over time. Raising involves creating conditions where the desired quality emerges through feedback loops, treating the agent as a cultivator of context rather than a printer of instructions.

## Context

Sean's infrastructure excels at executing clear, deterministic tasks but struggles with creative domains that require rejection-based calibration. Recognizing this distinction prevents him from wasting effort trying to 'prompt' taste into existence through better descriptions, shifting focus instead to the structural split between operational automation and creative emergence.

## Evidence

> You can't prompt taste into an LLM; it's raising, not describing.

> Sean's infrastructure relies on deterministic protocol instrumentation, where agents execute tasks based on explicit, verifiable inputs.

## Examples

- The failure of agents to 'sound like me' despite months of teaching them to run his life.
- The structural split between operational domains that succeed through automation and creative domains that require rejection-based calibration.
