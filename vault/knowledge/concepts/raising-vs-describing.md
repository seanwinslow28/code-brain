## Definition

This concept distinguishes between two modes of agent interaction: describing a static state versus raising a dynamic process. Descriptive prompts attempt to define an outcome through explicit parameters, which fails for abstract qualities like taste because they lack sufficient resolution. Raising involves creating conditions where the desired quality emerges over time through iterative feedback, treating the agent as a cultivator rather than a printer.

## Context

Sean's infrastructure is built on deterministic protocols that excel at describing and executing clear tasks. However, his creative work requires aesthetic emergence, which cannot be fully specified by these protocols. Recognizing this distinction prevents him from wasting effort trying to 'prompt' taste into existence through better descriptions.

## Evidence

> You can't prompt taste into an LLM; it's raising, not describing.

> Sean's infrastructure relies on deterministic protocol instrumentation, where agents execute tasks based on explicit, verifiable inputs.

## Examples

- The failure of agents to 'sound like me' despite months of teaching them to run his life.
- The structural split between operational domains that succeed through automation and creative domains that require rejection-based calibration.
