## Definition

This mechanism distinguishes between two modes of instruction: describing provides explicit, positive definitions of a target state, while raising involves cultivating a latent space through iterative feedback and environmental conditioning. In the context of LLMs, descriptive prompts fail to capture nuanced taste because they attempt to map high-dimensional aesthetic qualities onto low-dimensional token sequences. Instead, 'raising' treats the model as an organism to be conditioned, where the user's role is to curate the environment and reject deviations rather than define the endpoint.

## Context

Sean’s infrastructure struggles when applying operational logic (description) to creative tasks (raising), leading to a failure in agents producing content that sounds like him despite precise instructions. This tension creates a bottleneck where automation works for deterministic tasks but fails for aesthetic ones, requiring a shift from prompt engineering to curation.

## Evidence

> You can't prompt taste into an LLM; it's raising, not describing.

> The tension lies in the fact that the same tools used to stabilize his daily operations fail when applied to aesthetic tasks.

## Examples

- The inability of agents to 'sound like me' despite months of teaching them to run life tasks.
- The requirement for a non-deterministic, iterative calibration process for creative output.
