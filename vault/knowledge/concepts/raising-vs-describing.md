## Definition

This distinction separates the act of defining a target state (describing) from the act of guiding a process toward an outcome through iterative feedback (raising). Descriptive prompts assume the system can infer intent from static definitions, which fails for complex, subjective tasks like creative writing. Raising involves setting up a dynamic environment where the system's output is continuously evaluated against a standard, allowing for emergent refinement. This mechanism highlights the limitation of LLMs in handling taste without an external evaluation function.

## Context

Sean recognizes that he cannot simply describe his desired voice to an agent; he must raise it through interaction. This insight is critical for his job hunt and creative studio work, as it dictates that he must build systems that support iterative feedback rather than one-shot generation.

## Evidence

> You can't prompt taste into an LLM; it's raising, not describing.

> Sean's infrastructure relies on deterministic protocol instrumentation to manage operational tasks, but this same rigidity prevents the emergence of aesthetic qualities that require non-deterministic calibration.

## Examples

- The failure of agents to sound like Sean despite months of training on his life data.
- The need for Sean to maintain separate mental models for operations and creativity.
