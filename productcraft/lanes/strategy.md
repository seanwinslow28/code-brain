# Lane manifest — Strategy

Mandatory first read for the [Product Strategist](../bench/product-strategist.md). Entries
are title + pointer + one-line when-to-read — shelf labels, never the books: the content
lives in the private corpus (`../corpus/`, gitignored), so pointers resolve only on a
machine that has it (degradation ladder otherwise). Follow only the pointers relevant to
the task. Seats stop at `## Reading path`; that section is the owner's.

## What strategy is, and what it is not

- **Vision vs. Strategy** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — a leader hands you a vision and asks for a strategy, or the two words are being used interchangeably in the room.
- **Product strategy: the definition, the fifteen contrasts, and what is not strategy** — [`shreyas-doshi.md`](../corpus/canon/shreyas-doshi.md) — a document is about to be called a strategy and you need a test that settles it before the review.
- **Strategy vs. execution, and prioritization as a misdiagnosed strategy problem** — [`shreyas-doshi.md`](../corpus/canon/shreyas-doshi.md) — a team insists it has a resourcing or prioritization problem and you suspect the diagnosis is wrong.

## Focus, and the bets

- **Product Strategy: Focus, Insights, Action, Management** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — a portfolio carries more priorities than teams and you have to argue for cutting rather than sequencing.
- **Product Market Fit vs. Product Vision** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — someone wants the whole vision shipped to the whole market in one move.
- **Get to the core of the thing** — [`shreyas-doshi.md`](../corpus/canon/shreyas-doshi.md) — a strategy discussion has climbed to an altitude where everyone sounds smart and nobody names a customer.

## Evidence under the strategy

- **Insight Sources for Product Strategy** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — a strategy draft has no evidence under it and you need to name where the evidence should come from.
- **Discovery, the Focusing Illusion, and the Customer Problems Stack Rank** — [`shreyas-doshi.md`](../corpus/canon/shreyas-doshi.md) — customers have said yes to the idea and you need to know whether the problem ranks high enough to fund.

## Judgment, and how strategies fail

- **Product sense as the upstream capability** — [`shreyas-doshi.md`](../corpus/canon/shreyas-doshi.md) — deciding what a product person should be graded on, or defending judgment against tool-fluency as the differentiator.
- **Why products fail: the builder's cognitive bias and the Bias-for-Building fallacy** — [`shreyas-doshi.md`](../corpus/canon/shreyas-doshi.md) — before greenlighting more feature work on a product whose adoption never arrived.
- **Strong Opinions, Loosely Held** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — before citing any pre-2020 SVPG position; check whether the author has since revised the one you are leaning on.

## Book layer

Both of this lane's books are ingested. Rumelt supplies the instrument you write with and
the instrument you audit with; Dunford supplies the positioning method the free layer has
no entry for at all.

- **Rumelt, *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* — ch. 3, 5** — [`books/good-strategy-bad-strategy/`](../corpus/books/good-strategy-bad-strategy/) — auditing a strategy document, or writing one from diagnosis through to coordinated action.
- **Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* — ch. 2, 7, 9** — [`books/obviously-awesome/`](../corpus/books/obviously-awesome/) — choosing a market category, a competitive frame, or a differentiation claim.
- **Cagan & Jones, *EMPOWERED* — ch. 12, 37–39, 48–51, 62–69** — [`books/empowered/`](../corpus/books/empowered/) — a company has goals and a roadmap but nothing between them, or focus needs an intervention.
- **Patton, *User Story Mapping* — ch. 2** — [`books/user-story-mapping/`](../corpus/books/user-story-mapping/) — defining an MVP by outcome rather than feature count, or slicing a release roadmap.
- **Lombardo, McCarthy, Ryan & Connors, *Product Roadmaps Relaunched* — ch. 4, 7** — [`books/product-roadmaps-relaunched/`](../corpus/books/product-roadmaps-relaunched/) — grounding a roadmap in mission, vision and business objectives, or choosing a prioritization framework.

Read the corpus honestly: every figure in Rumelt was destroyed in capture and is described
from prose only, and the ch. 10 policy-to-target table there is a reconstruction from the
book's classroom dialogue, not a transcription. The *Obviously Awesome* in the corpus is
the **second edition** (2026), which restructures the first edition's ten steps into five
components plus five steps and adds the Pre-Work chapter — anything citing the 2019
structure will not match. EMPOWERED's strategic-context diagram is a prose reconstruction,
and its case study is a composite the author assembled, not one real quarter.

### Next

- Lafley & Martin, *Playing to Win* — the five-question cascade is a clean scaffold, but Rumelt covers the same ground first.
- Helmer, *7 Powers* — a defensibility checklist; buy when a strategy needs a moat argument.
- Rumelt, *The Crux* — same author; the Strategy Foundry workshop format is the reason to add it.
- Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap* — scores across four seats and anchors none; the best learner's book in the set, so it may enter as Sean's reading before it enters the corpus.
- Dikkers, *Creativity Machine* — contingent hold: the only candidate covering creative option generation; revisit if the Strategist's option generation feels thin after a couple of engagements.
