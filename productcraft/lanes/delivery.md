# Lane manifest — Delivery & Execution

Mandatory first read for the [Delivery & Execution seat](../bench/delivery-execution.md).
Entries are title + pointer + one-line when-to-read — shelf labels, never the books: the
content lives in the private corpus (`../corpus/`, gitignored), so pointers resolve only on
a machine that has it (degradation ladder otherwise). Follow only the pointers relevant to
the task. Seats stop at `## Reading path`; that section is the owner's.

Three sources, thin by count and durable by nature. SVPG supplies the argument against
roadmaps and the team-objectives model, Wodtke the whole OKR apparatus (*Radical Focus* is
a tier-2 book the studio has not bought, so the free layer is all this lane has on OKRs),
and Cutler the flow, sizing and diagnostic material no other source in the studio holds.

## Instead of a roadmap

- **The Alternative to Roadmaps, plus the Roadmap Alternative FAQ** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — a stakeholder demands a feature roadmap and you need both the replacement and answers to the eight standard objections.
- **High-Integrity Commitments** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — the business needs a hard date and you must give one without quietly reinstating a roadmap.
- **The predictability trap and the eight-bet roadmap** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — leadership's stated problem is say/do ratio or missed commitments, and before proposing any roadmap or planning cadence change.

## Objectives and key results

- **The Team Objectives series** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — writing or reviewing team OKRs and it is contested who sets the objective and who sets the key results.
- **Ambition: Roof Shot vs. Moon Shot** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — one team is being held to the same bar for a long-shot bet and a safe optimization.
- **Writing the Objective / Writing the Key Results** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — before drafting or red-lining any O or KR, and when a reviewer needs the output-vs-outcome test to reject a task wearing a result's clothes.
- **The Confidence Mechanic** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — a target has to be set with no baseline, a team is arguing about how hard a KR should be, or nobody can say whether the quarter is on track.
- **Scope and alignment: how many, and cascading vs. decoupling** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — the org is growing, teams are queued behind an approval step, or someone proposes one OKR per department.
- **What isn't an OKR: health metrics, KPIs, and vital ongoing work** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — a saturated service team has been handed a stretch goal, or a team is asked for OKRs in a quarter where nothing needs to change.

## Running the goal cadence

- **Running the OKR-setting meeting** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — the week before you facilitate a goal-setting session; carries the pre-work ask, the six timed steps and the worksheet's check questions.
- **The weekly cadence: Monday commitments and Friday wins** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — a set has been written but nothing is keeping it alive, or the weekly meeting has decayed into a status readout.
- **Grading, the 60–70% rule, and the quarter-end learning loop** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — two weeks before a quarter closes, or the first time someone proposes wiring OKR scores to compensation.

## When goals are not working

- **Failure modes and how each is detected** — [`wodtke-okrs.md`](../corpus/canon/wodtke-okrs.md) — an OKR program is visibly not working and you need to name the specific pathology before prescribing a fix.
- **Outcomes Are Hard** — [`svpg.md`](../corpus/canon/svpg.md) — an outcome or OKR rollout has stalled and you need the known failure modes before diagnosing your own.
- **The three-years-later correction to the feature factory** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — before recommending an outcome shift, so you cost it honestly at 12–18 months and do not mistake understaffing for bad intent.

## Diagnosing the delivery environment

- **The twelve signs of a feature factory** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — before diagnosing a new client's delivery environment; the signs are scoreable off roadmap format, board columns and whether anything shipped was ever killed.
- **Demand mix before delivery prescriptions** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — before recommending any intake, discovery or WIP practice, to check what "good" even means in that team's input conditions.
- **Ten prioritization traps with their fixes** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — a pass over an existing backlog, quarter plan or portfolio when something feels wrong but nobody can name it.

## Flow, WIP and batch size

- **Why lowering WIP fails, and the boards that show it** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — a team has agreed to limit WIP and has not, or you need to read overload off a board rather than argue about it.
- **Three slots, three-day batches, and the Shape Up constraint set** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — before writing any WIP limit, cycle length, intake rule or dependency policy into a delivery design.
- **Capacity is not hours, and allocation is an output** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — the argument is about what percentage goes to debt or platform work, before conceding that framing.
- **Points, hours and the rollup paradox** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — a stakeholder wants capacity sliced by initiative, or you are deciding what a delivery design should ask teams to track.

## Sizing and slicing

- **The 1s-and-3s ladder and the two uncertainty curves** — [`john-cutler.md`](../corpus/canon/john-cutler.md) — a team is asked to commit to a 1–3 month chunk, or work is clear this week and on a slide next year but incoherent in between.

## Book layer

All three of this lane's books are ingested, and they close the two silences the free layer
named — story mapping, and the theme-based roadmap artifact.

- **Singer, *Shape Up* — ch. 8, 11, 12, 13, 14** — [`books/shape-up/`](../corpus/books/shape-up/) — sizing cycles, slicing work into scopes, reporting progress, or cutting scope to a deadline.
- **Patton, *User Story Mapping* — ch. 4, 11, 16, 17** — [`books/user-story-mapping/`](../corpus/books/user-story-mapping/) — sizing, splitting, sequencing or planning work into buildable, risk-aware slices.
- **Lombardo, McCarthy, Ryan & Connors, *Product Roadmaps Relaunched* — ch. 2, 6, 10** — [`books/product-roadmaps-relaunched/`](../corpus/books/product-roadmaps-relaunched/) — composing the roadmap artifact, signalling confidence and stage, or absorbing mid-flight change.
- **Grove, *High Output Management* — ch. 1, 2, 6** — [`books/high-output-management/`](../corpus/books/high-output-management/) — bottleneck scheduling, indicator design and forecasting, and the planning process that became OKRs.
- **Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* — ch. 4, 12, 15** — [`books/trustworthy-online-controlled-experiments/`](../corpus/books/trustworthy-online-controlled-experiments/) — planning a staged rollout, release safety rings, or platform maturity investment.
- **Hughes Johnson, *Scaling People* — ch. 2, 4** — [`books/scaling-people/`](../corpus/books/scaling-people/) — setting goals and cadence, structuring teams, or unblocking cross-team dependencies.

All three primary books are diagram-led and **every figure was lost in extraction** — the
hill charts and breadboards in *Shape Up*, 134 images in Patton (map anatomies, the
Opportunity Canvas layout, the skateboard-to-car illustration), 92 in Lombardo, whose
roadmap exhibits *are* images. Each is reconstructed from prose and flagged in place; treat
any visual-layout claim as inference from text. The sharpest single loss is Lombardo's ROI
scorecards — the narrative explains why feature A beat B and C, but the numeric cells are
gone. Patton also reports two contested statistics as the book states them (Standish's
64–75% unused features, the 75–90% startup-failure figure), both flagged with their vintage.
