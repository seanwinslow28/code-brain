# Story Mechanics: Cited Reference

## The evidence floor (read first)

Almost all writing craft is **doctrine and practitioner lore, not controlled research**. Treat the mechanics below as high-confidence *heuristics*. Tags: **[science]** peer-reviewed, **[craft]** authority doctrine, **[lore]** practitioner/direct-response folklore.

**The Zeigarnik honesty note.** The one engagement mechanic that was actually lab-tested at scale, the Zeigarnik effect ("unfinished loops are better remembered"), **fails replication** (2025 *Nature HSSC* meta-analysis: no reliable memory advantage for interrupted tasks). What survives is the *motivational* resumption tendency (Ovsiankina), not the memory claim. So open loops work because they create **tension the reader wants to resolve**, not because they magically stick. Never justify a loop with "the Zeigarnik effect proves they'll remember it."

## The six mechanics, sourced

1. **But/Therefore** [craft]. Trey Parker's rule: between beats you must be able to insert *but* (conflict) or *therefore* (consequence); only *and then* = dead story. McKee's deeper version: the engine of story is the gap between expectation and result; turning points live in that rift and flip the scene's value.

2. **Cold open / in medias res** [craft]. Starting mid-action raises a question that functions as a contract ("this will be answered"). Only works *with* a satisfying payoff. Sugarman's complement [lore]: the first line's only job is to get the second line read ("grease the chute").

3. **Open-loop budget** [lore + science-corrected]. Loewenstein's information-gap theory [science]: curiosity is a perceived gap between what you know and what you want to know; intensity scales with *proximity* (the gap must feel small and closeable). Practitioner consensus: in short form close loops within ~1-3 paragraphs or readers disengage. (See Zeigarnik note above for why the *memory* rationale is wrong.)

4. **Closeable gap, not chasm** [science]. From Loewenstein: curiosity rises when the missing piece feels attainable and the reader already half-knows the domain. A vast vague promise ("the secret of great writing") kills curiosity; a small specific one ("the one line that broke the build") ignites it.

5. **Withhold the rescue** [craft]. Sol Stein: "Take your reader to the cliff's edge, hang your hero by his fingertips, avoid rescuing him as long as possible." McKee: scenes complicate progressively, past points of no return.

6. **Slippery-slide section ends** [lore]. Sugarman: every sentence/section ends with forward pull, never a clean stop. Apply the but/therefore logic at the sentence seam, not just the beat level.

**Particularity** [craft] (Stein) cuts across all six: one precise, never-quite-seen-this-way detail does the engagement work generic description cannot. This is the same force as McKee's archetype-vs-stereotype distinction (the anti-formula guard).

## The four scaffolds and when each fits

Apply as **form, not formula** (McKee: "all notions of foolproof story models are nonsense"; Truby: externally-imposed structure produces "hopelessly generic, formulaic" stories, organic structure grows from the idea). The scaffold sets beat ORDER; it never dictates phrasing.

- **Problem-Struggle-Fix** (default for maker/technical/AI-PM posts). The real problem you hit, the attempts that failed, the fix that worked. Folds naturally into a blameless post-mortem, the highest-leverage hiring signal. Best when the post IS the solving of a real itch.

- **Before-After-Bridge**. The painful before-state, the better after-state, the bridge that connects them. The bridge is the value. Best for transformation stories where the contrast is the hook.

- **In-Medias-Res Quest** (Thompson/Gonzo-friendly). Cold open mid-crisis, backfill context in fragments, resolve. Best when the moment of failure is vivid and the chronology can be scrambled for tension.

- **Pixar pitch** (Once upon a time / Every day / Until one day / Because of that / Until finally). Best for arc-heavy personal narratives where a stable world is disrupted and re-formed.

## Anti-formula: the seam must stay invisible

The failure mode (LinkedIn "broetry"): hook → staged line → "and that's when I realized..." → CTA. The algorithm rewards the template; the template homogenizes every voice into one cadence. Writers using rigid beat-sheets (Save the Cat) report output that feels "more stale, less unique, a dumbed-down version of my own style."

Two guards, both applied to the beat map (not the prose, which this skill never writes):

- **Specificity check** (McKee archetype vs stereotype): archetype = universal experience in a *specific, lived* expression; stereotype keeps the skeleton and strips the specific surface. Every beat must carry a detail specific to THIS story.
- **Nameable-template check** (structure as scaffolding the reader feels but never sees): if a reader could reverse-engineer your template, the scaffold has leaked. Bend the canonical order: start later, reorder a reveal, fold two beats.

**Constraint amplifies voice, it does not suppress it** [science-adjacent]: the sonnet's 14 lines, *Green Eggs and Ham* from a 50-word bet; a fixed exterior focuses sentence-level invention. The beat map is the sonnet's fixed shape; the voice layer improvises freely inside it.

## Why the handoff is lossy on prose by design

Zinsser: "adding style is like adding a toupee, at second glance it doesn't look right." Voice is native material, never bolted onto a structural draft. Le Guin: voice lives at the sentence/syllable level (rhythm, sound, syntax), *below* the level any beat-framework operates on. Therefore the beat map must NOT carry drafted sentences forward: if it did, architecture's default phrasing would survive and flatten the voice. Pass beats; let the voice layer author every line fresh.

## The 2026-08-28 research pass (mechanics 2, 6, 7 and the evidence floor)

Full note: [`vault/20_projects/research/2026-08-28-story-hooks-and-narrative-through-line.md`](../../../../vault/20_projects/research/2026-08-28-story-hooks-and-narrative-through-line.md). Commissioned after a draft passed every but/therefore seam and still read as "I did this, then this."

**Openings carry two obligations** [craft]. A question *and* a promise about what the piece will feel like — named *contract* by Nelson and Banaszynski, with Poe's unity of effect (1846) as the ancestor. Nelson's own limit, kept for honesty: opening lines "only get you so far." A good first line is not evidence of a working piece.

**Orientation, and the dimension asymmetry** [science]. The event-indexing model tracks five dimensions — time, space, protagonist, causality, intentionality (Zwaan, Langston & Graesser, *Psychological Science* 6:292–297, 1995). Readers reliably slow at temporal, causal, goal and protagonist discontinuities but **not** spatial ones (Rinck & Weber, *Memory & Cognition*). Hence: place is cheap to withhold, the other four are not.

**Given-new contract** [science]. Clark & Haviland (1977): information presented as *given* whose antecedent the reader cannot locate forces a bridging inference at measurable cost, and can fail outright. This is the mechanism behind an opening that reads as rambling, and it is checkable referent by referent.

**The turn** [craft]. McKee's gate on a scene: if the value-charge is unchanged end to end, nothing happened. This is mechanic 6 and it is the one the file was missing.

**Terminology** [correction]. What this skill calls a *beat* is McKee's *scene*; his *beat* is an action/reaction exchange inside one. Corrected in SKILL.md 2026-08-28; the old wording made the McKee citation wrong.

**Situation vs story** [craft]. Gornick: the *situation* is what happened, the *story* is what the writer came to say about it. Now a required Handoff Block field.

**A live disagreement, recorded rather than resolved** [craft]. The journalistic nut graf (orientation gets its own early paragraph) against Gardner's fictive dream (exposition breaks the spell). Scanlan argues both sides across two publications. What both camps share: orientation lands early. Whether it gets its own beat is a form choice, not a correctness question. Do not let this file pretend the argument is settled.

## Second honesty note: reader abandonment [industry data]

Sitting beside the Zeigarnik note, and for the same reason.

- **There is no empirical literature on where readers abandon essays or narrative nonfiction.** What exists is 2013 Chartbeat scroll telemetry (38% bounce, median scroll ~50%) and Jellybooks e-book completion data (40–50%, with loss often before chapter 1). Neither is about essays and neither is peer-reviewed. Tagged `[industry data]`, a tier this file did not previously have.
- **The mind-wandering effect on expository text (r = −0.21) goes to null under trait-based measurement.** Measurement-fragile. Do not build a gate on it.
- **Never cite narrative transportation or the Narrative Engagement Measure as evidence that a structure makes people finish.** Both are measured on people who already finished. That is the same category error this file already corrects for Zeigarnik, and it is the easier one to make because the constructs sound like they are about engagement.
- The **"~1-3 beats" loop-closure window** in mechanic 3 has no empirical support. It stays, tagged `[lore]`, because it is a useful working bound — not because anyone measured it.

**What the pass did not change.** The anti-formula guard, form-not-formula treatment of the four scaffolds, and the chain contract all survive contact with the sources and are reinforced by them. Freytag was considered and stays out. Vonnegut's sentence-level rules stay downstream where they belong.
