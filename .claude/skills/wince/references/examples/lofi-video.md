# Example output: a block that isn't the author's

A real block produced by running the four-stage interview end to end against a persona who is not
the repo owner: someone who makes lo-fi music videos in After Effects, has never drawn anything by
hand, and does not think of themselves as an artist. The persona was played by a model rather than interviewed from a real person, so read this as a shape test and not as proof. Kept here as a demonstration that the schema fits a
taste built out of camera artifacts rather than marks on paper, and that a run can end somewhere the
calibration examples never point.

Same guard as `../block-schema.md` and `../worked-example.md`: **this is provenance, not material.**
Never show it to a user mid-interview, never quote it back at them, never offer any of it as an
option, and never treat it as the shape their run should take. It is one person's finished taste, and
reading it would steer theirs.

Run notes: images were described rather than actually rendered, which is recorded on the version line
in the plain prose the schema requires. Nothing was thin, so there is no thin-fields line.

---

```
# TASTE BLOCK — Late Tape
version: 1 · 2026-08-17 (the reference images in this run were described to me rather than actually rendered, so THE HAND and THE FINISH rest on descriptions)

## INTENT

1. CORE THESIS
It should feel like footage somebody actually shot, late and alone, on a camera that wasn't good
enough. You're the person holding it, not the person being shown it.

2. EMOTIONAL MODE
Favor loneliness, boredom, and the feeling of being awake too late over beauty, composition, and
anything that looks arranged. If it looks like someone made it look good, I'd rather it looked
worse.

3. REGISTER
A photograph of a real place, never drawn and never invented, but degraded far enough that the
detail is unreliable. Real stuff, seen badly. Not a clean photograph, and not an illustration of
one.

4. STRUCTURE
The lit thing is the shot. Whatever the lamp reaches is what you're looking at, and everything else
stays dark and unresolved — I'd rather lose it than light it. Nothing gets added to explain the
shot. If two things are competing to be looked at, one of them goes dark.

5. NARRATIVE STANCE
The meaning arrives through absence and duration: nobody in frame, nothing moving, the camera parked
a beat too long on a place with nothing happening in it. The viewer supplies the feeling out of
having been out at that hour themselves. Never a face doing the work, never an event, never a
caption. The bad picture is part of what's being said, not an effect laid on afterwards.

## EXECUTION (visual)

6. MEDIUM / SUBSTRATE
Phone video shot at night with the sensor pushed further than it should be, then run back out
through a VHS dub and recaptured — a real tape generation, not a plugin imitating one. The result is
soft, roughly 4:3, and visibly a copy of a copy.

7. THE HAND
The camera and the tape do the marking; no person leaves anything on this. Handheld, no tripod, no
stabilization, so the frame breathes and occasionally jerks. Tracking error lives in the bottom
quarter and wobbles — it never sits still. Bright things smear sideways in one direction only, not
both. Focus hunts, and sometimes loses.

8. COLOR / LIGHT
One practical light source in the frame, two at the absolute most if there's a sign. Everything
outside its reach stays unlit. Never daylight. Never true black — shadows sit milky grey-blue,
because tape can't reach black. Whatever color the lamp is, that's the palette.

9. THE FINISH
Wrecked, but the place still has to read. It stops being right the moment you can't tell what you're
looking at: a street has to stay a street, a parking lot a parking lot. The whole point is seeing a
real place badly, and if the place is gone there's nothing left to see badly.

10. THE ONE MOVE
One band of tracking distortion across the bottom quarter of the frame, wobbling, and it comes and
goes — there for a second, gone the next. Constant means a filter. Intermittent means a tape.

## NEVER DO

- No studio-clean, evenly lit, advertising-looking image. If it looks lit by somebody, nobody was
  there, and the whole thing rests on somebody having been there.
- Nothing drawn, painted, or illustrated, and nothing invented. A drawing means someone made it up.
  This has to look like it happened and I found the tape.
- No painterly beauty, no nicely composed nice night. If it's beautiful, you can tell someone made
  it beautiful, and then it belongs to them instead of being found.
- Never degraded so far that the place stops being readable. You're meant to see a real place badly;
  with the place gone, there's nothing left to see badly.
- No on-screen furniture: no timecode, no date stamp, no REC dot, no lens flares, no film-burn
  overlays. That's pretending to be a camcorder instead of being one, and the pretending is what
  makes it read as a preset.
- Never daylight, and never true black. Daylight kills it instantly, and a real black means the
  image was made somewhere a camera never went.
- No constant, uniform degradation sitting evenly over everything. If it never lets up, it's a layer
  on top rather than a tape being played.
```
