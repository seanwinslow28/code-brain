## Licensed moves: six, and the shape they make is the point

Budget heat 1, room 2. Six of thirty-six:

**Expectation / Instead** · **Then / Now Narrator** · **Short Declarative Drop** ·
**Anaphoric Stack** · **Blunt-Literal Description** · **Rhetorical Catechism**

Read that list for what it is rather than as a leftover. Every one of them is **structural** — a
two-beat hinge, a past-self and a present-self, a rhythm break, a repeated opening, a thing described
flat, a question answered immediately. Not one of them is a joke.

**So the cover letter is the one Professional document that gets a narrative and no humour.** It can
tell the story of a decision. It cannot be funny while it does. That is the register: a person who
has done the work, telling you what he learned, in his own sentence rhythms, without performing.

Everything at heat 2 or above is banned. No pop culture, no self-deprecation as structure, no
under-reaction, no canon line. The moves that make a Substack post his are exactly the ones that
make a cover letter read as a candidate who does not know where he is.

## The exception that keeps the chain from mangling this document

**Here, the ask is the point.** `substack-value-engine`'s sideways-ask rule — the piece ends on the
work or the lesson, never a request; if the last sentence makes the reader feel they should *do*
something for you, rewrite it — **does not apply to this medium.** A cover letter that ends on the
work and never asks for the interview has failed at the genre.

What carries over is the *reason* behind that rule: confidence is shown by not needing to grovel.
The ask is direct, one sentence, and unembarrassed. It is not a plea, it does not enumerate need, and
it never reaches for the predicament (`LANE.md`, do-not-promote).

This exception is stated because the chain gate will otherwise flag a correct closing and route a
revise request that makes the document worse.

## What a cover letter has to deliver

- **One real decision, narrated.** Not a summary of the resume in paragraph form. The reader already
  has the resume. Pick a decision, name the tradeoff, say what he would do differently.
- **A reason this role and this organization**, traceable to something he said. Generic enthusiasm is
  an untraced claim wearing a friendly face.
- **Every claim traced.** Facts-only form; the origin gate blocks.
- **The ask, once, at the end.**

## Format

- **One page. Three or four paragraphs.** A fifth paragraph is almost always the resume being
  restated.
- **Addressed to a person** where a person can be found.
- **The first paragraph is where the reader decides whether to read the second.** A format fact about
  how this document is read, not the Expressive lane's first-screen test, which does not apply here.
  Open on the decision or the thing that happened, never on "I am writing to apply for".

## Negative specimens — what this must never look like

- **The resume in prose.** "In my role at X, I was responsible for Y and Z, where I managed…" The
  reader has that document. This one is for what the bullets cannot hold.
- **Borrowed enthusiasm.** "I was thrilled to see this opening" / "your innovative approach to…"
  Template strings, banned by the lane, and transparently so.
- **The grovel.** Any sentence enumerating what he needs, or apologizing for a gap. The predicament
  is suppressed by default and this is the document most likely to reach for it.
- **A joke.** Even a good one. Heat 2 is banned here; a landed joke in a cover letter still costs the
  reader's read of the candidate's judgement.
- **A hedged claim.** "Helped contribute to a significant reduction in…" — ask or strike.

## Gates, in order

Value → structure → shape → critique → humanity (neutral register) → **origin (blocks)** →
do-not-promote.

`writing-humanity-pass` runs **FULL** scrub. The six licensed moves are structural rather than
voice-bearing, so there is no signature-move layer to protect — but a licensed move is still a
deliberate choice and is not a tell.

## Delivery

A ship packet: the letter, the ORIGIN LEDGER, the ASK LIST. The machine never sends an application.

## What this contract does not own

Whether to apply (Sean), which role (Sean), or the claims (the transcript).
