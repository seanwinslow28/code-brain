## LinkedIn is a syndication target, never a composition surface

[L4](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/158). A LinkedIn post is a **cut of an
artifact that already exists** — a Substack piece, a shipped project, a portfolio write-up. Nothing
is originated here.

**The seam that keeps syndication from becoming a laundry.** A cut inherits the source artifact's
transcript, and it is the only transcript it gets. **A cut may not add a claim the source did not
carry.** Without that rule, "syndicate the post" is the one route by which new, uninterviewed
material could enter the machine wearing the source's credibility. The origin gate runs the cut
against the source's transcript, not against nothing.

If the cut wants a claim the source lacks, that is not a cut. It is a new piece, and it goes back to
stage 1.

## Licensed moves: twenty, the most of any Professional medium

Budget heat 2, room 2. Twenty of thirty-six:

**Funniest Word Last** · **Unsignposted Pivot** · **Tool-as-Character** · **Expectation / Instead** ·
**Then / Now Narrator** · **Flat Collision** · **Short Declarative Drop** · **Anaphoric Stack** ·
**Hard Cut / Deflation** · **Rule of Three + Emotional Pivot** · **Pop Culture Anchoring** ·
**Blunt-Literal Description** · **Reader-Dismissal** · **Equation / Formula Defamiliarizer** ·
**Borrowed Canon Line** · **Faux-Ignorance Aside** · **Comic Under-Reaction** · **Fumbled Idiom** ·
**Zeugma Paint** · **Rhetorical Catechism**

**This is the only Professional medium that licenses a joke.** It shares its budget and therefore its
roster with the portfolio write-up, which the matrix records as known coarseness. The difference
between them is not the roster; it is that one is read by a recruiter searching and one is read by a
visitor already looking.

**Room 2 and the mic drop agree, which is worth noticing.** The verified post anatomy ends on a
contrast, and every move that builds a contrast — Flat Collision, Hard Cut / Deflation,
Expectation / Instead — is room 2 and licensed. The moves that would carry an ending by *return*
instead — Callback Closer, Inverted Refrain, Sincerity Punished by the World — are room 4 and banned.
The form and the matrix reached the same place independently.

## The two surfaces have opposite flare budgets

LinkedIn is not one surface, which is why no single flare percentage is supportable
([#170](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/170) Q2).

| | **Profile** | **Feed** |
|---|---|---|
| Read by | recruiter search, then a human skimming | someone scrolling past |
| Flare budget | near zero | **required** — a post with no hook fails invisibly |
| Governed by | skimmability: three lines before "see more", a results list | the fold |
| Failure mode | reads as gaming, or does not surface at all | 12 likes and no idea why |

The near-zero profile budget is a **skimmability** constraint, not decorum. Nothing about this
platform asks him to sound like a brochure.

## Keywords are a procedure, not a list

**Ruled here** ([#170](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/170) handoff Q1). A
hardcoded keyword list rots, and worse, it invites writing toward claims Sean cannot substantiate —
the origin gate's exact failure mode. No trustworthy source publishes such a list; every one that
does is vendor SEO written for search traffic, which the research tier-audited and rejected.

**The procedure, per search:**

1. Collect **five tightly-clustered job descriptions** for the roles actually being targeted.
2. Extract the terms and gate at **≥ 75% overlap** — a term must appear in four of five.
3. **The threshold is a halt condition, not a target.** If the five do not converge at 75%, the
   finding is that the target is not one role. Stop and rethink the search. Never lower the gate to
   produce a list.
4. **Every surviving term is checked against the transcript before it is placed.** A keyword naming a
   skill he cannot substantiate is an untraced claim, and the fact that a machine derived it from
   real JDs does not make it his. Untraceable terms go on the ASK LIST or are dropped.
5. Place per the field-tolerance table below.

## The field-tolerance table

Per [LinkedIn's own Recruiter Boolean documentation](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295),
keyword matches hit the profile card, Summary / About, Experience (header, description **and**
location) and Skills. Operators are AND / OR / NOT; `+` and `-` are unsupported. Those fields do
**not** share a tolerance for density:

| Field | Indexed | Human-read | Tolerance |
|---|:--:|:--:|---|
| **Endorsable Skills section** | yes | effectively never | **Unbounded.** Load it. |
| **Skills under an Experience entry** | yes | **yes** | **Low.** Forty here reads as gaming and damages the page. |
| **Headline** | yes, weighted heaviest | yes, highest traffic | Capped by readability. Always visible; it is the whole first impression. |
| **About** | yes, weighted heavily | yes | Capped by readability, and by three lines before "see more". |
| **Experience header / description / location** | yes | yes | Moderate. Location is indexed, which is not obvious. |
| **Banner** | **no** | yes | **Zero.** Not indexed at all. Pure persuasion. |

The banner row is the one most likely to be got wrong in both directions: nothing written there helps
a search, and it is still seen by every human who lands on the page.

## Flare is governed by skim cost and pillar membership

**Ruled here** ([#170](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/170) handoff Q3). Two
empirical governors replace a percentage:

**1. Skim-cost budget.** Does this survive being read in three lines? Profile: before "see more".
Feed: above the fold. A post that needs its fourth line to make sense has already failed for most of
the people who saw it.

**2. Pillar membership.** Three to five declared pillars. A post outside them **banks rather than
posts.** The pillars are **Sean's input, declared per campaign** — this contract owns the rule, not
the list, for the same reason it owns a keyword procedure and not a keyword list. Add Gupta's
two-audience test: good to a future employer without alarming a current one.

**The finding behind this, and it is counterintuitive:** the "your posts are coming off as spammy"
message that nearly ended the source's practice was a **topic-discipline failure, not a tone
failure**. It was fixed with brand pillars, not by turning the voice down. The instinct when someone
says you sound spammy is to get quieter. That is the wrong lever, and this contract exists partly to
stop the machine pulling it.

Cadence, while job-searching: about three posts a week, two authority and one reach. Emoji carry no
position either way — they are skim devices, interchangeable with numbers or whitespace.

## Post anatomy

Five parts, verified from the source transcript ([#170](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/170) Q3):

1. **Hook** — two to three lines above the fold, working **as a pair with the image**.
2. **Bridge** — the story into it.
3. **Meat** — the payload. People are there to learn something.
4. **Mic drop** — two to four lines, built on a contrast.
5. **CTA** — the engagement question or ask.

**The image is part of the hook, not decoration, and it is chosen after the mic drop is written.**
Its job is to make the first two lines land, and it cannot be selected before the piece knows what it
is arguing.

No trustworthy source gives a character count for any of these. Every source that did was vendor SEO.
The bounds here are structural, not numeric, on purpose.

## Reach versus authority: not this contract's fork

The research surfaced a structural fork on the same topic — "how to X" is shareable because it is
detached from a person; "how I did X" is not shareable by construction, and does not need to be,
because its job is the activity feed a hiring manager scrolls after landing on the profile.

**Ruled: the contract owns the authority shape only.** L4 fixes LinkedIn as syndication of existing
artifacts, and a cut of a Pencil & Prompt post or a shipped project is "how I did X" by construction.
The reach shape would require composing natively for the platform, which L4 bans. The fork resolves
itself; it is recorded so nobody re-opens it as a gap.

## Borrow structure, never strings — this is the medium it was written for

The lane law ([`LANE.md`](LANE.md#borrow-structure-never-strings)) already holds the rule. The
concrete practice it was ruled against lives here, so it is named:

**Scraping a post that beat a virality bar, extracting its reusable skeleton — which phrases are
static, which slots are variable — and filling the slots.** It is the industry-standard drafting
loop, it is genuinely effective, and it imports a stranger's sentence strings into Sean's mouth. The
words are not invented, which is why the gate was not built to catch them. They are someone else's.

The shape is free. Hook-then-contrast is a shape. The clauses are not.

**Also recorded and recommended against:** connecting a model to LinkedIn through third-party
scraping actors behind proxies, specifically so the platform cannot attribute the scraping. That is
deliberate ToS evasion, and a syndication contract does not need to scrape anything.

## First screen and reply surface

The Professional lane does not inherit the Expressive first-screen test, and this contract does not
smuggle it back in under another name. What it states, as **format**, is what the reader sees in the
first few seconds:

- **Feed:** the first two to three lines plus the image, before "see more".
- **Profile:** the headline, always visible, everywhere his name appears. Then the first three lines
  of About before "see more".

**Reply surface: yes — the only one in this lane.** LinkedIn posts have comments, and the verified
anatomy ends in a CTA. This contract therefore **adopts the Expressive lane's
[reply-hook memo](../expressive/LANE.md#the-reply-hook-memo-advisory-never-a-gate) by name.** The
Professional lane excludes it on the grounds that these documents are not conversation surfaces,
which is true of every medium in the lane except this one; adopting it adds an advisory artifact and
deletes nothing. It ships with every post and binds nothing.

**A CTA is not a reply hook.** "What do you think?" is the absence of one, and the memo will say so.

## Negative specimens — what this must never look like

- **The filled-in skeleton.** Someone else's post with the nouns swapped. Banned by the lane and the
  reason this contract names the practice explicitly.
- **The keyword-stuffed Experience entry.** Forty skills under one role. Indexed, read, and it reads
  as gaming.
- **The broetry post.** One sentence per line for nine lines to farm the "see more" click. A format
  trick standing in for a hook.
- **The unearned lesson.** A prescription to an audience of builders from one run of one experiment.
  `writing-critique`'s **false authority** tell, and this is the platform that rewards it hardest.
- **The claim the source never made.** A cut that got better in translation. That is not a cut.
- **The engagement-bait CTA.** "Agree?" / "Thoughts?" / "Repost if this resonated."
- **The quiet turn-down.** A post that reads as a memo because someone once said the account sounded
  spammy. The lever is topic discipline, never volume.
- **A keyword he cannot substantiate**, however cleanly derived.

## Gates, in order

Post-draft as of 2026-08-31, in the machine's current order: **origin (claims tier) →
do-not-promote + coined-lines sweep → humanity scrub → critique / analyzer**. Professional lane:
**origin blocks delivery** while any claim is untraced (`origin_check.py` exits 1), run against the
**source artifact's transcript**.

`writing-humanity-pass` runs the **voice-aware** scrub here, not the full neutral one — this is the
only medium in the lane with a twenty-move roster to protect. A licensed move is not a tell.

**Coined lines carry one wrinkle, and it matters.** A cut reuses lines from its source by design, so
it runs with the **source artifact's** slug:

```bash
python3 gates/coined_lines.py <cut.md> --lane professional --artifact <source-artifact-slug>
```

The one-artifact rule says a coined line lives in exactly one artifact. A syndicated cut is the same
artifact on a second surface, not a second artifact, so `--artifact` exempts the source's own lines
and the gate still catches a line borrowed from a *different* piece. Passing the cut's own slug
would flag every correct line in it.

## Delivery

A ship packet: the post, the image brief (chosen after the mic drop), the reply-hook memo, the ORIGIN
LEDGER pointing at the source's transcript, and the ASK LIST. For profile work: the field-by-field
diff and the derived keyword set with its five source JDs attached, so the derivation is auditable.
**The machine never posts and never edits the profile.**

## What this contract does not own

Whether to syndicate (Sean), the pillars (Sean, per campaign), the source artifact (its own
contract), or the claims (the source's transcript).
