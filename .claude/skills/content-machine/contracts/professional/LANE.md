# Professional lane: the law every Professional contract inherits

Resume, cover letter, application questionnaire, email, LinkedIn. This file holds what is true of all
of them so each contract holds only what is true of *its* medium. A contract may **narrow** anything
here and may not delete it; where a contract is silent on something this file covers, this file
governs.

Written with wave 1 ([#166](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/166)). Wave 2 inherits
it rather than re-deriving it.

---

## What a contract is, after 2026-08-31

The rules-off re-scope changed **who reads this file**, and every contract in this lane inherits the
change. A contract is read by the **orchestrator**: it shapes the TOPIC CARD, tells the interview how
far it has to reach, and supplies the post-draft check. **It is never loaded into the shaping
context** — the drafting subagent gets the transcript, the corpus, the voice samples, the reference
universe and the do-not-promote list, and nothing else (`SKILL.md`, "The shaping context").

So a contract's **licensed-move roster is a statement of the register this medium lands in**, for the
orchestrator and for the post-draft read. It is not a checklist handed to a drafter, and it is not a
compliance target. Nothing here is a drafting-time constraint any more; the deliverables, the format
bounds and the negative specimens are what the orchestrator and the reader hold the piece against.

## The seam this lane exists to hold

**The transcript owns the claims. Convention owns the sentences.**

Both halves are load-bearing and they pull against each other. The machine's law is that draft words
come from the interview ([L2](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/158)); a resume
written in that register would be unreadable. So the Professional lane runs the **facts-only** form
of the law:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Every claim traces** | A number, a date, a title, a scope, an outcome, a tool, a named system. If Sean did not say it in the interview, it does not go in the document. This half does not relax. |
| **Phrasing may be conventional** | Verb-led bullets, standard section headings, the ordinary vocabulary of the genre. Nobody wants a resume in dive-bar register, and writing one is not fidelity, it is a category error. |

**The test:** point at any claim and name the transcript line it came from. Point at any *sentence*
and it may be ordinary. Those are different questions and the lane keeps them separate.

## Borrow structure, never strings

Every professional-writing template on the internet hands you sentence strings — "spearheaded
cross-functional initiatives", "proven track record of driving results". Importing one puts words
in Sean's mouth that he never said, from a stranger, which is the origin rule broken by a route the
gate was not built to watch: the words are not invented, they are *someone else's*.

Surfaced by the pre-LinkedIn research ([#170](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/170))
and ruled here.

- **A template may give you the shape.** Verb, object, measured outcome. Three bullets under a role.
  A four-paragraph letter. Shape is not authorship.
- **A template may not give you the words.** Not one clause, not one stock phrase.

If a bullet reads like it could sit on any resume in the industry, a string got borrowed. Rewrite it
from what he actually said.

## The origin gate blocks here

Expressive advises; Professional **blocks delivery** while any claim is untraced (`origin_check.py`
exits 1). A made-up joke in an essay costs a rewrite. A fabricated number on a resume is a different
class of harm, and it is the applicant who carries it.

The block is on the document, never on Sean's judgement. He clears it by confirming the fact or
striking it.

**Hedging is not tracing.** The tempting way to clear the gate is to soften an untraced claim until
it is unfalsifiable — "helped drive", "contributed to", "supported efforts around". That converts a
claim the gate would catch into a claim it cannot, and it makes the document weaker in exactly the
place it needed to be strong. An untraced claim gets **asked about or struck**. It never gets blurred.
The **ASK LIST** is mandatory in this lane, not optional.

## Voice enters only where the matrix licenses it

[`move-licensing.md`](../move-licensing.md) governs. Professional budgets run heat 0 to 2, so most of
the 36-move roster is banned in most of this lane, and one medium licenses nothing at all. That is
the design, not an oversight.

**A banned move is banned even when it would be good.** The move that would land beautifully in a
Substack post is the exact move that reads as a candidate who does not know where he is.

## The do-not-promote sweep runs harder here

The suppressed backstory topics stay out, as everywhere. This lane adds the pressure that makes them
tempting: a document written to be judged invites the writer to explain himself. It never becomes an
explanation. The work stands on its own, and the reader is deciding about the work.

## What this lane does NOT inherit

- **The first-screen test.** It is Expressive-only by
  [L8](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/158). Where a Professional medium has a
  scan pattern that matters — and most do — it belongs in that contract as a **format** rule, stated
  as what the reader sees in the first few seconds, not as the lane test under another name.
- **The reply-hook memo.** These documents are not conversation surfaces — **except LinkedIn**,
  which has comments and an anatomy that ends in a CTA. `linkedin.md` adopts the Expressive
  lane's memo by name (wave 2, [#171](https://github.com/seanwinslow28/code-brain/issues/171)).
  Adopting an advisory artifact adds something the lane omitted; it deletes nothing.
