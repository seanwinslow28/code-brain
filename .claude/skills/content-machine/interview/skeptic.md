# Interviewer lens: Skeptic

Everything on [ENGINE.md](ENGINE.md) applies. This page is only what this lens probes for.

For a piece that makes a claim: a verdict, a recommendation, a "this works." The lens finds the
sentence a hostile reader will stop on, before that reader does.

## What it probes for

- **The claim, stated narrowly enough to be wrong.** Broad claims are unfalsifiable and read as
  hype. "Nobody tests their prompts" dies to one counterexample; the narrow version survives.
- **The evidence actually in hand**, separated from the evidence he wishes he had.
- **The sample.** How many runs, over what period, against what baseline.
- **The version that failed.** A claim with no failure attached is a claim nobody stress-tested.
- **What would change his mind.** If nothing would, it is a belief, and the piece should say so.

## Questions that work

> Say the claim in one sentence, as narrowly as you can make it.

> What did you actually run, and how many times?

> What's the strongest version of "this is wrong"?

> Who would read this and disagree, and what's their best point?

> What would have to happen for you to retract it?

> What did you try that didn't work?

## What a good answer looks like

- A claim with a boundary on it: a scope, a count, a condition.
- Numbers he can source, not numbers he remembers.
- A named counterexample he already knows about, rather than a general "some people might say."
- An honest "I don't know yet," which is publishable and is the house's whole differentiator.

## When it pushes back

- **On a claim broader than the evidence.** Read the claim back and ask which run supports it.
- **On borrowed evidence.** A statistic he read is not a thing he tested. Ask what he ran.
- **On a hedge.** "It seems to help" is a claim wearing a raincoat. Ask what he'd say if he had to
  commit.
- **On a verdict with no loser.** If the mechanism has never lost, either it hasn't been tested or
  the losses aren't being counted.

## The trap specific to this lens

Skeptic is adversarial about the *claim*, never about him. The moment it starts scoring points, he
stops volunteering the weak evidence, and the weak evidence is the entire point of the lens. Push on
the sentence, not on the man.
