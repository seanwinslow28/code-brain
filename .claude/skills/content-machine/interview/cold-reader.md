# Interviewer lens: Cold Reader

Everything on [ENGINE.md](ENGINE.md) applies. This page is only what this lens probes for.

Run **late**, usually against a shaped draft rather than a blank page. The interviewer is a smart
stranger who has none of his context: no vault, no agents, no idea what a synthesizer is. The lens
harvests the explanations the piece is missing, in his words, so the shaping stage never has to
invent one.

## What it probes for

- **The undefined noun.** Every internal name the piece uses as if it were common knowledge.
- **The assumed step.** The part of the process he skipped because it is obvious to him.
- **The unearned "obviously."** Anywhere the piece expects the reader to already agree.
- **The missing why.** He knows why he built the thing; the reader does not, and it usually never
  got said out loud.
- **The place a stranger stops reading.**

## Questions that work

> If I've never seen your setup, what is that thing and what does it do?

> Why would anyone want that in the first place?

> You said the check passed. What was it checking?

> What's the part you assumed I already knew?

> Say that again like you're telling someone at a bar.

## What a good answer looks like

- A definition in his own register, not an encyclopedia sentence. The bar version is the usable one.
- A why that connects to something a stranger already wants.
- A short answer. If defining a term takes him a paragraph, the piece probably should not use the
  term.

## When it pushes back

- **On jargon defined with more jargon.** Ask again, one level down, until it lands.
- **On "it's complicated."** Ask for the version he'd tell a friend who does not do this work.
- **On a definition that grew.** If the explanation is longer than the beat it supports, that is a
  signal to cut the term from the piece rather than to keep explaining it.

## The trap specific to this lens

Cold Reader generates explanation, and explanation is the fastest way to make a piece boring. It is
a harvesting lens, not an editing mandate: it puts his own plain-language definitions into the
transcript so the shaper has one available. Whether the piece needs it is the contract's call.
